MEXICO -- two different figures in the project's own notes
==============================================================================
SAT/Senado (2019): 5bn MXN/yr, avg 2014-2018 GDP -> 0.0238% of GDP -> MATCHES claimed 0.03%
  In USD (avg fx): $290.8 million/yr
  As % of avg tax revenue: 0.195%

OECD (2017) Box 2: EUR 3bn total, 2007-2009, forged invoices generally
  = $4.23bn USD (avg 2007-09 EUR/USD rate)
  vs. cumulative 3-yr GDP $3207.3bn USD -> 0.132% of 3-yr GDP
  Annualized: $1.41bn/yr

  -> The two Mexico figures are NOT the same thing: different sources (OECD's own reporting vs. Mexico's tax authority SAT), different periods (2007-09 vs 2014-18), different mechanisms (forged invoices generally vs. specifically identified 'ghost firms'). Annualized, OECD's figure (~$1411M/yr) is ~4.9x the SAT figure (~$291M/yr) in nominal USD.

  WHY THE TWO DIFFER -- checked 2026-09-22, a real dateable story, not a discrepancy to
  paper over: Mexico's e-invoicing (CFDI) became MANDATORY FOR ALL TAXPAYERS on Jan 1,
  2014 (independently confirmed via web search, not assumed) -- a rollout the OECD report
  itself documents (optional from 2005, +134% e-invoices issued 2010->2011, universal by
  2014). OECD's figure (2007-09) is entirely PRE-mandate; SAT's (2014-18) is entirely
  POST-mandate -- a clean split, no straddling year. The ~4.9x drop lines up with
  the mandate. CAVEAT: 'forged invoices' (pre-2014, crude fake paper invoices) and 'ghost
  firms' (post-2014, Mexico's EFOS problem -- valid e-invoices for fake transactions) are
  not quite the same mechanism -- e-invoicing killed the former, the latter is its evolved
  successor. Correct claim: losses from this channel fell ~4.9x after the mandate,
  NOT 'e-invoicing solved fake invoicing.'
