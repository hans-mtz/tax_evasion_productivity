## Suggested revision (Option A, not applied)

> Materials are the input that is both flexible and deductible. Because materials are flexible, the firm's first-order condition equates the log share of materials over sales to the log of the output elasticity of materials, up to measurement error in output. If the elasticity were known, the gap between the reported share of materials over sales and the elasticity would measure overreporting, up to the measurement error. I call this the materials condition.
>
> Because materials are deductible, every overreported peso lowers a firm's tax bill by the sales-tax rate on purchases. But overreporting is costly. A firm that overreports weighs this benefit against the probability of detection and the cost of overreporting, defined as the opportunity cost of the resources reallocated from production, such as fake invoices. I call this trade-off the evasion condition.
>
> [This chapter]{.content-hidden when-meta="jmp"}[This section]{.content-visible when-meta="jmp"} derives both conditions, which the rest of the [thesis]{.content-hidden when-meta="jmp"}[paper]{.content-visible when-meta="jmp"} builds on. The materials condition (@eq-share-net) underlies the test, the deconvolution, and the production-function estimates (@sec-testing to @sec-pf). The evasion condition (@eq-foc-e) underlies the analysis of the 1983 reform and the counterfactual (@sec-fiscal, @sec-counterfactual).

## Round 4 (2026-10-06, afternoon): Setup, paragraph 1 (Hans's version)

Text reviewed: "The setup combines a production-function model with a tax-evasion model and departs from each. It departs from standard production-function methods that depend on the flexible input [...] in two ways. [...]"

Citation keys (Levinsohn2003, Doraszelski2013, Ackerberg2015, Gandhi2020) all resolve in `Thesis/biblio/references.bib`. Claims hold: none of the four builds sales taxes into the firm's problem, and all observe true materials.

1. **"model" vs "methods" (sentences 1–2).** Sentence 1 says the setup departs from a production-function *model*; sentence 2 says it departs from *methods*. A setup departs from a model, not from an estimation method. Suggest: "It departs from the production-function models behind methods that rely on a flexible input [...]", or keep it shorter: "It departs from the production-function models that rely on a flexible input [...]".
2. **"depend on the flexible input"** → "rely on a flexible input". "The flexible input" presupposes one the reader already knows; the Setup introduces materials as the flexible input only two paragraphs later.
3. **"firms choose material and how much material to report" (sentence 3).** "Material" singular and "choose material" read oddly; elsewhere the chapter says "materials". Suggest: "First, firms choose how much material to use and how much to report, so true materials are unobserved." ("and thus" → "so": shorter, same meaning.)
4. **Comma (sentence 5).** "This matters because when the rate on sales differs [...]" → "This matters because, when the rate on sales differs from the rate on purchases, the taxes change [...]". The embedded clause needs both commas.
5. **"Hence, the government can indirectly influence how much material the firm uses" (last sentence).** This is a new and good point: it is the input channel of the counterfactual (`cf_mresp`). "Indirectly" is vague, though, and "the government" makes it sound like a policy instrument aimed at materials. Suggest: "Hence, a change in either rate also changes how much material the firm uses (@sec-model-foc-m), a channel the counterfactual includes (@sec-counterfactual)." The second clause is optional.
6. **Fit with the next paragraph (in progress).** The paragraph promises a departure from "each" model; the next paragraph should then name the tax-evasion model it departs from. It currently departs from @Almunia2018 without introducing it (see chat, same day).
