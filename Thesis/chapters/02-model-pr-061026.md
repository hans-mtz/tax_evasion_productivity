## Suggested revision (Option A, not applied)

> Materials are the input that is both flexible and deductible. Because materials are flexible, the firm's first-order condition equates the log share of materials over sales to the log of the output elasticity of materials, up to measurement error in output. If the elasticity were known, the gap between the reported share of materials over sales and the elasticity would measure overreporting, up to the measurement error. I call this the materials condition.
>
> Because materials are deductible, every overreported peso lowers a firm's tax bill by the sales-tax rate on purchases. But overreporting is costly. A firm that overreports weighs this benefit against the probability of detection and the cost of overreporting, defined as the opportunity cost of the resources reallocated from production, such as fake invoices. I call this trade-off the evasion condition.
>
> [This chapter]{.content-hidden when-meta="jmp"}[This section]{.content-visible when-meta="jmp"} derives both conditions, which the rest of the [thesis]{.content-hidden when-meta="jmp"}[paper]{.content-visible when-meta="jmp"} builds on. The materials condition (@eq-share-net) underlies the test, the deconvolution, and the production-function estimates (@sec-testing to @sec-pf). The evasion condition (@eq-foc-e) underlies the analysis of the 1983 reform and the counterfactual (@sec-fiscal, @sec-counterfactual).
