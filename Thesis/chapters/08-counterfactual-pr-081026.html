Suggested version (minimal edits)

How does tax revenue change as the government changes the sales-tax rate on purchases? As the rate increases, the incentive to claim additional fictitious deductions increases, but more overreporting raises the probability of detection. Therefore, firms face a trade-off. Conversely, a decrease in the rate reduces the incentive to overreport. Firms decrease their overreporting (intensive margin), and some stop overreporting altogether (extensive margin). An increase in the rate also affects firms’ choice of true materials because it lowers the net-of-tax price of materials relative to output. Hence, firms buy more materials and produce more. Larger output raises the tax collected on sales, but more materials lower revenue because claimed deductions rise. How does tax revenue change at the margin for a change in the rate? How much of that change comes from firms overreporting more, and how much from firms buying more materials?
