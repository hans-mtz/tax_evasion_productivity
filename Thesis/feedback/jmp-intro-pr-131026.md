## Claims

1. **Convex cost "rationalizes" the size pattern (P5, "Third, ...").** With the minimum of the cost at the 57th percentile of productivity (ch. 8), the model puts the most evasion near median productivity, not near the top; ch. 8's draft note adds that the percentile ranges from the 2nd (331) to the 98th (342) by industry. Suggested: "Third, the structural estimates show a cost of evasion that is convex in productivity, consistent with a pattern observed in other studies: evasion rises with firm size but drops sharply at the very top [@Carrillo2022]."
2. **"including 4 of the 5 largest, which together produce about two fifths of manufacturing output" (P5).** In ch. 4 the 41 percent is the output share of all nine industries; "which" here attaches to the four largest. Suggested: "...in 9 of the 18 industries liable for the sales tax, which together produce about two fifths of manufacturing output, including 4 of the 5 largest."
3. **"from these firms" (P3, twice).** No antecedent: the paragraph last named "unincorporated firms in the affected industries" (the reform) and then "firms overreporting more". The structural results are for unincorporated firms in the nine industries where the test detects overreporting. Suggested at first use: "net sales-tax revenue from unincorporated firms in the industries where overreporting is detected is negative".

## Sentences

4. **"Colombia's sales tax worked as a value-added tax, and in 1983 it raised the rate..." (P3).** "It" is the sales tax, which does not raise rates. Suggested: "Colombia's sales tax worked as a value-added tax. In 1983, Colombia raised its rate for most manufacturing industries but left a few exempt."
5. **"which were exempt from the sales tax and thus incentives to overreport were negligible" (P5).** The second clause has no link to "which". Suggested: "which were exempt from the sales tax and therefore had negligible incentives to overreport."
6. **"A rejection is also unambiguous, because no firm gains by underreporting deductible inputs, so it can only point to overreporting." (P5)** ", so" construction. Suggested: "A rejection is also unambiguous: no firm gains by underreporting deductible inputs, and a rejection can only point to overreporting."
7. **"the purchases-side rate" (P3)** vs "the rate on purchases" everywhere else (ch. 8, conclusion). Suggested: "the rate on purchases".
8. **"The test ... holds under any common production technology" (P5).** Appendix F: the size holds for any common technology; the one-sided alternative needs $\ln G$ concave in materials. Optional: "...and its size holds under any common production technology."
