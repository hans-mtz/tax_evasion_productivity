## First-stage / density-transformation corrections

**Jacobian correction (2026-07-30).** Earlier version of the Jacobian line read $J_i(\varepsilon)=|h'(e)|(M^*-e)$, from differentiating $u=\ln(M^*/(M^*-e))$ holding $M^*$ fixed. That's a partial/total-derivative error — $M^*=M+e$ is not fixed as $e$ varies (it's the thing that moves), so that derivative silently assumed $\partial M^*/\partial e=0$. $M$ (from the production-side materials FOC, independent of $e$) is the quantity actually fixed. The value of $u_{it}$ itself is unaffected (both formulas give $\ln(M^*_{it}/M_{it})$); only the Jacobian's functional form changes, by a factor of $M^*/M$. Corrected to $J_i(\varepsilon)=|h'(e)|\,M^*$. Full worked correction in `Paper/sections/9999-density-trans.qmd`.

**Correction (2026-08-12): $\omega\perp(K,L)$ was silently assumed and is untenable.** The bare $f_\omega(\omega(\varepsilon))$ in the unconditional likelihood silently assumed $\omega\perp(K,L)$ — untenable, since $\omega$ is AR(1)-persistent and $K$ is a dynamically accumulated state chosen anticipating that persistence; asserting independence would strip the AR(1) moment conditions (`eq-eta-moments` in `56-id-evasion.qmd`) of the instrument relevance that identifies $\alpha_K,\alpha_L$ in the first place. Fix: Markov timing ($K,L$ predetermined; the AR(1) innovation $\eta_{it}$ is news independent of everything dated $t-1$) gives $\omega_{it}\perp(K_{it},L_{it})\mid\omega_{it-1}$ — $\omega_{it-1}$ is a sufficient statistic for $(K,L)$'s relevance, so the likelihood should condition on the firm's own lagged $\tilde{\mathcal W}_{it-1}$ instead of $(K,L)$, replacing bare $f_\omega$ with the AR(1) innovation density $f_\eta$ and marginalizing the one remaining unknown $\varepsilon_{it-1}$ (via $\omega_{it-1}=\tilde{\mathcal W}_{it-1}-(1-\beta)\varepsilon_{it-1}$) — a 2-D integral over $(\varepsilon_{it},\varepsilon_{it-1})$, collapsing back to the static $\mathcal L_i^{\text{unc}}$ exactly when the AR(1) has no persistence. $f_\eta$ itself is recoverable by Fourier deconvolution from `56-id-evasion.qmd`'s AR(1) regression residual (`eq-big-w-reg`), reusing stage-1's $f_\varepsilon$. **Notation clash:** `56-id-evasion.qmd`'s AR(1) drift is also called $h$ (linear case $\delta_0+\delta_1\xi$) — collides with both the MSL doc's $h(e)$ and $\delta_0,\delta_1,\delta_2$; renamed locally to $g(\xi)=\gamma_0+\gamma_1\xi$ wherever the two are combined. Full derivation, and the correction to an earlier draft claim that the bare unconditional likelihood already sufficed, in `Paper/sections/9999-density-trans.qmd`. **Left genuinely unresolved at the time:** a firm's first-observed period has no $\tilde{\mathcal W}_{it-1}$ to condition on (dynamic-panel initial-conditions problem). This whole MSL/quadrature dynamic-likelihood strand was subsequently superseded in practice by the ELVIS moment-based stage-2 approach (see below), which never needs $f_\eta$ or $f_\varepsilon$ at all — so this correction remains a valid derivation (kept in `9999-density-trans.qmd`) but is no longer on the critical path for estimation.

## Estimation method selection: MSM rejected, ELVIS adopted

**MSM — investigated and rejected (2026-07-28).** The natural-looking alternative — simulating $\psi(\varepsilon^{(s)})$ (or $M^{(s)}=\beta P_tY_{it}e^{-\varepsilon^{(s)}}/\rho_t$, $e^{(s)}=M^*-M^{(s)}$, algebraically **identical** to the $\mathcal V$-based construction since $\textbf{e}^{-\mathcal V_{it}}=\beta P_tY_{it}/(\rho_tM^*_{it})$ makes them the same map) and averaging, or wrapping that average in classic GMM with instruments — does **not** deliver a valid moment: $\psi(\cdot)$ is nonlinear in $\varepsilon$, so the simulated average converges to $\int\psi(\varepsilon;\theta)f_\varepsilon(\varepsilon)d\varepsilon$, not to the firm's true $\psi_{it}=\psi(\varepsilon_{it};\theta)$ — it's missing exactly the posterior reweighting $f_\psi(\psi(\varepsilon))J_i(\varepsilon)f_\omega(\omega(\varepsilon))$ that makes MSL's analogous simulated average valid (MSL never averages $\psi(\varepsilon^{(s)})$ itself, only the reweighted density). GMM's instruments/weighting-matrix machinery only combines already-valid moments efficiently; it cannot fix an invalid one. A valid simulated-moments design would instead match a data-computable, $\psi$-free population object (e.g. $E[\mathcal V_{it}|Z]=E[u_{it}|Z]$) against a forward-simulated analog from trial $(\psi,\omega)$ draws — ties to stage 1's deconvolved $f_u$ — but delivers $\theta$ not $f_\psi$ and adds machinery for no real gain over quadrature-MLE. See `600-opt-tax.qmd` for the full explainer (kept as a "why not" section).

**ELVIS — investigated and adopted as a genuine third alternative (2026-08-19).** Schennach (2014, *Econometrica*, "Entropic Latent Variable Integration via Simulation," `@Schennach2014` in `biblio/b100422.bib`) solves exactly the failure mode identified in the MSM rejection above — a moment function nonlinear in an unobservable — without the naive-averaging bug. Recast the moment in the **true materials level** $M_{it}$ as the one unobservable (mirroring Schennach's own canonical errors-in-variables example, her Example 1.5, unobserved $X^*$ vs. observed $X=X^*+\Delta X$): $e(M)=M^*_{it}-M$, $\varepsilon(M)=\ln(M^*_{it}/M)-\mathcal V_{it}$, $\omega(M)=\tilde{\mathcal W}_{it}-(1-\beta)\varepsilon(M)$, $g(M,Z_{it},\theta)\equiv\psi(M)=h(e(M))-\delta_0+\delta_1\omega(M)-\delta_2\omega(M)^2$, support $\mathcal U_{Z,\theta}=(M^*_{it}-\tfrac1{2\lambda},M^*_{it})$ ($\theta$-dependent support is explicitly covered, her §4.1). Her Theorem 2.1: the infinite-dimensional question "does *some* conditional distribution of $M$ given $Z$ satisfy $E[g(M,Z,\theta)]=0$" is **equivalent** to a finite-dimensional system in $(\theta,\gamma)$ built from an entropy-maximizing **exponential tilt** $\exp(\gamma'g(M,Z,\theta))$ of a user-chosen dominating measure $\rho(M|Z)$ — solved via simulated draws $M^{(s)}\sim\rho(\cdot|Z)$ and a ratio-of-averages $\tilde g(Z,\theta,\gamma)=E_\rho[g\cdot e^{\gamma'g}]/E_\rho[e^{\gamma'g}]$, fed into standard GMM/EL over $(\theta,\gamma)$ jointly. **This tilt is precisely the missing posterior reweighting** that the rejected naive-MSM average lacked — not assumed, but solved for as a Lagrange multiplier on the moment constraint (her §2.2 derivation). Two practical payoffs: (i) doesn't need $f_\psi$ (same limit as MSM, expected), but (ii) **doesn't need $f_\varepsilon$ either** — by her Remark 2.3, the choice of $\rho$ has *zero effect* on the point estimate (only support matters), so stage 1's deconvolution of $f_\varepsilon$/$f_u$ becomes optional for stage 2 estimation of $\theta$ (only $\hat\beta$ is needed); $\hat f_\varepsilon$ can still be reused as an efficient $\rho$ to cut simulation variance, but that's a computational choice, not an identification requirement. Delivers $\theta=(\lambda,\delta_0,\delta_1,\delta_2)$ only, not $f_\psi$ — same trade-off as MSM, just valid. Needs $\ge4$ instrument-interacted moments for point identification (order condition); under-identification degrades gracefully to a set estimate. Identification of $\lambda$ still comes from sector–time variation in $\tau\rho_t$ interacted into $Z$, same source as the MLE. See `Lit-Papers/ELVIS.pdf` (primary source) and `Lit-Papers/schennach-2022-measurement-systems.pdf` (her 2022 *JEL* survey, §3.6.3) for the full derivation.

**Unified single-stage ELVIS — explored, held in reserve (2026-08-19).** The recast-in-$M$ move generalizes further — **there is only one true unobservable in the whole model: $M_{it}$**, for every firm-period, corp and unincorporated alike. Once $M_{it}$ is the ELVIS latent, the materials FOC and production identity solve in closed form with no $\mathcal V/\tilde{\mathcal W}$, no Jacobian, no density-transformation theorem needed at all:
$$\varepsilon_{it}(M_{it})=\ln\beta-\ln\!\left(\frac{\rho_tM_{it}}{P_tY_{it}}\right),\qquad \omega_{it}(M_{it})=(1-\beta)\ln M_{it}-\alpha_Kk_{it}-\alpha_Ll_{it}-\ln\beta+\ln\rho_t-\ln P_t$$
$y_{it}$ cancels out of $\omega_{it}(M_{it})$ — verified, not a bug: this is Gandhi–Navarro–Rivers's own $\Phi_{it}=y_{it}-\hat\varepsilon_{it}$ step, specialized to constant (Cobb-Douglas) elasticity. AR(1) moment reduces to $\eta_{it}(M_{it},M_{it-1})=\omega_{it}(M_{it})-\gamma_0-\gamma_1\omega_{it-1}(M_{it-1})$, no $\varepsilon$ left, needing only $E[\eta_{it}Z_{it}]=0$ (never the AR(1) innovation density). Corp/unincorp evasion relaxes from hard constraints to moments: corp's "$e\equiv0$" becomes a centering moment $E[e_{it}(M_{it})\mid\text{Corp}_i]=0$; unincorp's "evade on average" becomes an inequality moment via ELVIS's auxiliary-variable device. All parameters ($\beta,\alpha_K,\alpha_L,\gamma_0,\gamma_1,\lambda,\delta_0,\delta_1,\delta_2$) would be estimated jointly from the full panel, no separate first stage. Counterfactual revenue under this route: either a plain unweighted MC fallback, or an ELVIS-native auxiliary-parameter moment delivering a genuine interval for expected revenue. Set-identification mechanics: for candidate $\theta$, solve the inner problem to get $\hat L_n(\theta)$, evaluate over a grid, identified/confidence set is $\{\theta:\hat L_n(\theta)\le\text{critical value}\}$ from Chernozhukov–Hong–Tamer (2007) or Schennach's own conservative $\chi^2$ shortcut — not an ad hoc tolerance. **Status:** drafted as slides for `600-opt-tax.qmd`, kept in an HTML-comment reserve block, not merged into the live "recast-in-$M$" ELVIS section — sequencing was to show ELVIS fixing the MSM failure first (already live), only pitch the fully-unified version if that lands. Full derivation in `Paper/sections/9999-elvis.qmd`.

**Implementation details from the supplement/reference code (2026-08-25), evergreen.** Read `Lit-Papers/ELVIS_supplement.pdf` (32pp) and the reference GAUSS implementation in `Lit-Papers/ELVIS_code/` (`elvisutil.g`, `elvisexample.g`). (i) The confidence region should use the *profiled* statistic $\hat Q_n(\beta)=-(\sup_{\eta,\gamma}\hat L_n((\beta,\eta),\gamma)-\sup_{\theta,\gamma}\hat L_n(\theta,\gamma))$ — subtracting the global sup over the whole unrestricted model — not the raw $\hat L_n(\theta)$ against a threshold; (ii) Theorem F.1 hands a ready closed-form conservative critical value, $\{\theta:\sup_\gamma-2n\hat L_n(\theta,\gamma)\le\chi^2_{d_g,\alpha}\}$, no subsampling needed for a first pass — upgrade to Chernozhukov-Hong-Tamer (2007) subsampling only if a tighter region is wanted; (iii) two standard objective choices, both covered by her Definition F.1: empirical likelihood (Owen) or a CUE-type quadratic $-\tfrac12\hat g'\hat V^{-1}\hat g$ — the reference GAUSS code defaults to the CUE version; (iv) numerically, the reference code samples directly from the $\gamma$-tilted density via Metropolis (not a naive self-normalized importance-sampling ratio over the untilted $\rho$) — more robust with a bounded/awkward support like ours; (v) computationally, $\theta$ is gridded/profiled (its optimization problem is "difficult" — possibly a whole set of maximizers) while $(\text{nuisance},\gamma)$ is optimized via a derivative-free simplex (Nelder-Mead) for each $\theta$ (its problem is convex, unique global optimum).

**Sign/scaling convention for the eventual test statistic, evergreen (clarified against AK2020's actual code, 2026-08-31).** The $-2n\hat L_n(\theta,\gamma)\le\chi^2_{d_g,\alpha}$ formula is written in **Schennach's own sign convention**: her $\hat L_n$ is the *negative*, maximized CUE quadratic (her Definition F.1, $-\tfrac12\hat g'\hat V^{-1}\hat g$) — so $-2n\hat L_n=n\cdot g'V^{-1}g$, always non-negative, as a test statistic must be. Confirmed this is exactly what AK2020 do too, checked directly in their code (`/Volumes/SSD Hans/Github/TopicsDecisionMaking/ReplicationAK/FirstApp/`, also duplicated at `/Volumes/SSD Hans/Github/Adv Micro/Market Power/REP_0621/FirstApp/`): `cudafunctions/cuda_fastoptim.jl:67,100` defines their objective as `Qn2 = 1/2*dvecdum2'*Omega2*dvecdum2` — **positive**, **minimized** (opposite sign/direction from Schennach's $\hat L_n$) — and `procedures/1App_main.jl:78,96,103` builds their reported test statistic as `TSMC = 2*minf*n`, i.e. $2n\cdot Q_{n2,\min}$. Since $\hat L_n=-Q_{n2}$, this is algebraically identical to $-2n\hat L_n$ — the two conventions agree.
- **Why this matters for our own code, not just theory:** `cue_objective_common` in `Code/Deconvolution/1211-stage2-elvis-driver-AB.R` follows AK2020's convention exactly (`0.5*sum(d2^2/eig$values[pos])` — positive, minimized), not Schennach's. So `Lhat` (the `f$value` logged at every lambda point) is the AK2020-style object, not $\hat L_n$. **The correct test statistic is `TS = 2*n*Lhat`** (mirroring AK2020's literal `TSMC=2*minf*n`), compared against $\chi^2_{d_g,\alpha}$ — applying `-2n*Lhat` directly would be wrong, producing a negative statistic. $n$ is `n_used` (feasibility-filtered sample size); $d_g$ is the number of moment rows: checked directly in `Code/Rcpp/1200-stage2-elvis.cpp`, 8 for moment set A, `8+J+6` for moment set B ($J\approx29$ industries, so $d_g\approx43$ there — this was later corrected again to $8+J+7$ once the $h'$ score moment was added to B, see below).

**Second real-world ELVIS implementation found — Aguiar & Kashaev 2020 (2026-08-25).** `Lit-Papers/AK2020.pdf` (*Review of Economic Studies*, "Stochastic Revealed Preferences with Measurement Error") builds an ELVIS-based test of revealed-preference rationality under measurement error, with a full working Julia/CUDA implementation at `TopicsDecisionMaking/ReplicationAK/` — a second concrete reference beyond Schennach's own GAUSS code, and the first one built for an applied economics moment system that mixes inequality and equality/centering moments like ours. **Warm-start `M_hat` clipping fix, and what it revealed about $\hat\varepsilon$ heterogeneity (2026-08-27).** `1205-stage2-warmstart.R`'s plug-in $\hat M=M^*\exp(-(\mathcal V+\hat\varepsilon))$ (constant $\hat\varepsilon=$ median CORP epsilon) gives $\hat M>M^*$ (negative implied evasion) for **44.4%** of unincorp firm-periods — not measurement noise, but the model's own implication that a single constant plug-in must occasionally overshoot. **Stage-2 moment system, first finalized version — 15 moments (2026-08-28).** Replaced the old 7-moment system (`psi`, `psi·Z` for lagged K/L/cross-instrument, `eps·psi`, `om·psi`, `eps·om`) with 15, adding $\sigma^2_\psi$ as a fourth smooth parameter. $\gamma\in\mathbb R^{15}$, BOBYQA inner problem $4+15=19$-dimensional.
- **Dropped `psi*lag_k`/`psi*lag_l`/`psi*(cross-instrument)` entirely.** The closed-form materials-FOC inversion shows that given $\omega$, $M$ is a deterministic function of $(k,l)$ alone — so the lagged-K/L interactions were already, indirectly, testing $M\perp e\mid\omega$ rotated into observables. - **Rejected `E[psi*e]=0`** as tautological: given $\omega$, $e$ is the evasion FOC's own solution, a strictly monotonic invertible function of $\psi$ — not a testable independence.
- **Rejected `E[eM]-E[e]E[M]=0`** as actively misspecified (not merely uninformative) under $M\perp e\mid\omega$: by the law of total covariance, $\mathrm{Cov}(e,M)=E_\omega[\mathrm{Cov}(e,M\mid\omega)]+\mathrm{Cov}(E[e|\omega],E[M|\omega])$ — conditional independence kills only the first term; the second is generically nonzero since both $E[e|\omega]$ (U-shaped cost) and $E[M|\omega]$ (materials FOC) vary systematically with $\omega$.

**Planned optimization, scoped 2026-08-31: move `cue_objective_common`'s linear algebra into C++/Accelerate — plus a free algebraic simplification found while scoping it.** User wanted to eventually use Apple's Accelerate (vecLib/LAPACK) instead of R's `solve()`.
- **Found first: `solve(V2)` in `cue_objective_common` is mathematically redundant, verified numerically to ~1e-15.** `A`'s columns are `Omega`'s own eigenvectors, so $V_2=A'\Omega A$ is exactly diagonal by orthonormality — `solve(V2)` is just `diag(1/eig$values[pos])`. The whole function collapses to `d2 <- t(A) %*% dvec; 0.5 * sum(d2^2 / eig$values[pos])`, dropping two matrix multiplies and the `solve()` call entirely. Flagged to the user before applying (numerical-core function).
- **Honest scope check:** matrices here are tiny ($d_g\le21$); the per-firm Metropolis sampler (already C++/RcppParallel, thousands of firms × `n_burn+n_keep` steps) is almost certainly the dominant cost by orders of magnitude. The plausible win from C++ is removed R-interpreter/dispatch overhead per BOBYQA call, not raw compute — worth profiling (`Rprof()`) before investing effort.
- **Implementation route preference:** RcppArmadillo (`arma::eig_sym`, `arma::solve`) over raw Accelerate LAPACK calls — far less code, and Armadillo links against Accelerate as its BLAS/LAPACK backend when configured, avoiding the raw Fortran-interface gotchas below.
- **Raw-Accelerate gotcha, if ever pursued:** as of macOS 13.3+/Xcode 15, Accelerate's LAPACK symbols are exported under Apple-specific names (`$NEWLAPACK` suffix), not standard Fortran names. Also: `dgesv` (general LU solve, the routine on Apple's docs link) is the wrong routine here — `Omega`/`V2` are symmetric PSD, so `dsyev`/`dsyevr` or Cholesky-based `dpotrf`+`dpotri` would be the matching routines.

**Moment system upgrade: add $h'\cdot\varepsilon$ (set A) and $h'\cdot(\ln M-\mu_{m,j})$ (set B only), drop `eps*e` — agreed, then implemented with a scaling bug found and a fix agreed (2026-09-05/06).**
- $h'\equiv\partial h/\partial\lambda$ (linear $q$: $h'=\frac{-2e}{1-2\lambda e}$) is the Schennach-style score moment for $\lambda$ — the existing system already has this structure for every other smooth parameter (`psi`, `psi*om`, `psi*om^2` are the score moments for $\delta_0,\delta_1,\delta_2$) without anyone naming it that way; $\lambda$ was the one missing its own. $h'\cdot\varepsilon$ closes the gap validly (not tautologically): $\varepsilon\perp(\psi,\omega)$ is already maintained, and since $e$ (hence $h'$) is a deterministic function of $(\psi,\omega)$ alone, this implies $\varepsilon\perp h'$. $h'\cdot(\ln M-\mu_{m,j})$ (B only) is structurally identical to the already-valid `psi*(lnM-mu_m,j)`, adds exactly one pooled row. `eps*e` dropped (least $\lambda$-informative of the existing rows) to make room.
- **Ruled out:** $h'\cdot\omega$ (tautological — $\omega$ is a direct argument of $e^*$, same error class as the rejected `psi*e`); $h'\cdot k$/$h'\cdot l$ directly (redundant with $h'\cdot\ln M$); a "score for $\eta$" (doesn't exist in this form — $\eta$ enters via a sampler support restriction, not smoothly through $h(e)$).
- **Parked:** $h'$ interacted with the stage-1 AR(1) instruments — unclear which independence assumption would license it. Floated, not decided: second-order versions $h'\cdot(\varepsilon^2-\sigma^2_{\varepsilon,j})$, $h'\cdot(\ln M-\mu_{m,j})^2$.
- **Implemented, both `.cpp` files compile clean, moment set A run-tested — but the raw (untransformed) $h'$ has a real numerical scaling bug, found before trusting any result.** A first-pass 16-point run at trim=1% (lag_m), seeded from the already-known trim=1% fit under the old 8-moment system, produced suspiciously exact-zero $\hat L_n$ values (2e-12, 1.4e-13, 5e-17) at the high end of the grid. Diagnosed: at $\lambda=10^{-5}$, `diag(Omega)` showed row 6 (`h_prime*eps`) with variance $1.16\times10^{16}$ — ten orders of magnitude larger than every other row. `cue_objective_from_moments`'s relative eigenvalue truncation discards anything below `max(eig)*1e-10` — with row 6 inflating `max(eig)` this much, only the top 2 of 8 eigenvector directions survived, so the near-zero $\hat L_n$ reflected BOBYQA satisfying one numerically-dominant direction, not the whole system.
- **Why raw $h'$ blows up:** $h'$ diverges as $e\to(\frac1{2\lambda})^-$ for a *given* trial $\lambda$ — the same ceiling mechanism behind the whole extreme-evader investigation, now showing up numerically. Even after 1% trimming, some remaining firm's $e$ can land close enough to whatever $\lambda$ is currently being tested. The objective's own search does not naturally avoid this — the eigenvalue-truncation artifact made the danger zone look spuriously *good*, actively drawing BOBYQA toward it.
- **Fix agreed, not yet implemented at the time of this entry: bound $h'$ via $\exp(h')$** (or $c^{h'}$ for any base $c>1$) before forming the moment — still validly independent of $\varepsilon$ under the same argument, since validity never depended on $h'$'s specific functional form, only on it being *some* function of $e$ alone. Chose $\exp$ over a signed-log alternative since $h'<0$ always makes $\exp(h')\in(0,1)$ automatically, monotonic, no reference scale needed. **Theoretical justification for using *any* transform, not just monotonic ones:** (i) full-independence route — $E[f(e)\cdot\varepsilon]=E[f(e)]\cdot0=0$ for any $f$, given $\varepsilon\perp(\psi,\omega)$ and $E[\varepsilon]=0$; (ii) weaker conditional-mean route — $E[\varepsilon|e]=0\Rightarrow E[\varepsilon|h'(e)]=0$ by the tower property for *any* function of $e$. Monotonicity only matters for informativeness, not validity. Closest textbook precedent: Huber's M-estimation (Huber 1964) — standard practice in robust-GMM, not something Schennach's or AK2020's own moments needed since theirs don't hit this kind of derivative-driven blow-up.
- **Status at the time of this entry:** not yet implemented. The trim=1% product file with raw $h'$ is broken, do not use; the pre-$h'$ 8-moment trim=1% result is preserved with a `.bak-old8moments` suffix.

## $h'$ numerical fix and warm-start prototyping (2026-09-07)

**Starting point: user's own prototype in `1205-stage2-warmstart.R` tried z-score standardization of raw $h'$, reviewed for bugs.** Independently of the agreed `exp(h')` plan, the user had experimented with `(h_prime - mean(h_prime))/sd(h_prime)` as the bounding transform, handling the near-zero-denominator case via `ifelse(1-2*lambda*e_hat==0, -9e200, raw_formula)`. Code review found this didn't actually work as intended:
- **The `==0` guard is dead code.** Floating-point equality to exactly 0 essentially never happens for a continuous `lambda`/`e_hat` combination — the real danger is the denominator landing at `~1e-15`, not `0`. In that zone raw `h_prime` returns something like `±1e14`, which is `is.finite()` in R, so it silently passes every check.
- **`lambda_min <- 1/(2*max(e_hat))` sits at an EXACT mathematical singularity of the unclipped formula.** Plugging in: $1-2\lambda_{\min}e_{\max}=1-2\cdot\frac1{2e_{\max}}\cdot e_{\max}=0$ exactly (up to floating-point rounding) — the optimizer's own lower search bound was defined at precisely the point where the most extreme firm-draw's `h_prime` blows up.
- **`h`'s clip (`arg<-pmax(1-2*lambda*e_hat,1e-6)`) and `h_prime`'s denominator (the raw, unclipped expression) disagreed** — meaning `h_prime` could report a large nonzero slope exactly where the clip had already made `h` locally flat in $\lambda$, a derivative inconsistent with the function it's supposed to be the derivative of.

**Fix applied: `h_prime` reuses the SAME clipped `arg` as `h`.** One line (`h_prime <- -2*e_hat/arg`), removes the `ifelse`/sentinel entirely (never divides by less than `1e-6`), and restores derivative consistency. Rerun: `lag_m` still ran ~24 minutes without finishing (killed) — worse, not better, than before the fix. Diagnosed: `1e-6` is a far looser tolerance than the old (never-firing) `==0` check, so the floor now caps a real, non-trivial share of the 7.26M-row sample simultaneously as $\lambda$ moves (each observation has its own threshold $\lambda_i^*$ where it crosses onto the floor) — this changes the objective's shape at many points at once, and with `mean`/`sd` recomputed fresh every `gmm_obj` call, L-BFGS-B's numerical gradient/line search started chasing a moving target.

**Switched the warm-start's transform to `exp(h_prime)` (of the now-floored `h_prime`), not z-score normalization.** Once floored, `h_prime<=0` always (`e>=0`, `arg>0`), so `exp(h_prime)` is bounded in $(0,1]$ — a pointwise transform of one observation's own $e$, no dependence on the rest of the sample. Rerun: `lag_m` converged cleanly in ~4.3 min (`optim`'s `convergence=0`). `lag_2_cal_W` returned `convergence=52` — an L-BFGS-B **line-search failure** (not just hitting `maxit`), still finished (finite output), but not a validated optimum.

**Root cause of the line-search failure, and why it's mostly a warm-start-specific wall.** `arg`'s floor creates a kink at each observation's own crossing-$\lambda$; with ~7.26M distinct values these are effectively continuous kinks across the tested $\lambda$ range. L-BFGS-B's finite-difference gradient can be locally unreliable near enough of them. But the *real* ELVIS estimator never searches $\lambda$ smoothly at all — $\lambda$ is gridded, and the inner BOBYQA optimization runs at one fixed $\lambda$ per call, so the specific mechanism that broke L-BFGS-B here (kinks accumulating *as $\lambda$ varies*) can't recur the same way there. Two things do carry over: (i) BOBYQA is already derivative-free (trust-region quadratic model, no line search) — the real estimator was never as exposed to this failure mode as the warm-start's L-BFGS-B choice; (ii) $\eta$ (the relative-evasion floor, which sets the sampler's support ceiling) is the one inner-problem parameter that could echo a milder version of the same kink, worth watching once the floor lands in the real C++ code, though BOBYQA should handle it better than L-BFGS-B did.

**Switched the warm-start's optimizer to `nloptr::neldermead` (bounded), not `stats::optim(method="Nelder-Mead")`** — base R's Nelder-Mead silently ignores `lower`/`upper` (only `L-BFGS-B`/`Brent` respect bounds in `stats::optim`), which would have quietly stopped enforcing `lambda_min`/`lambda_max`/the delta box. Nelder-Mead never estimates a gradient (compares function values at simplex vertices only), so it has no line search to fail. Rerun (`maxeval=2000` default): both instruments finished without error in ~7.7 min combined — `lag_m` genuinely converged (`NLOPT_XTOL_REACHED`, $\hat\lambda\approx1.817\times10^{-8}$), `lag_2_cal_W` hit the `maxeval` cap (`NLOPT_MAXEVAL_REACHED`, not an error, just not fully converged, $\hat\lambda\approx7.57\times10^{-8}$). A targeted rerun of `lag_2_cal_W` alone with `maxeval=30000` (new `ins_only`/`maxeval_nm` CLI args added to `1205-stage2-warmstart.R`) converged genuinely in only ~7 min / 2725 iterations (`NLOPT_XTOL_REACHED`, $\hat\lambda\approx1.879\times10^{-8}$) — far faster than a naive linear extrapolation from the 2000-eval run suggested, and landing almost exactly on `lag_m`'s own value.

**Cross-check against the real estimator: both warm-start $\hat\lambda$'s land almost exactly on the real, full-scale untrimmed estimator's own Phase-0 finding** ($1.836\times10^{-8}$ for `lag_m`, $1.800\times10^{-8}$ for `lag_2_cal_W`, from the 2026-09-05 continuous-chain sweep). A crude 4-moment GMM warm start, built completely differently (plain pooled mean, no gamma/tilt, no MCMC), landing almost on top of the real 8-moment CUE/BOBYQA/Metropolis machinery's minimum is good evidence the `h'` score moment carries real identifying content once computed soundly, not just numerical noise.

**Ported the same fix into the real C++ estimator.** `Code/Rcpp/1200-stage2-elvis-common.h`: added a shared `H_DENOM_FLOOR=1e-6` and `h_denom(e,lambda)` helper, used by both `h_of_e` and `h_prime_of_e` (flooring `h_of_e` too, not just `h_prime_of_e`, for the same consistency argument — a real behavior change to every `psi`-based moment, though only in the same vanishing near-ceiling sliver, flagged explicitly before applying). `moment_g_A_one`/`moment_g_B_one` (`1200-stage2-elvis.cpp`/`-B.cpp`) changed to `std::exp(h_prime_of_e(e,lambda))` in place of the raw value. Compiled clean via `sourceCpp` for both files. Smoke-tested end-to-end: moment set A (single $\lambda$ point, real sample) converged with `max|gamma|=1.45` (vs. the ~1e16-variance-row collapse the raw version produced); moment set B ran clean too (hit its toy `maxeval` cap as expected at that tiny scale, no NaN/crash).

**Does `draw_from_rho_eta` already prevent the singularity? No — confirmed directly, not just reasoned about.** `u01` comes from `std::uniform_real_distribution<double>(0.0,1.0)`, mathematically `[0,1)` — never returns exactly 1, so the sampler genuinely guarantees $e<1/(2\lambda)$ (or the $\eta$-adjusted bound) strictly, no division by exactly zero. But a continuous uniform draw puts positive density arbitrarily close to `u01=1`, so across enough draws (thousands of firms × `n_burn+n_keep` steps × every BOBYQA evaluation) some land close enough that $1-2\lambda e\sim10^{-14}$ — finite, undetected, still large enough to dominate `cov(Ghat)`'s eigenvalues. Direct proof this isn't just theoretical: the original raw-$h'$ bug (row 6 variance $\sim1.16\times10^{16}$x every other row) was found **with** `draw_from_rho_eta` already active in the sampler — the sampler fix and the floor fix solve two different problems (legal support vs. bounded magnitude within that support), both needed.

**Is the floor a numerical fix only, or does it change the theoretical estimates?** Judged fundamentally numerical: the floor only ever activates in the sliver where $1-2\lambda e<10^{-6}$, and in that sliver the *true* $h(e)$ already diverges to $-\infty$ — there's no finite ground truth for a floor to distort, since any floating-point implementation has to pick *some* way to represent that limit (the uncontrolled alternative, raw hardware rounding, isn't more faithful, just less controlled). Doesn't touch $\theta$'s definition or the population moment conditions. One open, cheap-to-run robustness check flagged but not yet done: refit with a tighter floor (e.g. `1e-8`/`1e-10`) and confirm $\hat\lambda$/the CI don't move — if stable across floor choices, confirms it's purely numerical; if not, the floor is doing quiet economic work and needs more care.

## Trim re-check with the fixed $h'$ moment (2026-09-07)

**Question reopened: does the now-bounded `exp(h')` score moment remove the NEED to trim, or just the numerical corruption it was causing?** Hypothesis going in (later borne out): probably still need to trim. The original flat-plateau finding that motivated the 1% decision was established using the *old* 8-moment system, before the `h'` score moment existed at all (that slot held `eps*e`) — so it's a property of `h(e)` itself (used in `psi`, most of the other rows), not the new moment's numerics. And `h'(e)\to-2e$ as $\lambda\to0$ (regardless of transform) still scales with $e$, so distinguishing small candidate $\lambda$'s still needs firms with large enough $e$ — `exp(h')` is a *monotonic* transform of `h'` alone, so it can bound how much one draw's raw magnitude dominates the CUE weighting matrix, but doesn't change *which* firms carry the identifying signal.

**Design: 5 trim levels (0%, 0.1%, 0.5%, 1%, 5%) instead of the original 11, both instruments, full 16-point $\lambda$ grid ($10^{-9}$ to $10^{-4}$), real settings (`n_burn=500,n_keep=1000`)** — smoke-tested first at toy scale (confirmed the cross-trim warm-start chaining, including the never-before-explicitly-tested `trim_top_pct=0` case, works cleanly), then launched at real scale via `run-trim-sequential.sh`, both instruments sequentially on one machine (not concurrently, to avoid oversubscribing 12 cores with two `n_cores=10` jobs at once).

**Result: the old pathology (flat, never-turns-around plateau as $\lambda\to0$) is gone for BOTH instruments** — every trim level, both instruments, now shows a genuine bracketed minimum (all 80 grid points converged, `conv=1` throughout, verified not assumed). That part of the hypothesis was right.

**But the clean multi-level stabilization turned out to be instrument-specific, not automatic.** `lag_m` locks onto $\hat\lambda\approx2.15\times10^{-5}$ across three consecutive levels (0.5%, 1%, 5%) — argmins $4.64\times10^{-7}$ (0%), $4.64\times10^{-8}$ (0.1%, actually *smaller* than untrimmed), then $2.15\times10^{-5}$, $2.15\times10^{-5}$, $2.15\times10^{-5}$ (0.5%/1%/5%). `lag_2_cal_W` instead sits at a much smaller, flatter plateau around $\lambda\approx10^{-6}$ through 0%, 0.1%, 0.5% (three levels agreeing with *each other*, not with `lag_m`), a broad weakly-resolved trough at 1% ($\lambda\approx4.64\times10^{-7}$, nearly tied across a decade of the grid), and only moves toward `lag_m`'s territory at 5% ($10^{-5}$, about half of `lag_m`'s value) via a sharp jump rather than a gradual convergence. Consistent with (not contradicting) this project's repeated prior finding that `lag_2_cal_W` behaves less stably than `lag_m`.

**Built `Code/Deconvolution/1222-stage2-trim-all-gridpoints-plot.R`** (fills a gap — the original all-gridpoints figure, `Paper/images/1223-stage2-trim-all-gridpoints.png` from 2026-09-05, was generated ad hoc with no saved script) — plots every tested grid point, test-inversion pass/fail ($TS=2n[\hat L_n(\lambda)-\min_{\lambda'}\hat L_n(\lambda')]$ vs. $\chi^2_{8,0.95}=15.51$) rather than just a summary CI band, trim on a discrete equally-spaced axis (a continuous axis was tried and rejected the first time this kind of figure was made — it bunches everything near zero). Output: `Paper/images/1222-stage2-trim-all-gridpoints-refixed.png`, raw table `Code/Products/1222-stage2-trim-all-gridpoints.csv`.

**Decision: trim cutoff REVISED from 1% to 0.5%**, reading the criterion off the 95% CI's **upper bound** across levels (per the user's own read of the figure), not the point estimate. For `lag_m`, the upper bound jumps between 0.1% and 0.5%, then holds flat across 0.5%/1%/5% (small further jump at 5%) — 0.5% is the first level where it stabilizes. For `lag_2_cal_W`, the upper bound is broadly stable from 0% through 5% except a one-off widening exactly at 1% (visible in the figure as a much wider spread of passing points at that level) — since 0.5% itself isn't affected by that widening, it doesn't argue against 0.5% as a safe choice. Both instruments are consistent with 0.5%; neither points at needing the heavier 1% cut. Same underlying investigation as the original 1% decision (extreme-evader leverage on $\lambda$ under linear $q$, firm/industry composition sanity checks) — only the specific cutoff value changed, not the reasoning behind trimming at all.

**Not yet done:** a full Phase-0-style dense/continuous-chain sweep + closed-form test-inversion CI *at* the 0.5% cutoff specifically (this revision used the coarser 16-point grid across 5 trim levels, not Phase 0's original fine sweep) — needed for a precise $\hat\lambda$/CI to actually feed the counterfactual, not just to confirm the cutoff choice. The counterfactual's Phase 1 remains blocked on this.

## Standalone $(\lambda,\delta_1,\delta_2)$ grid estimator: built, calibrated, then paused in favor of moment set A (2026-09-07/08)

**Motivation:** the 0.5%-trim moment-set-A fit came back with $\hat\delta_2<0$ for both instruments — wrong sign for the U-shaped cost story. Per the "grid parameters of interest jointly, profile true nuisance" rule, decided to grid $(\lambda,\delta_1,\delta_2)$ directly (profiling $\delta_0$) rather than trust a single black-box inner optimization, to see the actual shape of the objective surface and whether $\delta_2>0$ is genuinely rejected.

**Built a standalone C++ binary** (`Code/C-estimator/`), not another Rcpp module, on the premise that eliminating R-interpreter overhead between BOBYQA calls would meaningfully speed this up. Along the way:
- **`h'` transform corrected a second time.** The first fix (`exp(h')`, from the earlier session) turned out to underflow to exactly `0.0` for ~73-75% of firms (median $e\approx841\Rightarrow h'\approx-1683$, deep in `exp`'s underflow zone) — silently gutting the score moment for most of the sample, not a numerical nicety. Replaced with $h'/(1-h')$ (bounded $(-1,0]$, decays polynomially not exponentially, stays numerically distinguishable across the full range of $e$) — implemented once as `h_prime_bounded` in the shared `1200-stage2-elvis-common.h`, live in moment sets A, B, and the (now-paused) grid estimator's set C simultaneously. Corner-firm handling corrected to match: `h_prime_bounded(0,\cdot)=0` exactly (the old `exp(0)=1` value was inconsistent with A/B's own established "leave this row at 0 for corner firms" convention).
- **Moment set C, built and then found wanting.** Started at the user's proposed 6 rows (psi, psi·om, psi·om², eps·h', psi·eps, psi·eps·om) — dropped ALL of A's eps-only rows, so corner firms (10.36% of the trimmed sample) contributed exactly zero to every row. Added raw `eps` back as a 7th row (gradually, one at a time). $\hat L_n$ values from this reduced set came back substantially higher than moment set A's — a real signal the reduction cost real fit quality, not just corner-firm identification, reinforcing the eventual decision to abandon it (see below).
- **R vs. C++ speed: no gain at this scale.** Single-threaded, R/Rcpp and the C++ binary are identical (1.72s vs 1.73s per CUE-objective call at `n_burn=500,n_keep=2000`; `dvec` bit-identical). The premise was wrong for this regime — R's per-call overhead mattered a lot back when moment set B was built at `n_burn=100,n_keep=1000` (R's `cov()` was ~95% of an ~18ms call then), but at 500/2000 raw MCMC compute dominates so heavily that marshalling overhead is noise. What actually mattered was per-firm threading, which the standalone build initially lacked — added via `std::thread` (mirroring RcppParallel's split) plus a shell-level shard mechanism (`run_grid_shards.sh`).
- **Amdahl's law fit from real measurements: serial fraction $f\approx5\%$.** From $T_1=1.73$s, $T(4\text{ threads})=109.0$s, $T(12\text{ threads})=49.0$s per point (same 101 evals both times, deterministic). Implied pure task-parallelism ($S=12$ single-threaded shards) is throughput-optimal — confirmed no contention penalty running concurrent single-threaded shards. This was a genuine, useful result, but see below for the gap it had.
- **Settled: `n_burn=500,n_keep=2000` is necessary, `250/1000` is not an adequate substitute even for exploration.** Same grid point, both genuinely converged (`conv=1`, not a `maxtime` artifact): $\hat L_n$ differed by ~2x (0.061 vs 0.113), $\hat\eta$ by ~36%, several $\gamma$ by an order of magnitude. Real, substantive sensitivity to MCMC noise level, not optimizer noise.
- **Progressive random point sampling (with a persistent "already-run" tracker) was designed, then abandoned in favor of Chebyshev-shell center-out expansion** before ever being run — random sampling has no natural "nearby already-solved point" to warm-start from, reintroducing the exact path-dependence problem the trim sweep already taught this project to avoid (naive/reset warm starts produced 2-4 orders of magnitude of spurious noise there). Center-out on the lattice guarantees a genuine neighbor is already solved before each new point is attempted.
- **Shell-mode built:** center solved via multi-start (3 genuinely different starting points, best kept — since a bad center would silently propagate outward through every chained point, looking stable while being consistently wrong); shells 1..$d$ processed in Chebyshev-distance order, each point warm-started from its nearest already-solved neighbor (log-$\lambda$ + linear $\delta_1,\delta_2$ distance), shell-wise dynamic thread allocation.
- **First real 27-point ($1+26$, shell 0+1) run at `n_burn=500,n_keep=2000`, `threads_per_point` dynamic (1 for the 26-point shell): only 7 of 27 points (26%) genuinely converged** (`conv=1`, not `maxtime`-capped) — the 90s→300s `maxtime` inherited from earlier smoke tests was far too tight once shell-1 batched down to 1 thread/point (single-threaded convergence takes meaningfully longer in wall-clock, even though total compute is the same). Test-inversion on just those 7 converged points ($d_g=7$, $\chi^2_{7,0.95}=14.07$): only the argmin itself passed, everything else (including the center) rejected by 500-6000+ — not read as a real finding, almost certainly a symptom of the tiny/unreliable converged sample, not the true surface shape.
- **Added real diagnostics** before rerunning: per-point wall-clock time (not just cumulative), full pass-1+pass-2 iteration/convergence accounting, and an explicit `threads_per_point` override (the earlier "pure $S=12$" throughput conclusion assumed points always converge — it never accounted for a fixed `maxtime` interacting with convergence reliability, a real gap once single-threaded points needed far longer wall-clock to hit the same eval count). A second 27-point run at `threads_per_point=2` and generous `maxtime=1200` was in progress (center fully converged genuinely, 97-671 evals/pass, 126-255s/point at 12 threads) when the session redirected — killed before shell 1 finished, not wasted (the calibration data already gathered — real per-point timings, real iteration counts — is preserved in the `.Rout` log even though the run itself was stopped).

**Decision: paused, not abandoned outright — moment set A is the active path again, only swapping one row.** $\hat L_n$ from the reduced moment set C came back substantially worse than moment set A's fits; moment set A's three eps-only rows give corner firms real identifying content the counterfactual will need once firms start transitioning into the corner as $\Delta$ varies; and moment set A already has reference estimates ($\hat\delta_1,\hat\delta_2$ in economically sensible ranges, $\hat\lambda$ around $10^{-5}$) to compare a new grid against. The one thing moment set A didn't have until today: its `h'` row already uses `h_prime_bounded` (fixed in the SAME edit that fixed the standalone build's version, since both draw from the shared header) — so "going back to moment set A" is not undoing today's core fix, just dropping the 6/7-row reduction and the standalone-C++ detour.

**Plan for next session:** (1) grid $\lambda$ alone on moment set A (reusing `fit_one_lambda_A`'s existing structure, nothing new to build) — coarse then fine, compare against the known reference estimates; (2) grid $(\delta_1,\delta_2)$ alone at the best $\lambda$ from (1) — coarse then fine (this needs a small new piece: `1211-stage2-elvis-driver-AB.R` currently only grids $\lambda$, not $(\delta_1,\delta_2)$ at fixed $\lambda$); (3) use the results of (1) and (2) as an *informed* center — not an arbitrary grid-midpoint — before attempting anything like the full joint 3-D exploration again. The `Code/C-estimator/` shell/chaining/threading infrastructure is validated and reusable (real, working engineering) if a joint grid is wanted later with moment set A's structure ported in — just not the next step.

The fix that actually worked: every $\lambda$ point fit **independently** from the identical seed (`lag_m`'s best-fit par), no chaining across $\lambda$ at all (only the existing within-point 2-pass BOBYQA refinement still applies). **Context: the 49-point (delta1,delta2) grid (lag_m, moment set A, trim=0.5%) came back visibly jagged** (Section: earlier same-day entry) — only 5/49 points passed the $\chi^2_9$ test, scattered rather than contiguous, and the 3D surface showed sharp spikes rather than a smooth bowl. ## Counterfactual revenue collapse traced to a structural property: linear $q$ + linear $\kappa$ makes new evasion an exact linear function of baseline evasion, with a firm-invariant intercept (2026-09-10)

**Motivating question: why does the forward-simulated revenue curve drop so fast after $\Delta>0$?** Re-ran the forward simulation (no optimization) with the new best no-$\eta$ lag_m fit ($\lambda=3.501\times10^{-7}$) — crossing point moved to $\Delta\approx0.033$ (from $\approx0.020$ at $\lambda=2.2\times10^{-7}$ and $\approx0.012$ at the original $\eta$-based $\lambda=1.3\times10^{-7}$), confirming the crossing scales linearly with $\lambda$ to two decimal places across all three fits (ratios 1.70 and 1.59 matching the $\lambda$ ratios 1.69 and 1.59 exactly) — strong evidence this is the model's own math, not noise from any one fit.

**The user's own question sharpened the mechanism precisely: is $e'(\Delta)$ weighted by the firm's original evasion?** Expanding the closed form settles it exactly:
$$e'(\Delta)=\frac{\Delta+2\lambda e_i}{2\lambda(1+\Delta)}=\underbrace{\frac{e_i}{1+\Delta}}_{\text{weighted by }e_i}+\underbrace{\frac{\Delta}{2\lambda(1+\Delta)}}_{\text{same for every firm}}$$
Yes and no: $e_i$ enters with slope $1/(1+\Delta)$, but there's a second, additive term with no $e_i$ (or $\tau_P,\rho_t,\omega,\psi$) dependence at all — identical across the entire firm population. Verified this is exact, not approximate, by re-deriving from the FOC directly: $\kappa$ linear in $e$ $\Rightarrow$ $\partial\kappa/\partial e=C(\omega,\psi)$ is a *constant* (not a function of $e$) $\Rightarrow$ the FOC $\tau\rho_t(1-2\lambda e)=C$ is linear in $e$ $\Rightarrow$ a benefit-side shock ($\Delta$) shifts every firm's equilibrium $e$ by the *same absolute amount*, independent of firm size or current evasion level. This is THE reason the intercept term is firm-invariant, and it's a mechanical consequence of the headline linear-$\kappa$ specification, not a bug in the closed-form derivation — a cost function with increasing marginal cost (e.g. quadratic in $e$) would instead dampen the response for firms already evading heavily.

**User's own framing of the scope caveat, worth keeping verbatim: under this linear assumption, the counterfactual shouldn't be pushed too far in $\Delta$, because every firm's evasion increases by the same uniform shift on top of a term that already embeds their productivity and cost draw — and since $(\omega,\psi)$ are held fixed throughout the exercise (by construction, per the Nail Kashaev correction against "recovering and holding fixed" a type), the model has no way to let firms respond on any OTHER margin (output, materials sourcing, exit) that a large tax change would plausibly trigger in reality.** This is now recorded as a settled structural property of the model (CLAUDE.md's Counterfactual section) rather than a one-off empirical curiosity.

## Diminishing marginal returns to a purchases-tax cut identify an economically motivated "optimal" stopping point — the headline story (2026-09-10)

**Before relaunching with a wider R-grid, the user asked the sharper question: why is $\theta_{\text{smooth}}=(\lambda,\delta_0,\delta_1,\delta_2)$ being reprofiled at every cell in the first place, rather than fixed?** The economic argument, laid out directly: we already know $\lambda$ is a *weakly-identified-once-it's-free* parameter (that's the entire $\eta$-removal story from the day before) — if $\lambda$ is left free at every $(\Delta,R)$ cell, it can silently readjust the implied detection probability to make almost *any* candidate $R$ look consistent with the data, exactly the mechanism that made $\eta$ dangerous, one level up. **Checked directly against the just-finished run's own output: confirmed, dramatically.** $\hat\lambda$ ranges $1.63\times10^{-8}$ to $9.19\times10^{-7}$ across the 35 cells — a $56\times$ spread — and swings $43\times$ *within a single* $\Delta=-4\%$ across just its own 5 $R$ candidates ($1.69\times10^{-8}$ to $7.28\times10^{-7}$). The "same-point independent seeding, no chaining" fix from the day before only prevented cross-cell *drift* into different basins (which it did — $\delta_1,\delta_2$ stayed rock-stable) — it never stopped $\lambda$ from moving *within* each cell's own optimization to whatever value best rationalizes that cell's candidate $R$. Seeding and fixing are different things; this run only did the former.

**Literature check, performed before adopting the fix (not just asserted): does AK2020 or Schennach say anything about fixing vs. profiling structural parameters when constructing a policy counterfactual?** Full-text search of `ELVIS.pdf` and `ELVIS_supplement.pdf`: zero occurrences of "counterfactual." Schennach's 2022 JEL survey: exactly one occurrence, a throwaway motivating clause in the introduction with no methodology attached. She simply never addresses this question. Schennach's Example 1.5/Fig S.3 (the OTHER citation on that same CLAUDE.md bullet) turned out on inspection to be a joint CI for a model's own two full structural parameters, with no separate nuisance block at all — it never spoke to this question either way; the citation there was over-generalized, not wrong per se.

**Settled design going forward, user's own framing worth keeping verbatim: the counterfactual question is "given these sensible, fixed parameter estimates, what would the counterfactual revenue be, using the distribution of unobservables that is consistent with the data and the most reasonable parameter estimates?" — $\gamma$ freely chosen by the optimizer at each cell IS the "distribution of unobservables" half of that sentence.** $\theta_{\text{smooth}}$ gets fixed at a well-justified point estimate; $\gamma$ (all of it) stays the only thing NLopt touches per cell.

## Why $\lambda$ is structurally harder to identify than $(\delta_1,\delta_2)$ — a theoretical derivation, not just an empirical pattern (2026-09-11/12)

**Motivated by staring at the cube's own $\delta_0$ column: $\delta_0$ tracks $(\delta_1,\delta_2)$ almost mechanically (nearly constant across different $\lambda$'s within a column, jumping when $(\delta_1,\delta_2)$ change), while $\hat\omega^*=\delta_1/2\delta_2$ stays remarkably close to $\approx4$ across nearly every point that passes, at every grid this project has run.** First framed the question as an $E[\psi|\omega]$ conditional-mean argument: since $\psi=h(e)-\delta_0+\delta_1\omega-\delta_2\omega^2$ holds firm-by-firm as an identity (the FOC rearranged), and $\psi\perp\omega$ is a maintained model assumption, $E[\psi|\omega]=0 \Rightarrow E[h(e)|\omega]=\delta_0-\delta_1\omega+\delta_2\omega^2$ — a quadratic regression whose coefficients are, in the idealized nonparametric-conditional-mean sense, exactly identified given $\lambda$ (standard polynomial-regression identification, needing only that $\{1,\omega,\omega^2\}$ isn't collinear over $\omega$'s support). Initial read: "identified given $\lambda$, with $\lambda$'s own identification coming from a separate, thinner channel" — presented as basically fine.

**User's direct pushback, and it's the sharper, correct version of the point: for a GIVEN $\lambda$, can't you always just refit $\delta_0$ (and the rest) to match, with $(\delta_1,\delta_2)$ unaffected since they capture curvature, not level?** Re-derived properly rather than defending the first answer. The key fact the first pass glossed over: $\psi$ is **linear** in $(\delta_0,\delta_1,\delta_2)$ for *any* fixed $\lambda$ — $\lambda$ only ever enters through $h(e)$, never through the $\theta_{\text{smooth}}$ coefficients themselves. Moment set A's four $\psi$-only rows ($\psi$, $\psi\omega$, $\psi\omega^2$, $\psi\ln M$) are consequently four *linear* equations in the three unknowns $(\delta_0,\delta_1,\delta_2)$, holding for whatever $\lambda$ is plugged in. Taking the first three of these: a $3\times3$ linear system, solvable exactly for $(\delta_0,\delta_1,\delta_2)$ given *any* $\lambda$ at all (generic nonsingularity of $\omega$'s moment matrix is the only requirement). **These three moments therefore have *zero* power to discriminate between candidate $\lambda$'s — not weak power, none, by construction** — confirming the user's intuition exactly and precisely, not just approximately.

**Where $\lambda$'s real identifying power comes from, once this is accounted for:** (1) the 4th $\psi$-moment ($\psi\ln M$) is the only overidentifying restriction within this block, capped by how independently $\ln M$ varies from $\{1,\omega,\omega^2\}$ — likely thin, since $\omega$ is itself built from $\ln M$ via the production-function/AR(1) machinery; (2) the genuinely nonlinear channel, $\ln(1-2\lambda e)$'s curvature, exploited *only* by the $h'_{\text{bounded}}(e,\lambda)\cdot\varepsilon$ score moment — informative only for firms near the FOC ceiling, a thin tail of aggressive evaders. **This reframes the entire session's $\lambda$-identification saga as one consistent structural fact, not a series of unrelated problems**: $\eta$ stealing $\lambda$'s work (because $\eta$ gave the sampler support an alternative, easier way to satisfy the moments without $\lambda$ doing anything), the coarse-vs-fine grid basin instability, and now the $\lambda$-$(\delta_1,\delta_2)$ ridge in the cube — all downstream of $(\delta_1,\delta_2)$ being pinned down by a strong, whole-sample linear signal while $\lambda$ only ever gets a thin, nonlinear, tail-only one.

**Confirmed by the user as consistent with prior project history: this is exactly why adding the $h'_{\text{bounded}}\cdot\varepsilon$ score moment (2026-09-07) helped in the first place** — it was, and remains, the *only* source of genuine nonlinear leverage on $\lambda$ in the whole moment set. **Proposed next step, not yet implemented: a second, higher-order score-type moment** (e.g. $E[\varepsilon^2\cdot h'_{\text{bounded}}]$, or another nonlinear-in-$e$ functional) to give $\lambda$ a second, independent nonlinear anchor rather than relying on the single existing score row alone. Flagged for discussion with Nail Kashaev before implementing — a real methodology question, not just an engineering one.

## Diagnosing why the $(\Delta,R)$ confidence sets are so wide: a new `dvecdiag` mode, the "gamma is frozen at $\Delta=0$" puzzle solved, and the R-moment's SE traced to firm-size heterogeneity (2026-09-12)

**Motivating question, following up on the wide/overlapping $(\Delta,R)$ CIs found the previous session (see "Real $(\Delta,R)$ revgrid relaunched overnight" above): does $\gamma$ actually adjust across candidate $R$'s at a given $\Delta$, and if the answer is "essentially no" at $\Delta=0$, what mechanically lets a wide range of $R$ candidates pass the test?**

**New read-only diagnostic, `dvecdiag` mode (`Code/C-estimator/grid_estimator.cpp`), reuses the existing `compute_dvec_omega_R` internals — dumps the full 10-row $\hat d_n$ vector and each row's own sampling SE ($\sqrt{\text{diag}(\Omega)/n}$) at a GIVEN, already-fixed $(\theta,\gamma,\Delta,R)$, no NLopt call at all.** Smoke-tested against a known combined-CSV row (bit-for-bit $\hat L_n$ match) before use.

**Finding 1: at exactly $\Delta=0$, $\gamma$ is frozen (bit-identical, distance $\sim10^{-10}$) to the shared seed for EVERY tested candidate $R$ — including wide-grid extremes that reject. This is a real structural degeneracy, not an optimizer failure.** Traced to the closed form itself: $e'(\Delta)=\max(0,\frac{e_i}{1+\Delta}+\frac{\Delta}{2\lambda(1+\Delta)})$ collapses to $e'(0)=e_i$ identically (verified directly against `resolve_e_prime_revenue`'s code). At $\Delta=0$ the new R-moment is trying to tilt the exact same object ($e_i$) that three existing moment-set-A rows already pin down — there's no genuinely new identifying direction for $\gamma_{10}$ to exploit there, so NLopt (which DID run ~230–260 evals, `XTOL_REACHED`, not an early exit) correctly finds no improving move. Away from $\Delta=0$ the extra $\Delta/(2\lambda(1+\Delta))$ term is a firm-invariant shift absent from every other moment, which is presumably why $\gamma$ moves normally at every other tested $\Delta$ (confirmed: $\Delta=+0.1\%$, only 0.1pp away, shows normal $\gamma$ movement).

**Finding 2: even where $\gamma$ DOES move a lot (checked at $\Delta=-0.04$, where the $\psi$-multiplier ranges 0.034–0.183 across 5 candidate $R$'s), the achieved implied mean revenue barely moves at all (1764.8–1768.2, a 3-unit range) regardless of which $R$ is being tested.** The large $\gamma$ swings are being spent trading off *other* moments against each other (e.g. $\mathbb E[\varepsilon\cdot e]$ swings from 14.5 to $-0.04$ across the same 5 cells) — not on shifting $E[R]$. The model genuinely tries, and fails, to move its own implied revenue mean.

**Finding 3: the R-moment's own standard error, $\approx559.4$, is essentially IDENTICAL across every cell checked — both $\Delta$'s, frozen-$\gamma$ or wandering-$\gamma$, passing or rejected — and, verified empirically, completely unaffected by MCMC chain length** (`n_keep\in\{300,3000,30000\}$, a 100x range, gives `se10=559.36` to 4 significant figures every time). This settles that `se10` is a **between-firm** quantity (sample variance of each firm's own MCMC-averaged $g_{10}$ across the $n=32{,}232$ firms), not a simulation-noise artifact — no amount of extra MCMC draws will tighten it.

**Conclusion handed to the user: the wide, overlapping confidence sets are a precision/power problem, not a flexibility-of-the-model problem.** Nothing is being "found" by the optimizer to rationalize far-off $R$'s; the R-moment alone just doesn't have enough power, given how much revenue varies by firm size, to sharply pin down $E[R]$.

### Fix, proposed and empirically validated: a control-variate correction using the firm's OWN observed $t1_i/pgdp_i$ — beats industry×year TWFE by ~15x

**User's proposal: reduce the heterogeneity via industry × year fixed effects (different sectors pay different rates, tax policy changed over years).** Checked directly rather than assumed (`/tmp/twfe_check2.R`, using the ACTUAL model-implied $R_i(\Delta)$ from a fresh `revenue_baseline` forward-sim, not a naive $t1/pgdp$ proxy — an early version of this check made exactly that tautological mistake and was caught/redone): industry+year *additive* FE barely helps (SE $559\to508$); the full industry×year *interaction* (318 cells) does real work (SE $559\to223$, R²=0.84) but carries real overfitting risk (100/318 cells have <30 obs, 25 have <10).

**A much better, much cheaper alternative exists: the firm's own already-observed $t1_i/pgdp_i$ (raw sales-tax paid, no model dependence at all) is a near-perfect linear predictor of $R_i(\Delta)$ — R²$\approx$0.999 at both $\Delta=0$ and $\Delta=-0.04$, slope $\hat\beta\approx0.999$ both times.** This is the standard control-variate / regression-adjusted-mean trick (CUPED in the causal-inference literature; TWFE is the same family of idea with a saturated-categorical control instead of a continuous one) — replace $g_{10,i}=R_i/pgdp_i-R$ with $g_{10,i}^{\text{adj}}=(R_i/pgdp_i-\beta c_i)-(R-\beta\mu_c)$, $c_i=t1_i/pgdp_i$, $\beta\approx0.999$ and $\mu_c=\text{mean}(c_i)\approx2507.6$ both **precomputed constants from data, not new NLopt decision variables** (verified algebraically and numerically that this preserves $E[g_{10}^{\text{adj}}]=E[g_{10}]$ exactly — the target $R$ is untouched, only its variance shrinks). Implied SE: $559\to\approx14$, a ~38-41x improvement — roughly 15x better than the industry×year interaction's 2.5x, with zero added dimensionality and none of the small-cell risk. Once $t1_i$ is controlled for, industry×year adds almost nothing further (R² 0.9993→0.9994), confirming it was mostly a noisy proxy for firm size all along.

### Second alternative proposed by the user, implemented and compared head-to-head: test the REVENUE LOSS due to evasion instead of raw R

**Idea: since $t1_i$ (pure firm-size heterogeneity, nothing to do with evasion) is what's swamping $R_i$'s precision, why not target the loss directly — the uncaught-evasion leakage $\text{Loss}_i(\Delta)=\tilde\tau_P\cdot(1-q(e'))\cdot e'$ — which by construction excludes the $M_i\tilde\tau_P$ "legitimate credit" term that carries the same size heterogeneity as $t1_i$?** New output added to `revenue_baseline` mode (`RevenueResult` struct extended with `Loss_nominal`/`Loss_real`, computed on the same MCMC draws already being generated for $R$ — no extra simulation cost).

**Result, compared head-to-head with the CV-adjusted-$R$ approach at two $\Delta$'s:**

|---|---|---|

## Economic mechanism behind the cut/increase asymmetry worked out; convex/concave detection curvature solved analytically; a revenue-floor robustness check designed (not launched) (2026-09-13)

**User asked directly: is the "everyone jumps by a constant factor on the increase side, gradual staggering on the cut side" pattern an economic mechanism or just a modeling artifact?** Worked through via the closed form $e'(\Delta)=e_i/(1+\Delta)+\Delta/(2\lambda(1+\Delta))$: the common intercept has no firm-specific terms in it at all, and since $\hat\lambda$ is tiny, $1/(2\lambda)\approx920{,}000$ dwarfs typical $e_i$ (median 370-840), so the intercept swamps heterogeneity on the increase side; on the cut side the same intercept (negative) staggers firms into the zero floor in order of their own $e_i$ (small firms clipped first), which is the "gradual jumping out of evasion" the user described. Concluded: this is a modeling-artifact consequence of linear $q$ + linear $\kappa$ jointly, not a general economic law.

**User self-corrected this to a sharper, verified statement: it's not linearity per se, it's linearity *combined with $\lambda$ being small*.** Rewriting as $e'(\Delta)=[e_i+\Delta/(2\lambda)]/(1+\Delta)$ makes this a horse race between $e_i$ and $\Delta/(2\lambda)$ — for $\lambda$ large enough the heterogeneity term would dominate even under exact linearity. Verified quantitatively: the threshold $\lambda\sim\Delta/(2e_i)$ evaluates to $\sim5\times10^{-6}$ for $\Delta=1\%,e_i=1000$, about 10x above $\hat\lambda=5.427\times10^{-7}$ — confirming the estimated enforcement intensity, not the functional form alone, is what drives the result. Also confirmed the "refund compounding" interpretation: $E[e'(\Delta)]=E[e_i]/(1+\Delta)+\Delta/(2\lambda(1+\Delta))$, and the intercept term is literally the average extra credit claimed/paid out economy-wide.

**Convex and concave detection curvature solved analytically (not estimated) to check whether the uniform-jump result is robust to $q$'s functional form.**
- Convex $q=(\lambda e)^k$: $e=\frac1\lambda\left(\frac{1-C/(\tau\rho_t)}{1+k}\right)^{1/k}$ — nests the linear closed form exactly at $k=1$ (verified). For $k>1$ this is a concave transform of the benefit term, dampening high-$e_i$ firms' response relative to low-$e_i$ firms.
- Concave $q=1-e^{-\lambda e}$: FOC reduces to $(1-\lambda e)e^{-\lambda e}=C/(\tau\rho_t)$, solved via a substitution $u=1-\lambda e$ landing exactly on the Lambert $W$ function's defining equation ($ue^u=z$): $e=[1-W_0(K\exp(1))]/\lambda$, $K=C/(\tau\rho_t)$. Consistency check passed: $W_0(\exp(1))=1$ exactly, matching the already-documented ceiling $\lambda e<1$ for this functional form.
- **Both break the uniform-shift result** — the mapping from $\Delta$ to $e$ is genuinely nonlinear in the firm's own type in both cases, so $\Delta$ and firm heterogeneity stop being additively separable. Conclusion: the uniform-jump property is a knife-edge feature specific to linear-$q$-AND-linear-$\kappa$, not a robust prediction of "simple" functional forms generally.
- **Identification caveat raised and accepted**: the observed right-skew of $e$ doesn't cleanly distinguish convex from concave $q$, since that skew is confounded with the (already right-skewed-generating) distribution of firm type $C(\omega,\psi)$ — matching skewness alone isn't valid identification for $q$'s curvature.

**Cap idea evolved through two iterations, the second one caught by the user as more economically correct.**
1. First proposal (mine): cap the evaded credit's tax value directly at $t1_i$ (can't extract more fraud-credit than output tax paid): $e'_{\text{capped}}=\min(e', t1_i/\tilde\tau_P(\Delta))$.
2. **User caught the flaw**: this still allows $R_i<0$ (a net cash refund) because it doesn't net out the LEGITIMATE credit on true materials $M_i$ first. User's own framing, kept close to verbatim: going from "pay reduced tax" to "the government owes me money" is a much bigger, more suspicious jump than reducing evasion all the way to zero tax paid — the natural constraint is $R_i\ge0$, not a cap on $e'$ in isolation.
3. **Corrected formalization, proven (not assumed) equivalent to a "rational" cap on $e'$ itself**: since $R_i(e')=t1_i-\tilde\tau_P[M_i+(1-q(e'))e']$ is strictly monotonically decreasing in $e'$ over the model's existing domain $e'<1/(2\lambda)$ (because uncaught evasion $e'-\lambda e'^2$ is strictly increasing there — the model's own FOC ceiling already guarantees this domain is never exceeded), $\max(0,R_i(\Delta))$ gives the *exact same number* as would come from a firm rationally capping its own $e'$ at the point where $R_i=0$. This means the robustness check needs **zero changes to the FOC or the closed form for $e'(\Delta)$** — just wrap the existing revenue formula in $\max(0,\cdot)$. Much simpler than the quadratic-inequality cap the first (flawed) proposal would have required.

He proposed a concrete, checkable alternative instead of a philosophical objection: simulate from the joint distribution of $(\varepsilon,\omega)$, compute the GMM objective at a parameter guess using the efficient (inverse-covariance) weight, and use the fact that this object has a $\chi^2$ limiting distribution to test the fit. The user implemented this directly in `Code/Deconvolution/1201-MSM.R`.

**First pass (4 moments, exactly identified): confirmed the textbook Hansen (1982) J-test requires $d_g>d_\theta$, which this design didn't have.** With $g(\theta)=(\psi,\psi\omega,\psi\omega^2,\varepsilon h'_{\text{bounded}})$ against 4 parameters $(\lambda,\delta_0,\delta_1,\delta_2)$, $d_g=d_\theta=4\Rightarrow d_g-d_\theta=0$: an exactly-identified GMM system can (generically) drive its own moments to zero regardless of whether the model is true, so $\chi^2_0$ is a degenerate point mass at zero and no valid test exists — a standard textbook fact (Hansen 1982; Newey & McFadden 1994), not a flaw in Tim's reasoning, which implicitly assumed the usual overidentified setup. Confirmed this numerically by recomputing the objective from the exact same seeded draws: both instruments converged cleanly (NLopt `XTOL_REACHED`) but landed at economically implausible fits (lag_m: $\hat\delta_1=-0.069$, wrong-signed; $\hat\lambda$ 25–170$\times$ ELVIS's own operating range).

**User added a genuine overidentifying moment, $\psi\cdot\ln M$ (one of ELVIS's own moment-set-A rows), making $d_g=5>d_\theta=4$ — reviewed for bugs before rerunning, per the user's request.**

**Bug 1 (pre-existing, not introduced by the user's edit, but load-bearing once a real test exists): the CUE quadratic form was computed in the wrong basis.** The code projected the moment vector and weight matrix onto the retained eigenvectors of $\Omega$ (`d2 <- t(A)%*%dvec`, `Omega_inv <- solve(t(A)%*%Omega%*%A)`) but then paired `Omega_inv` with the raw, un-rotated `dvec` instead of the projected `d2` in the final quadratic form. Dimensionally legal whenever no eigenvalue is trimmed, but mathematically wrong unless $\Omega$ is already diagonal in the original coordinate order. Confirmed numerically on a synthetic 4-variable example: correct $\text{dvec}'\Omega^{-1}\text{dvec}=0.0518$, the code as written returned $0.0439$ (~15% error there); worse in the actual application, where $\Omega$'s eigenvalues span 3+ orders of magnitude (1584.8 to 0.22 in the exactly-identified diagnostic). **Fix: use `d2` in place of `dvec` on the final line.**

**Bug 2 (also pre-existing): the moment covariance pooled all $n_{\text{firms}}\times S$ stacked rows as i.i.d.** Rows sharing a firm share every covariate ($M^*$, $\mathcal V$, $\tilde{\mathcal W}$, $\tau_P$) and differ only in the resampled $\varepsilon_s$ — not independent draws. Pooling them mixes genuine between-firm sampling variance with pure simulation noise from resampling $\varepsilon$; the asymptotically correct object to pair with $n=n_{\text{firms}}$ is the cross-firm covariance of each firm's own $S$-draw average (Pakes & Pollard 1989; McFadden 1989 SMM asymptotics — $S$ integrates out $\varepsilon$ by simulation, it is not additional real sample size). **Fix: `build_moments()` now collapses to one row per firm via `rowsum()` before `Omega`/`dvec` are computed.** Separately confirmed the user's own choice of $n=n_{\text{firms}}$ (not $n_{\text{firms}}\times S$) was already correct — only the accompanying code comment was stale. Also made `df_test` self-computing (`d_g_eff - length(par)`) instead of hardcoded, so it survives future eigenvalue-trimming or moment-count changes.
