**Coverage:** abstract paragraphs 1 and 2 only. The recording ends with Tim saying the introduction comes next; paragraph 3 (1983 reform) was not discussed.

## Paragraph 1

- The first four sentences are very good. They spell out the mechanism in plain language that any economist would follow. He wants the introduction to read the same way.
- The VAT sentence stays as it is. Colombia had separate rates on sales and on purchases, so the net-of-tax version is the special case; Tim agreed.
- "Identifying what from what?": change "identifying" to "detecting". He reads "identifying" as "A from B".
- Use the term "validation sample":
    - It is a shorthand that does not limit the method to one special case.
    - It signals the measurement-error literature, where the contribution sits.
    - It sounds softer than "truth-reporting group", which feels aggressive and hard to get.
    - He called it "not critical": Hans decides.
- Split the "I then use the common technology to predict" sentence in two:
    1. First, use the validation sample to estimate the common technology.
    2. Then use the estimates to predict and compare.
- Replace "truth-reporting group" later in the paragraph by the new term.
- Drop "corporations" in the last sentence. The reader does not know the legal forms, and it invites questions. Say that a subset of firms correctly report inputs.

## Paragraph 2

- Be careful with "detects overreporting". It is common usage, but the test rejects a null.
- Avoid double negatives. Pick one null ("correct reporting" sounds better than "no overreporting") and keep it. Refer back to the null stated in the previous sentence.
- Write "exempt from the sales tax", not "exempt from it".
- The 16 percent sentence is unclear ("median of what?"). The median of averages is not something he wants; he suggests a range, or an unambiguous statement in words.
- Add one sentence in money or revenue terms, so the reader sees why it matters. It can be approximate but not wrong. Hans chooses the framing. (His "five to seven billion dollars against 20 billion" was an illustration of the kind of sentence, not a finding.)
- For the production-function sentence, add a setup sentence saying why people care:
    - Cite two or three of the "golden oldies" (Olley and Pakes, Ackerberg et al.), so even a non-specialist recognizes the names.
    - Then state the finding: these estimates are off when overreporting is ignored.
    - Do not accuse anyone. They did not have this data; the point is that overreporting makes their method give the wrong answer.
