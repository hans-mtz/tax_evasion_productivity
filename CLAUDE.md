## The model

- Firm overreports materials: observed $M^*=M+e$ (additive), or $u=\ln(M^*/M)$ (multiplicative). $e\ge0$ is evasion.
  - **Notation:** this deck uses $u$ throughout for the overreporting log-ratio (Quarto-Slides: `200-model.qmd`, `600-opt-tax.qmd`, `500-opt-tax-claude.qmd`, `700-deconvolving-evasion.qmd`). The approved first-stage paper (`56-id-evasion.qmd`, footnote `linear-exp`) is untouched/read-only and defines its own "$e$" *multiplicatively* ($M^*=M\exp(e)$) — that paper's $e$ **is** this deck's $u$, not the additive structural $e$ used in $q(e)$, $\kappa(e,\omega)$. Translate when citing it. Deconvolution only ever recovers $u=\ln(1+e/M)$, never the level $e$ — a property of the current CD/log-linear identification strategy (under translog the map would need rederiving, though the general principle survives: whatever enters the log residual $\mathcal V=u-\varepsilon$ additively is some transform of $e$ scaled by $M$). See [[reorg-history]] in `Research-log/log.md` for the switch from $\tilde e$.
  - Firm-invariance of $f_u$ is **not a requirement for deconvolution — it's the (separate, testable) claim about what deconvolution outputs.** The convolution formula $f_V=\int f_\varepsilon(V-u)f_u(u)\,du$ only needs $\varepsilon\perp u$ and known $f_\varepsilon$; it always returns *some* population marginal $f_u$ regardless of whether individual firms' distributions actually differ. Firm-invariance is the stronger, separate claim that this recovered marginal is the same object for every firm — motivated by a scale argument (pooling raw $e$ across firms of different size mixes distributions of different scale; the relative measure $u$ is more plausibly size-invariant), not (yet) empirically tested. Don't test it by regressing $\mathcal V_{it}$ on $M^*_{it}$ — that's mechanical, since $\mathcal V_{it}=\ln(\rho_tM^*_{it}/P_tY_{it})-\ln\hat\beta$ has $M^*_{it}$ inside its own formula; a valid diagnostic buckets by a proxy that doesn't enter $\mathcal V$'s definition (e.g. $K_{it}$ or $L_{it}$) and repeats the 1-D deconvolution within each bucket — equivalently, deconvolves $f_{u|Z}$ under the joint condition $\varepsilon\perp(u,Z)$ (continuous $Z$ needs nonparametric deconvolution with a covariate, e.g. Fan–Truong 1993 — much harder, slower rates).
  - $M_{it}\perp e_{it}\mid\omega_{it}$ ($\omega$ exogenous): neither firm's materials FOC nor its evasion FOC contains the other's choice variable. Relevant to a supervisor's general framing: any $h(M,e)$ entering $\mathcal V=h(M,e)-\varepsilon$ is recoverable by deconvolution provided $\varepsilon\perp(e,M)$ and $M\perp e$ — here $h(M,e)=u=\ln(1+e/M)$ is the specific instance.
- Production: $Y=K^{\alpha_K}L^{\alpha_L}M^{\beta}\exp(\omega+\varepsilon)$. $\omega$ = productivity, $\varepsilon$ = ex-post measurement-error shock with $E[\exp\varepsilon]=1$.
- Firm maximizes expected profit with detection probability $q(e)$ (audit + always caught, à la Allingham 1972) and evasion cost $\kappa(e,\omega)$.
- **Evasion FOC:** $\tau\rho_t\big(1-(1+\phi)(q+q'e)\big)=\partial\kappa/\partial e$ (usually $\phi=0$).
- **SOC (cost linear in $e$):** $-q''e/q'<2$.
- $\rho_t$ = materials price (sector–time); $\tau$ = tax rate. $\tau\rho_t$ is the **benefit shifter** — the key source of identification (see Estimation). Under the two-tax-rate model below, this is specifically $\tau_P\rho_t$.

### Detection function $q(e)$ — DECISIONS
- **Linear $q=\min\{\lambda e,1\}$ is the HEADLINE spec, and stays headline after testing the alternative below.** SOC holds unconditionally ($q''=0$); FOC ceiling $\lambda e<\tfrac12$; detection $=50\%$ at the ceiling. Audit-threshold story: $T\sim U[0,1/\lambda]$, audit if $e\ge T$; $1/\lambda$ = max tolerated overreporting, mean threshold $1/2\lambda$.
- **Concave/exponential $q=1-e^{-\lambda e}$** — robustness check, tested and rejected as a fix for the extreme-evader leverage problem (see Estimation → current status): only delays the ceiling by a fixed ~2x factor ($\lambda e<1$ vs. linear's $<\tfrac12$), doesn't come close to matching what trimming the top 1% of firms by $M^*$ buys. Kept as a documented robustness check, not pursued further for that purpose. $T\sim\text{Exp}(\lambda)$, ceiling $\lambda e<1$, $\approx63\%$ at $e=1/\lambda$.
- **Power (convex) $q=(\lambda e)^k$** — nests linear at $k=1$; SOC holds for all $k>0$; ceiling $\lambda e<(1+k)^{-1/k}<1$. **Cannot normalize $\lambda e\in[0,1]$** (violates the FOC ceiling → log of negative). Not tested (would only tighten the ceiling further — the wrong direction for the extreme-evader problem); deprioritized.

### Cost function $\kappa$
$\kappa_{it}=e_{it}\exp\{\delta_0-\delta_1\omega_{it}+\delta_2\omega_{it}^2+\psi_{it}\}$ — linear in $e$, **U-shaped in $\omega$** (min at $\omega^*=\delta_1/2\delta_2$; high cost at both tails → most-productive firms substitute to legal avoidance). $\psi$ = idiosyncratic cost shock. **$f_\psi$ is the central object MSL would recover** (moot for the ELVIS route currently in use, which never recovers $f_\psi$ — see Estimation). $\theta=(\lambda,\delta_0,\delta_1,\delta_2)$, plus $\sigma^2_\psi$ and a relative-evasion floor $\eta$ in the current stage-2 code (see Estimation).

### Corporations — DECISIONS
- **Constrained non-evaders**: $e=0$ is a hard institutional constraint (audited statements, third-party reporting), **outside the FOC**, used **only in the first stage**.
- **No $\delta_C$ shifter** (supervisors rejected giving corps an exogenous cost shifter). **Corner term DROPPED from estimation** — can't classify corner firms exogenously; U-shaped cost handles both tails through the interior channel. Data show near-zero, imprecise evasion at both tails.

## Estimation

**Two stages.**
1. **First stage** (`Paper/sections/56-id-evasion.qmd`, supervisor-approved, read-only). Non-evaders (corps) with $e=0$ give $\mathcal V=-\varepsilon$ → recover $\beta$, $f_\varepsilon$, and by **deconvolution** $f_e$. Constructs the two observed residuals: $\mathcal V_{it}=u_{it}-\varepsilon_{it}$ (`eq-ob-ev`) and $\tilde{\mathcal W}_{it}=\omega_{it}+(1-\beta)\varepsilon_{it}$ (`eq-w-squiggle`).
2. **Second stage**: estimate $\theta$ (and $f_\psi$, under MSL only) from the unincorporated firms plus corp firms as a specification check.

**Key econometric insight:** $e$ and $\varepsilon$ are **one** latent, not two — observed $\mathcal V$ ties them. Integrate out only $\varepsilon$. $f_e$ was already consumed in the first stage; it does NOT reappear in the second-stage likelihood.

### Three candidate estimators for stage 2 — status

**MSL (quadrature-MLE), theoretical backup, not currently implemented.** Likelihood = density of the observables $(\mathcal V,\tilde{\mathcal W})$, a structural MLE in the sense of Hausman (1985), built from the FOC via change of variables:
$$\mathcal L_i^{\text{unc}}=\int f_\psi(\psi(\varepsilon))\,J_i(\varepsilon)\,f_\omega(\omega(\varepsilon))\,f_\varepsilon(\varepsilon)\,d\varepsilon$$
with maps $e(\varepsilon)=M^*(1-e^{-\mathcal V-\varepsilon})$, $\omega(\varepsilon)=\tilde{\mathcal W}-(1-\beta)\varepsilon$, $\psi(\varepsilon)=h(e)-\delta_0+\delta_1\omega-\delta_2\omega^2$, $h(e)\equiv\ln(\tau_P\rho_t)+\ln(1-2\lambda e)$ for linear $q$ (domain $e<1/2\lambda$). Jacobian $J_i(\varepsilon)=|h'(e)|\,M^*$ ($M$, the true unobserved materials level, held fixed as $e$ varies — not $M^*$, which moves with $e$). Correctly conditioning on the firm's own AR(1) persistence requires replacing bare $f_\omega$ with the innovation density $f_\eta$ and marginalizing $\varepsilon_{it-1}$ via the lagged $\tilde{\mathcal W}_{it-1}$ — a 2-D integral, unresolved for a firm's first-observed period (no lag to condition on). Full derivations, and the corrections that got the Jacobian and the $\omega\perp(K,L)$ assumption right, are in `Paper/sections/9999-density-trans.qmd` and `Research-log/log.md`. $\Psi=\psi+\nu$ is **NOT a clean deconvolution** ($\text{Cov}(\psi,\nu)\ne0$ because $e=e^*(\psi,\omega)$ is endogenous). Preferred implementation if pursued: deterministic quadrature (1-D integral) + logspline/sieve for $f_\psi$ (Chen 2007; Kooperberg–Stone 1991; Eilers–Marx 1996), or simulated MLE (Train 2009; consistent under Lee 1992, moot under quadrature). Two-step SEs need the Murphy-Topel (1985) correction or a joint bootstrap.

**MSM — rejected, don't revisit.** The natural-looking alternative (simulating $\psi(\varepsilon^{(s)})$ and averaging, or wrapping that in GMM) does **not** deliver a valid moment: $\psi(\cdot)$ is nonlinear in $\varepsilon$, so the simulated average converges to $\int\psi(\varepsilon;\theta)f_\varepsilon(\varepsilon)d\varepsilon$, not to the firm's true $\psi_{it}$ — missing exactly the posterior reweighting that makes MSL's analogous average valid. See `Research-log/log.md` for the full rejection argument and `600-opt-tax.qmd`'s "why not" section.

**ELVIS — the currently-implemented estimator.** Schennach (2014, *Econometrica*, `@Schennach2014`) solves exactly MSM's failure mode (a moment nonlinear in an unobservable) without the naive-averaging bug. Recast the moment in the **true materials level** $M_{it}$ as the one unobservable: $e(M)=M^*_{it}-M$, $\varepsilon(M)=\ln(M^*_{it}/M)-\mathcal V_{it}$, $\omega(M)=\tilde{\mathcal W}_{it}-(1-\beta)\varepsilon(M)$, $\psi(M)=h(e(M))-\delta_0+\delta_1\omega(M)-\delta_2\omega(M)^2$, support $(M^*_{it}-\tfrac1{2\lambda},M^*_{it})$ truncated at the physical floor $M\ge0$. Her Theorem 2.1: the question "does *some* conditional distribution of $M$ given $Z$ satisfy $E[g(M,Z,\theta)]=0$" is equivalent to a finite-dimensional system in $(\theta,\gamma)$ built from an entropy-maximizing exponential tilt $\exp(\gamma'g)$ of a chosen dominating measure $\rho(M|Z)$ — the tilt **is** the posterior reweighting MSM was missing, solved for as a Lagrange multiplier rather than assumed. Payoffs: doesn't need $f_\psi$ (like MSM), and — by her Remark 2.3 — doesn't need $f_\varepsilon$ either, since the choice of $\rho$ has zero effect on the point estimate (only support matters); only $\hat\beta$ is needed from stage 1. Delivers $\theta=(\lambda,\delta_0,\delta_1,\delta_2,\sigma^2_\psi,\eta)$ only, not $f_\psi$. Needs $\ge4$ instrument-interacted moments for point identification; under-identification degrades gracefully to a set estimate. Identification of $\lambda$ comes from sector–time variation in $\tau_P\rho_t$ interacted into $Z$. Primary sources: `Lit-Papers/ELVIS.pdf`, `Lit-Papers/schennach-2022-measurement-systems.pdf` (her 2022 *JEL* survey), `Lit-Papers/ELVIS_supplement.pdf`, and a second real-world reference implementation, Aguiar & Kashaev 2020 (`Lit-Papers/AK2020.pdf`, RES — advisor Nail Kashaev's own paper, Julia/CUDA code at `TopicsDecisionMaking/ReplicationAK/`). Full rederivation, including the counterfactual-without-$f_\psi$ auxiliary-parameter device, in `Paper/sections/9999-elvis.qmd`.

**Unified single-stage ELVIS — designed, held in reserve, not implemented.** The recast-in-$M$ move generalizes further: $M_{it}$ is the *only* true unobservable in the whole model, for every firm-period (corp and unincorporated alike). Solving the materials FOC and production identity in $M$ directly would let one system estimate $\beta,\alpha_K,\alpha_L,\gamma_0,\gamma_1,\lambda,\delta_0,\delta_1,\delta_2$ jointly from the full panel, with no separate first stage, no $\mathcal V/\tilde{\mathcal W}$, no Jacobian. Corp's "$e\equiv0$" and unincorp's "evade on average" both become moments rather than hard constraints or a separate sample split. Not pursued yet — a bigger sell than any one meeting has time for; sequenced after the stage-2-only version (already implemented) proves out. Full derivation in `Paper/sections/9999-elvis.qmd`, drafted (not rendered) in `600-opt-tax.qmd`.

### ELVIS implementation conventions (evergreen, apply to any moment system built)
- Gamma is **unbounded** in the optimizer — Schennach (ELVIS.pdf p.359) and AK2020 both treat $\gamma\to\pm\infty$ (a degenerate limiting conditional distribution) as an expected solution, not a numerical failure. Report `max|gamma|` per fit to watch it, but don't box-constrain it.
- **Gamma's dimension never counts against the order condition.** It's a Lagrange multiplier, sized automatically to the moment count and solved jointly with the nuisance parameters (not a structural unknown competing for identifying variation). The order-condition comparison is $\dim(\theta_{\text{smooth}}\text{ incl. nuisance})$ vs. $d_g$ (moment count) — gamma never on the parameter side.
- **Sign/scaling of the test statistic:** our `cue_objective_common` follows AK2020's convention (positive, minimized CUE quadratic `Lhat`), not Schennach's own sign (negative, maximized). The correct test statistic is **`TS = 2*n*Lhat`**, compared against $\chi^2_{d_g,\alpha}$ — mirroring AK2020's literal `TSMC=2*minf*n`. Applying the textbook `-2n*Lhat` formula directly to our own `Lhat` gives the wrong (negative) sign. $n$ is the feasibility-filtered sample size; $d_g$ is the actual moment-row count of whichever system produced `Lhat`.
- **Auxiliary-parameter moments (e.g. $E[e]$, counterfactual revenue) must be jointly estimated with their own moment** ($E[U-\tilde\theta]=0$), never read off a post-hoc average over a tilted sample generated at an already-fixed $(\hat\theta,\hat\gamma)$ — Schennach (2022 JEL, p.1250) warns explicitly against interpreting the entropy-maximizing distribution as the actual distribution of the unobservable. The identified set for $\tilde\theta$ is what's reported, not a point average.
- **When more than one parameter is genuinely "of interest" (e.g. the counterfactual's $(\Delta,R)$), grid them jointly as a surface and profile only the true nuisance parameters** — not separate coordinate-wise profiled curves for each. Matches Schennach's own Example 1.5 Continued (Fig. S.3) and AK2020's Appendix F practice.
- Confidence sets: Theorem F.1's conservative $\chi^2$ critical value is the current default (no subsampling); Chernozhukov-Hong-Tamer (2007) subsampling is the upgrade path if a tighter region is ever wanted.
- More moments strengthen an identified set (Schennach: "adding more moment conditions leads to substantial reductions in the uncertainty... requires no extra analytical work") at the cost of solve time, not validity — don't preemptively cut moments to save compute.
- An **"anchor" licenses a cross-moment regardless of the other factor's heterogeneity**: any quantity forced to exact zero mean by its own defining moment (e.g. $\psi$, $\varepsilon_c$, $\omega-\mu_{\omega,j}$) can be multiplied by *any* other quantity, at any power, and the product's expectation is still validly zero — even if that other quantity has substantial cross-group heterogeneity of its own (verified via $E[\text{anchor}\cdot(X-\mu_{X,j(i)})]=E_j[\mathrm{Cov}(\text{anchor},X\mid j)]$, since the anchor's own construction kills the between-group contamination term). This is the rule for deciding whether a candidate moment needs per-industry demeaning of its non-anchor factor, or can stay pooled.
- A quantity known to be tautological given the model's own optimality conditions (e.g. $\psi\cdot e$ — $e$ is literally the evasion FOC's solution given $\psi,\omega$) is not a valid independence moment; only quantities that are genuinely separate primitives given the conditioning variables (e.g. $M$, from the *materials* FOC, vs. $e$/$\psi$ from the *evasion* FOC) license one.
- A moment nonlinear enough in $e$ to blow up near $e$'s FOC ceiling (e.g. $h'(e)=\partial h/\partial\lambda$) needs a bounding transform before entering the moment vector — otherwise a single extreme-$e$ firm can dominate the CUE weighting matrix's eigenvalues and silently collapse the effective moment count. Any function of $e$ alone stays validly independent of $\varepsilon$ under the same argument that licensed the untransformed version (independence never depended on the specific functional form) — but not every bounded transform is equally usable: one that decays *exponentially* in the raw quantity (e.g. $\exp(h')$, or $\tanh$) can numerically saturate to the same machine-precision constant for most of the sample, silently discarding information rather than just controlling magnitude. A transform that decays only *polynomially* (e.g. $h'/(1-h')$, currently used for this exact row) stays distinguishable across a much wider range.

### Current stage-2 status
Two moment systems are implemented and actively compared, both recasting the evasion FOC in $M$ (stage-2-only ELVIS, not the unified single-stage version above): **moment set A** (8 moments, $\theta_{\text{smooth}}=(\delta_0,\delta_1,\delta_2)$, all raw/pooled — `psi`, `eps`, `psi·lnM`, `psi·om`, `psi·om²`, `eps·lnM`, `eps·om`, plus a bounded score moment for $\lambda$, `h'`-transform`·eps`) and **moment set B** (A's rows + per-industry nuisance $\mu_{m,j}$ for latent $\ln M$, externally-computed per-industry $\omega$ targets $\mu_{\omega,j},\sigma_{\omega,j}$, and their cross-moments — $d_g=8+J+7$, $J\approx29$ industries). An earlier 15-moment system (per-industry $\varepsilon$-heterogeneity targets, $\sigma^2_\psi$ as its own parameter) is kept in the code (`mh_tilted_average_cpp` / `1210-stage2-elvis-driver.R`) as a superseded reference point, not deleted. $\theta$ is always gridded/profiled over $\lambda$ (support-changing), with the rest of $\theta_{\text{smooth}}$, $\eta$, per-industry nuisance, and $\gamma$ profiled out via BOBYQA at each grid point (two-pass refinement: run once, re-run warm-started from the result, same unbounded bounds).

**Settled design decisions:**
- Corner firms ($\tau_P=0$, ~10% of unincorp firm-periods, concentrated in genuinely VAT-exempt goods like raw foodstuffs, not misreported zeros): included (`corner_mode="include_zero"`, `"drop"` retired) — $M$ fixed at $M^*$, psi-carrying moment rows set to exactly 0 (uninformative, not "satisfied at 0"), only $\varepsilon$/$\omega$-only rows computed. These firms carry zero identifying information about $\theta$ through the evasion-FOC channel but contribute real information to eps/omega moments.
- A relative-evasion floor $\eta\in[0,1)$ ($M\ge\eta M^*$) is jointly optimized alongside $\delta_0,\delta_1,\delta_2$, as a sampler support restriction (not a gamma-weighted moment) — guards against the CUE objective's eigenvalue-truncated weighting failing to penalize $M\to0$. Estimates come back at $\hat\eta\approx0$ so far — the data doesn't want this floor to bind, but it's a structural guarantee rather than an implicit hope.
- **Trim: top 0.5% of interior (non-corner) firms by $M^*$ dropped before fitting** (revised down from an earlier 1% decision, see below). Linear $q$'s $h(e)$ only meaningfully responds to $\lambda$ through a handful of extreme evaders near the FOC ceiling; without trimming, $\hat L_n(\lambda)$ plateaus flat as $\lambda\to0$ and the estimator cannot statistically rule out arbitrarily weak enforcement (a genuine, diagnosed finding — see `Research-log/log.md`, "Phase 0 finished"). A concave-$q$ robustness check was tested as an alternative fix and rejected (only ~2x relief vs. trimming's ~5-50x). $\hat\theta$ for the counterfactual should be taken from a 0.5%-trimmed moment-set-A fit.
  - **Cutoff criterion, revised (2026-09-07):** after fixing the $h'$ score moment (below), a smaller 5-level re-check (0%, 0.1%, 0.5%, 1%, 5%, both instruments, full 16-point $\lambda$ grid, test-inversion pass/fail at every point — `Code/Deconvolution/1222-stage2-trim-all-gridpoints-plot.R`, `Paper/images/1222-stage2-trim-all-gridpoints-refixed.png`) showed the deciding signal is the 95% CI's **upper bound**, not the point estimate: for `lag_m` ($m^*_{t-1}$) it jumps between 0.1% and 0.5% trim, then stabilizes across 0.5%/1%/5% (with a small further jump at 5%) — 0.5% is the first level where it holds. For `lag_2_cal_W` ($\mathcal W_{t-2}$) the upper bound is stable from 0% through 5% except a one-off widening exactly at 1% (not treated as informative against 0.5%, since 0.5% itself isn't affected). Both instruments are consistent with 0.5% as a safe cutoff; neither points at 1% being necessary. This does not repeat the original 1% decision's own investigation (firm/industry composition checks, etc. — see the entry below and `Research-log/log.md`'s "Trim-cutoff decision" entry) — those still apply, only the specific cutoff value changed.
  - **Not yet done:** a full Phase-0-style fine sweep + closed-form CI *at* the 0.5% cutoff (this revision used a coarse 16-point grid across 5 trim levels, not the dense continuous-chain sweep Phase 0 originally used at 0% trim) — the 0.5% decision itself is solid, but a precise $\hat\lambda$/CI at that cutoff for use in the counterfactual is still open.
- $\lambda$ grid construction targets $q@\text{median}=\lambda\cdot\text{median}(M^*)$ directly (from ~0.1% up to the 50% ceiling), not $M^*$-quantile probabilities — the latter compresses unevenly and isn't the economically meaningful axis (any $\lambda$ large enough that the median firm's hypothetical-full-evasion $q\ge0.5$ is uninformative, since such firms are already clipped at the ceiling).
- **Warm-starting must chain continuously across nearby $\lambda$/trim configurations, not restart independently at each one** — independent restarts produced 2-4 orders of magnitude of spurious noise between adjacent configurations in more than one investigation this project has run into. `1211-stage2-elvis-driver-AB.R`'s `par_init` CLI override + `FINAL_PAR:` stdout line, and `Code/Deconvolution/run-trim-sequential.sh`, implement this chaining.

**$h'$ score-moment numerical fix, done in two rounds, both 2026-09-07.** The $h'$-transform score moment for $\lambda$ (moment set A row, and B) computes $h'=\partial h/\partial\lambda$ from a **floored** denominator (`H_DENOM_FLOOR=1e-6`, shared between `h_of_e` and `h_prime_of_e` in `Code/Rcpp/1200-stage2-elvis-common.h` — both must agree, or `h_prime` reports a nonzero slope where the floor has made `h` locally flat) — raw, unfloored $h'$ diverges near the FOC ceiling (the sampler's own support puts positive density arbitrarily close to it, `u01~Uniform[0,1)` never reaching the edge exactly) and was found to silently collapse the CUE objective's effective moment count via eigenvalue truncation. **Round 1's transform, $\exp(h')$, is superseded — do not use it.** It bounds to $(0,1]$ but decays *exponentially* in $|h'|$, which underflows to exactly `0.0` for roughly 73-75% of firms in this data (median $e\approx841\Rightarrow h'\approx-1683$, deep in `exp`'s underflow zone) — silently uninformative for most of the sample, not a numerical nicety. **Current transform: `h_prime_bounded(e,\lambda) = h'/(1-h')`** — still bounded ($(-1,0]$), still licensed by the same "any function of $e$ alone stays valid" argument, but decays only *polynomially*, so firms stay numerically distinguishable across the full range of $e$ (checked directly: $e=59{,}171$ and $e=236{,}714$ give $-0.999992$ vs. $-0.999998$ — genuinely distinct in double precision, unlike either $\exp$ or $\tanh$, which both saturate to a shared machine-precision constant well before $|h'|$ reaches values this data routinely produces). z-score normalization was also prototyped (in the R warm-start, cheaper to iterate) and rejected — it introduced a rough, sample-mean/sd-dependent objective that made even that simpler search fail outright for one instrument. Live in the shared header, used by moment sets A and B. See `Research-log/log.md`'s "$h'$ numerical fix" and "standalone grid estimator" entries for the full diagnosis.

**Two-tax-rate model ($\tau_S\ne\tau_P$).** The evasion FOC only ever contains $\tau_P$ (purchases-side rate), never $\tau_S$ (sales-side) — `sales_tax_rate_purchases` is the theoretically correct $h(e)$ input, already wired into the stage-2 driver. $\partial e^*/\partial\tau_P>0$ (higher $\tau_P$ increases evasion — confirmed via closed form and the implicit function theorem; this sign **cannot** be checked by regressing $\mathcal V_{it}$ on $\tau_{P,it}$, since both are built from the same $M^*_{it}$ in opposite positions, a mechanical negative link regardless of the true economics — resolve this only via the 1983 fiscal-reform diff-in-diff or the structural estimation itself, not more bivariate regressions). The materials FOC picks up a multiplicative $(1-\tau_S)/(1-\tau_P)$ wedge, algebraically equivalent to defining $\varepsilon_{it}(M_{it})$ via net-of-tax nominal ratios rather than gross — both legs still directly-observed nominal quantities, so no price index is needed for this side of the model regardless. Full derivation in `Paper/sections/9999-tax-wedge.qmd`. **Status: derived, nests the single-$\tau$ model exactly at $\tau_S=\tau_P$, but not applied to stage 1** — `first_stage_panel`/`first_stage_panel_me` would need `var="log_mats_share_net"` instead of `"log_mats_share"`, which reopens the supervisor-approved stage 1; deliberately deferred pending a supervisor conversation.

**Nominal log-share as the FOC-side observable.** $\varepsilon_{it}(M_{it})=\ln\beta-\ln(\rho_tM_{it}/P_tY_{it})$ only ever uses the *ratio* of nominal materials expenditure to nominal sales — no price index or deflation needed for the evasion-FOC side of the model at all. The production-identity/AR(1) side is different and unavoidably exposed: $\omega_{it}(M_{it})$ needs $\ln M_{it}$ and $y_{it}$ individually (to combine with $K,L$ under Cobb-Douglas), so whatever price-index quality issue the dataset has enters there, not on the FOC side. This asymmetry is documented, not modeled — no separate deflation-error term is added; it's a known, out-of-scope limitation on the production/AR(1) side only.

**Identification of $\lambda$:** from the response of evasion to $\tau_P\rho_t$ across sector–time cells (within a cell, $\tau_P\rho_t$ is absorbed into $f_\psi$'s/the moments' location). **Location normalization:** $\operatorname{median}(\psi)=0$ (or $E[\psi]=0$); $\delta_0$ absorbs the level (no corner mass to anchor it).

References added to `Quarto-Slides/biblio/b100424.bib` (Hausman 1985, Train 2009, etc.) and `biblio/b100422.bib` (Schennach 2014, AK2020).

## Counterfactual

Optimal sales-tax rate: revenue rises mechanically with the rate but higher rates raise the evasion incentive (Laffer trade-off). Also framed as "the tax rate giving median evasion = 0."

**Each $(\Delta,R)$ grid cell requires estimating (profiling) the nuisance parameters and $\gamma$ jointly — this is NOT a forward simulation off a fixed $\hat\theta$.** Same nested-optimization structure as the $(\lambda,\delta_1,\delta_2)$ grid: at every cell, $\lambda,\delta_0,\delta_1,\delta_2,\sigma^2_\psi,\eta$, per-industry nuisance, and $\gamma$ all get (re-)estimated via NLopt, not plugged in from a single upstream fit.

**Per-firm type must NOT be "recovered and held fixed" from a post-hoc tilted MCMC draw either** (Nail Kashaev correction, 2026-09-04, full citation trail in `Research-log/log.md`): Schennach 2022 JEL p.1250 warns explicitly against interpreting the entropy-maximizing/tilted distribution as the actual distribution of the unobservable — an earlier draft of this section did exactly that, "recover the implied type $(\omega,\psi)$, hold it fixed." Corrected: the counterfactual objects below are deterministic functions of the *same* latent $M_i$ the main ELVIS system already carries, never a separately "recovered" per-firm point; any population quantity built from them ($E[R]$, the Laffer curve) comes from the auxiliary-parameter joint-moment device in the Inference plan below, never from averaging over draws from a $(\hat\theta,\hat\gamma)$-tilted chain after the fact.

Closed form under linear $q$, a deterministic function of $M_i$ evaluated at candidate $\tilde\tau_P$:
$$e'(\tilde\tau_P;M_i)=\max\!\left(0,\ \frac{\tilde\tau_P\rho_t-C(\omega(M_i),\psi(M_i))}{2\lambda\tilde\tau_P\rho_t}\right),\qquad C(\omega,\psi)\equiv\textbf{e}^{\delta_0-\delta_1\omega+\delta_2\omega^2+\psi}$$
**The $\max(0,\cdot)$ clip is required, not optional** — some interior (currently-evading) firms will have their true optimum become a corner solution ($e^*=0$) as $\Delta$ moves the benefit shifter, and this genuinely will bind for some firms, not just the institutionally-corner ($\tau_P=0$) ones already handled specially in estimation. The revenue formula below is already well-defined and continuous there with no extra special-casing needed: at $e=0$ the evasion term simply vanishes ($R_i(\Delta)=t1_i-\tilde\tau_{P,i}M_i$) — unlike the *estimation*-side moments, where $e=0$ makes the FOC-derived $\psi$ meaningless (not just zero, forced to 0 by convention), revenue itself is a real, always-defined outcome at any $e\ge0$, corner or interior.

**Parametrization:** $\Delta\in[-0.5,1]$ (50% cut to 100% increase) on the purchases-side rate, benefit shifter $\ln((1+\Delta)\rho_t\tau_P)$ replacing $\ln(\tau_P\rho_t)$ in $h(e)$ everywhere in the FOC-resolve step.

**Revenue formula (two-tax-rate model — $\tau_S$ enters and must be netted against $\tau_P$, even though only $\tau_P$ is shocked):** VAT revenue is sales-side tax collected minus the purchases-side credit claimed; only the credit side is exposed to evasion:
$$R_i(\Delta;M_i)=\underbrace{t1_{it}}_{\tau_{S,it}\cdot P_tY_{it},\ \text{fixed}}-\underbrace{\tilde\tau_{P,it}\Big[M_i+\big(1-q(\tilde e_i(\Delta))\big)\tilde e_i(\Delta)\Big]}_{\text{shocked by }\Delta},\qquad \tilde\tau_{P,it}\equiv(1+\Delta)\tau_{P,it}$$
$t1$ is the raw "sales tax paid on sales" column, used directly since $\tau_S$ never changes in this exercise. Corner firms ($\tau_P=0$): $R_i=t1_{it}-\tilde\tau_{P,it}M^*_{it}$, no detection term (nothing to catch). K/L don't enter (VAT-exempt). Units are nominal COP throughout — never substitute the real, log-scale `m`/`y` columns. Deflate once at aggregation ($R_i^{\text{real}}=R_i/P_t$, `p_gdp_new`), not mid-formula, since the panel spans a decade of inflation.

**Inference plan (AK2020-style test inversion, §5.5 and their Appendix F code, `Lit-Papers/ReplicationAK2020/Appendix_F/`):** grid $(\Delta,R)$ jointly — $R$ as its own ELVIS auxiliary parameter with its own moment $E[R_i(\Delta;M_i)-R]=0$ — and at each cell, profile out everything else ($\lambda,\delta_0,\delta_1,\delta_2,\sigma^2_\psi,\eta$, per-industry nuisance, $\gamma$). Confidence set: $\{(\Delta,R):TS(\Delta,R)<\chi^2_{d_g,0.95}\}$ where $TS=2n\hat L_n$ per the sign convention above, computed as the literal min/max of *tested* grid points passing the test (matches AK2020's `F_figure1.R`). A genuine interval-valued Laffer-curve conclusion (rather than a point $\tau^*$) is an acceptable, arguably more honest deliverable — set-identification is not a fallback to avoid.

**Phasing:**
- **Phase 0 (done for the untrimmed sample, not yet redone at the 1% trim decision or with the corrected $h'$ moment):** finish the $\lambda$-only confidence set via test inversion — grid $\lambda$, profile the rest, $TS(\lambda)=2n[\hat L_n(\lambda)-\min_{\lambda'}\hat L_n(\lambda')]$ vs. $\chi^2_{8,0.95}$. On the untrimmed sample this resolved a real upper bound ($\hat\lambda\approx1.8\times10^{-8}$, 95% CI upper $\sim2$–$3.4\times10^{-8}$) but not a lower one (flat plateau down to $10^{-12}$) — since attributed to extreme-evader leverage and addressed by the 1% trim decision above. **Next step:** rerun this Phase-0 sweep at 1% trim with the corrected (bounded) $h'$ moment before trusting $\hat\theta$ as an input to Phase 1.
- **Phase 1 (not started):** fix $\hat\theta$ from the refined Phase-0 fit, run the $(\Delta,R)$ joint grid above. Follow-up once that lands: profile $\theta$ jointly with $\gamma$ at every $(\Delta,R)$ cell instead of fixing it — a special case of the "grid parameters of interest jointly, profile true nuisance" rule above, applied to a larger parameter-of-interest set.

Per-firm type draws $(e_i,\omega_i,\psi_i)$ for the counterfactual only need to be drawn once after $\hat\theta$ is fixed (no re-tilting across candidate $\Delta$, since $\Delta$ only enters the closed-form FOC resolve, never the $M$-sampler) — save the realized draws directly rather than re-deriving from a seed each time.
