Job-market paper (economics PhD). Structural model of **tax evasion via overreporting of intermediate inputs**, and its interaction with firm productivity. The user is sharp on econometrics; engage at that level.

## The model

- Firm overreports materials: observed $M^*=M+e$ (additive), or $u=\ln(M^*/M)$ (multiplicative). $e\ge0$ is evasion.
  - **Notation trap:** the approved first-stage paper (`56-id-evasion.qmd`, footnote `linear-exp`) defines its own "$e$" *multiplicatively* ($M^*=M\exp(e)$) — that paper's $e$ **is** this deck's $u$, not the additive structural $e$ used in $q(e)$, $\kappa(e,\omega)$. Deconvolution only ever recovers $u=\ln(1+e/M)$, never the level $e$ — this is a property of the **current CD/log-linear identification strategy**, not a universal law: under translog the specific map would need rederiving, but the general principle survives (whatever enters the log residual $\mathcal V=u-\varepsilon$ additively is some transform of $e$ scaled by $M$). Previously kept $\tilde e$ for continuity with the paper; **as of 2026-07-28, switched notation to $u$ throughout the Quarto-Slides deck** so readers don't mistake the overreporting log-ratio for the structural level $e$ — the approved paper (`56-id-evasion.qmd`) is untouched/read-only and still uses its own $e$ for this object, so translate when citing it.
  - Firm-invariance of $f_u$ is **not a requirement for deconvolution — it's the (separate, testable) claim about what deconvolution outputs.** The convolution formula $f_V=\int f_\varepsilon(V-u)f_u(u)\,du$ only needs $\varepsilon\perp u$ and known $f_\varepsilon$; it always returns *some* population marginal $f_u$ regardless of whether individual firms' distributions actually differ. Firm-invariance is the stronger, separate claim that this recovered marginal is the same object for every firm — motivated by a scale argument (pooling raw $e$ across firms of different size mixes distributions of different scale; the relative measure $u$ is more plausibly size-invariant), not (yet) empirically tested. Don't test it by regressing $\mathcal V_{it}$ on $M^*_{it}$ — that's mechanical, since $\mathcal V_{it}=\ln(\rho_tM^*_{it}/P_tY_{it})-\ln\hat\beta$ has $M^*_{it}$ inside its own formula; a valid diagnostic buckets by a proxy that doesn't enter $\mathcal V$'s definition (e.g. $K_{it}$ or $L_{it}$) and repeats the 1-D deconvolution within each bucket — equivalently, deconvolves $f_{u|Z}$ under the joint condition $\varepsilon\perp(u,Z)$ (continuous $Z$ needs nonparametric deconvolution with a covariate, e.g. Fan–Truong 1993 — much harder, slower rates).
  - **Notation resolved (2026-07-28):** all four Quarto-Slides files that used this symbol (`200-model.qmd`, `600-opt-tax.qmd`, `500-opt-tax-claude.qmd`, `700-deconvolving-evasion.qmd`) now consistently use $u$ for the overreporting log-ratio — previously $\tilde e$, and previously inconsistently mixed with $u$ inside `700-deconvolving-evasion.qmd` specifically. The approved first-stage paper (`56-id-evasion.qmd`) is untouched and still uses its own $e$ (see notation trap above).
  - $M_{it}\perp e_{it}\mid\omega_{it}$ ($\omega$ exogenous): neither firm's materials FOC nor its evasion FOC contains the other's choice variable. Relevant to a supervisor's general framing (2026-07 meeting): any $h(M,e)$ entering $\mathcal V=h(M,e)-\varepsilon$ is recoverable by deconvolution provided $\varepsilon\perp(e,M)$ and $M\perp e$ — here $h(M,e)=u=\ln(1+e/M)$ is the specific instance.
- Production: $Y=K^{\alpha_K}L^{\alpha_L}M^{\beta}\exp(\omega+\varepsilon)$. $\omega$ = productivity, $\varepsilon$ = ex-post measurement-error shock with $E[\exp\varepsilon]=1$.
- Firm maximizes expected profit with detection probability $q(e)$ (audit + always caught, à la Allingham 1972) and evasion cost $\kappa(e,\omega)$.
- **Evasion FOC:** $\tau\rho_t\big(1-(1+\phi)(q+q'e)\big)=\partial\kappa/\partial e$ (usually $\phi=0$).
- **SOC (cost linear in $e$):** $-q''e/q'<2$.
- $\rho_t$ = materials price (sector–time); $\tau$ = tax rate. $\tau\rho_t$ is the **benefit shifter** — the key source of identification (see below).

### Detection function $q(e)$ — DECISIONS
- **Linear $q=\min\{\lambda e,1\}$ is the HEADLINE spec.** SOC holds unconditionally ($q''=0$); FOC ceiling $\lambda e<\tfrac12$; detection $=50\%$ at the ceiling. Audit-threshold story: $T\sim U[0,1/\lambda]$, audit if $e\ge T$; $1/\lambda$ = max tolerated overreporting, mean threshold $1/2\lambda$.
- **Concave/exponential $q=1-e^{-\lambda e}$** — demoted to robustness. $T\sim\text{Exp}(\lambda)$, ceiling $\lambda e<1$, $\approx63\%$ at $e=1/\lambda$.
- **Power (convex) $q=(\lambda e)^k$** — nests linear at $k=1$; SOC holds for all $k>0$; ceiling $\lambda e<(1+k)^{-1/k}<1$. **Cannot normalize $\lambda e\in[0,1]$** (violates the FOC ceiling → log of negative). Plan: keep $\lambda$ free, impose feasibility for every firm, **estimate and test $k=1$**; use as a curvature robustness check.

### Cost function $\kappa$
$\kappa_{it}=e_{it}\exp\{\delta_0-\delta_1\omega_{it}+\delta_2\omega_{it}^2+\psi_{it}\}$ — linear in $e$, **U-shaped in $\omega$** (min at $\omega^*=\delta_1/2\delta_2$; high cost at both tails → most-productive firms substitute to legal avoidance). $\psi$ = idiosyncratic cost shock. **$f_\psi$ is the central object to recover.** $\theta=(\lambda,\delta_0,\delta_1,\delta_2)$.

### Corporations — DECISIONS
- **Constrained non-evaders**: $e=0$ is a hard institutional constraint (audited statements, third-party reporting), **outside the FOC**, used **only in the first stage**.
- **No $\delta_C$ shifter** (supervisors rejected giving corps an exogenous cost shifter). **Corner term DROPPED from estimation** — can't classify corner firms exogenously; U-shaped cost handles both tails through the interior channel. Data show near-zero, imprecise evasion at both tails.

## Estimation

**Two stages.**
1. **First stage** (`Paper/sections/56-id-evasion.qmd`, supervisor-approved). Non-evaders (corps) with $e=0$ give $\mathcal V=-\varepsilon$ → recover $\beta$, $f_\varepsilon$, and by **deconvolution** $f_e$. Constructs the two observed residuals: $\mathcal V_{it}=u_{it}-\varepsilon_{it}$ (`eq-ob-ev`) and $\tilde{\mathcal W}_{it}=\omega_{it}+(1-\beta)\varepsilon_{it}$ (`eq-w-squiggle`).
2. **Second stage**: estimate $\theta$ and $f_\psi$.

**Key econometric insight:** $e$ and $\varepsilon$ are **one** latent, not two — observed $\mathcal V$ ties them. Integrate out only $\varepsilon$. $f_e$ was already consumed in the first stage; it does NOT reappear in the second-stage likelihood.

**Likelihood = density of the OBSERVABLES $(\mathcal V,\tilde{\mathcal W})$**, not of $(e,\omega)$. This is a **structural MLE in the sense of Hausman (1985)** — built from the FOC via change of variables, integrating out heterogeneity.
$$\mathcal L_i^{\text{unc}}=\int f_\psi(\psi(\varepsilon))\,J_i(\varepsilon)\,f_\omega(\omega(\varepsilon))\,f_\varepsilon(\varepsilon)\,d\varepsilon$$
with maps $e(\varepsilon)=M^*(1-e^{-\mathcal V-\varepsilon})$, $\omega(\varepsilon)=\tilde{\mathcal W}-(1-\beta)\varepsilon$, $\psi(\varepsilon)=h(e)-\delta_0+\delta_1\omega-\delta_2\omega^2$, where $h(e)\equiv\ln(\tau\rho_t)+\ln(1-2\lambda e)$ for linear $q$ (domain $e<1/2\lambda$).
- **Jacobian $J_i(\varepsilon)=|h'(e)|\,M^*$** stacks TWO changes of variables: $|h'(e)|$ for $\psi\to e$, and $M^*$ for $e\to\mathcal V$ (via $u=\ln\frac{M+e}{M}$, $M$ the true unobserved materials level held fixed as $e$ varies — not $M^*=M+e$, which moves whenever $e$ does; $M+e^*=M^*$ at the optimum, so the derivative lands on the observed total). It depends on $\lambda$, so dropping it makes the MLE inconsistent. Derivation: one CoV $(\psi,\omega,\varepsilon)\to(\mathcal V,\tilde{\mathcal W},\varepsilon)$ is block-triangular → single determinant $G_\psi$; $\omega$'s unit-slope translation carries NO Jacobian.
  - **Correction (2026-07-30):** earlier version of this line read $J_i(\varepsilon)=|h'(e)|(M^*-e)$, from differentiating $u=\ln(M^*/(M^*-e))$ holding $M^*$ fixed. That's a partial/total-derivative error — $M^*=M+e$ is not fixed as $e$ varies (it's the thing that moves), so that derivative silently assumed $\partial M^*/\partial e=0$. $M$ (from the production-side materials FOC, independent of $e$) is the quantity actually fixed here. The value of $u_{it}$ itself is unaffected (both formulas give $\ln(M^*_{it}/M_{it})$); only the Jacobian's functional form changes, by a factor of $M^*/M$. See `Paper/sections/9999-density-trans.qmd` for the full worked correction.
  - **Correction (2026-08-12):** the bare $f_\omega(\omega(\varepsilon))$ above silently assumed $\omega\perp(K,L)$ — untenable, since $\omega$ is AR(1)-persistent and $K$ is a dynamically accumulated state chosen anticipating that persistence; asserting independence would strip the AR(1) moment conditions (`eq-eta-moments` in `56-id-evasion.qmd`) of the instrument relevance that identifies $\alpha_K,\alpha_L$ in the first place. Fix: Markov timing ($K,L$ predetermined; the AR(1) innovation $\eta_{it}$ is news independent of everything dated $t-1$) gives $\omega_{it}\perp(K_{it},L_{it})\mid\omega_{it-1}$ — $\omega_{it-1}$ is a sufficient statistic for $(K,L)$'s relevance, so the likelihood should condition on the firm's own lagged $\tilde{\mathcal W}_{it-1}$ instead of $(K,L)$, replacing bare $f_\omega$ with the AR(1) innovation density $f_\eta$ and marginalizing the one remaining unknown $\varepsilon_{it-1}$ (via $\omega_{it-1}=\tilde{\mathcal W}_{it-1}-(1-\beta)\varepsilon_{it-1}$) — a 2-D integral over $(\varepsilon_{it},\varepsilon_{it-1})$, collapsing back to the static $\mathcal L_i^{\text{unc}}$ above exactly when the AR(1) has no persistence. $f_\eta$ itself is recoverable by Fourier deconvolution from `56-id-evasion.qmd`'s AR(1) regression residual (`eq-big-w-reg`), reusing stage-1's $f_\varepsilon$. **Notation clash:** `56-id-evasion.qmd`'s AR(1) drift is also called $h$ (linear case $\delta_0+\delta_1\xi$) — collides with both this doc's $h(e)$ and $\delta_0,\delta_1,\delta_2$; renamed locally to $g(\xi)=\gamma_0+\gamma_1\xi$ wherever the two are combined. Full derivation, and the correction to an earlier draft claim that bare @eq-Lunc already sufficed, in `Paper/sections/9999-density-trans.qmd`. **Open, unresolved:** a firm's first-observed period has no $\tilde{\mathcal W}_{it-1}$ to condition on (dynamic-panel initial-conditions problem).
- $\Psi=\psi+\nu$ is **NOT a clean deconvolution** ($\text{Cov}(\psi,\nu)\ne0$ because $e=e^*(\psi,\omega)$ is endogenous; kernel is nonlinear/firm-specific).
- **Identification of $\lambda$/$k$:** from the response of evasion to $\tau\rho_t$ **across sector–time cells** (within a cell $\rho_t$ is absorbed into $f_\psi$'s location). $f_\psi$ absorbs only cost heterogeneity orthogonal to that benefit. Curvature $k$ (power) is **fragile** when $\tau\rho_t$ varies little → another reason to headline linear.
- **Location normalization:** $\operatorname{median}(\psi)=0$ (or $E[\psi]=0$); $\delta_0$ absorbs the level (no corner mass to anchor it).

**Preferred implementation:** deterministic **quadrature** (integral is only 1-D) + **logspline/sieve for $f_\psi$** (Chen 2007; Kooperberg–Stone 1991; Eilers–Marx P-splines 1996). Alternative: simulated MLE (Train 2009; Gourieroux–Monfort 1996), consistent under @Lee1992 (unbiased, $\theta$-continuous simulator + $\sqrt N/S\to0$ — moot under quadrature, since there's no simulation noise to begin with). Two-step SEs (since $\beta,f_\varepsilon$ are first-stage estimates) need the @MurphyTopel1985 correction or a joint bootstrap. Refs added to `Quarto-Slides/biblio/b100424.bib` (also Hausman 1985).

**MSM — investigated and rejected (2026-07-28):** the natural-looking alternative, simulating $\psi(\varepsilon^{(s)})$ (or $M^{(s)}=\beta P_tY_{it}e^{-\varepsilon^{(s)}}/\rho_t$, $e^{(s)}=M^*-M^{(s)}$ — algebraically **identical** to the $\mathcal V$-based construction, since $\textbf{e}^{-\mathcal V_{it}}=\beta P_tY_{it}/(\rho_tM^*_{it})$ makes them the same map) and averaging, or wrapping that average in classic GMM with instruments, does **not** deliver a valid moment: $\psi(\cdot)$ is nonlinear in $\varepsilon$, so the simulated average converges to $\int\psi(\varepsilon;\theta)f_\varepsilon(\varepsilon)d\varepsilon$, not to the firm's true $\psi_{it}=\psi(\varepsilon_{it};\theta)$ — it's missing exactly the posterior reweighting $f_\psi(\psi(\varepsilon))J_i(\varepsilon)f_\omega(\omega(\varepsilon))$ that makes MSL's analogous simulated average valid (MSL never averages $\psi(\varepsilon^{(s)})$ itself, only the reweighted density). GMM's instruments/weighting-matrix machinery only combines already-valid moments efficiently; it cannot fix an invalid one. A valid simulated-moments design would instead match a data-computable, $\psi$-free population object (e.g. $E[\mathcal V_{it}|Z]=E[u_{it}|Z]$) against a forward-simulated analog from trial $(\psi,\omega)$ draws — ties to stage 1's deconvolved $f_u$ — but delivers $\theta$ not $f_\psi$ and adds machinery for no real gain over quadrature-MLE. See `600-opt-tax.qmd` for the full explainer (kept as a "why not" section, not the headline).

**ELVIS — investigated and adopted as a genuine third alternative (2026-08-19):** Schennach (2014, *Econometrica*, "Entropic Latent Variable Integration via Simulation," `@Schennach2014` in `biblio/b100422.bib`) solves exactly the failure mode identified in the MSM rejection above — a moment function nonlinear in an unobservable — without the naive-averaging bug. Recast the moment in the **true materials level** $M_{it}$ as the one unobservable (mirroring Schennach's own canonical errors-in-variables example, her Example 1.5, unobserved $X^*$ vs. observed $X=X^*+\Delta X$): $e(M)=M^*_{it}-M$, $\varepsilon(M)=\ln(M^*_{it}/M)-\mathcal V_{it}$, $\omega(M)=\tilde{\mathcal W}_{it}-(1-\beta)\varepsilon(M)$, $g(M,Z_{it},\theta)\equiv\psi(M)=h(e(M))-\delta_0+\delta_1\omega(M)-\delta_2\omega(M)^2$, support $\mathcal U_{Z,\theta}=(M^*_{it}-\tfrac1{2\lambda},M^*_{it})$ ($\theta$-dependent support is explicitly covered, her §4.1). Her Theorem 2.1: the infinite-dimensional question "does *some* conditional distribution of $M$ given $Z$ satisfy $E[g(M,Z,\theta)]=0$" is **equivalent** to a finite-dimensional system in $(\theta,\gamma)$ built from an entropy-maximizing **exponential tilt** $\exp(\gamma'g(M,Z,\theta))$ of a user-chosen dominating measure $\rho(M|Z)$ — solved via simulated draws $M^{(s)}\sim\rho(\cdot|Z)$ and a ratio-of-averages $\tilde g(Z,\theta,\gamma)=E_\rho[g\cdot e^{\gamma'g}]/E_\rho[e^{\gamma'g}]$, fed into standard GMM/EL over $(\theta,\gamma)$ jointly. **This tilt is precisely the missing posterior reweighting** that the rejected naive-MSM average lacked — not assumed, but solved for as a Lagrange multiplier on the moment constraint (her §2.2 derivation). Two practical payoffs: (i) doesn't need $f_\psi$ (same limit as MSM, expected), but (ii) **doesn't need $f_\varepsilon$ either** — by her Remark 2.3, the choice of $\rho$ has *zero effect* on the point estimate (only support matters), so stage 1's deconvolution of $f_\varepsilon$/$f_u$ becomes optional for stage 2 estimation of $\theta$ (only $\hat\beta$ is needed); $\hat f_\varepsilon$ can still be reused as an efficient $\rho$ to cut simulation variance, but that's a computational choice, not an identification requirement. Delivers $\theta=(\lambda,\delta_0,\delta_1,\delta_2)$ only, not $f_\psi$ — same trade-off as MSM, just valid. Needs $\ge4$ instrument-interacted moments for point identification (order condition); under-identification degrades gracefully to a set estimate (her method covers both transparently). Identification of $\lambda$ still comes from sector–time variation in $\tau\rho_t$ interacted into $Z$, same source as the MLE. See `Lit-Papers/ELVIS.pdf` (primary source) and `Lit-Papers/schennach-2022-measurement-systems.pdf` (her 2022 *JEL* survey, §3.6.3, situates ELVIS among bounding/set-identification techniques) for the full derivation.

**Unified single-stage ELVIS — explored, held in reserve (2026-08-19):** the recast-in-$M$ move above generalizes further — **there is only one true unobservable in the whole model: $M_{it}$**, for every firm-period, corp and unincorporated alike. Once $M_{it}$ is the ELVIS latent, the materials FOC and production identity solve in closed form with no $\mathcal V/\tilde{\mathcal W}$, no Jacobian, no density-transformation theorem needed at all:
$$\varepsilon_{it}(M_{it})=\ln\beta-\ln\!\left(\frac{\rho_tM_{it}}{P_tY_{it}}\right),\qquad \omega_{it}(M_{it})=(1-\beta)\ln M_{it}-\alpha_Kk_{it}-\alpha_Ll_{it}-\ln\beta+\ln\rho_t-\ln P_t$$
- **$y_{it}$ cancels out of $\omega_{it}(M_{it})$ — verified, not a bug.** This is Gandhi–Navarro–Rivers's own $\Phi_{it}=y_{it}-\hat\varepsilon_{it}$ step, specialized to constant (Cobb-Douglas) elasticity — their $\Phi$ loses $y_{it}$ the same way in this special case. Standard proxy-variable logic (Olley-Pakes/Levinsohn-Petrin/ACF/GNR): under the timing assumption, optimal $m$ is monotonic in $\omega$ *alone*, so $m$ already perfectly proxies $\omega$; $y_{it}$'s only job is revealing $\varepsilon_{it}$ via the share equation, used exactly once. Not double-counting. Before committing: still verify the stacked Jacobian $\partial E[g]/\partial\theta_{\text{full}}$ has full column rank numerically — algebra confirms the construction is sound, not that every parameter is well-identified in finite samples.
- **AR(1) moment, corrected and fully reduced:** raw form is $y_{it}-\beta\ln M_{it}-\alpha_Kk_{it}-\alpha_Ll_{it}-\gamma_0-\gamma_1(y_{it-1}-\beta\ln M_{it-1}-\alpha_Kk_{it-1}-\alpha_Ll_{it-1})=\eta_{it}+\varepsilon_{it}-\gamma_1\varepsilon_{it-1}$ (coefficient $+1$ on $\varepsilon_{it}$, $-\gamma_1$ on $\varepsilon_{it-1}$ — not "$\gamma_1\varepsilon$"); since $\varepsilon$ is itself a function of $M$, this collapses to $\eta_{it}(M_{it},M_{it-1})=\omega_{it}(M_{it})-\gamma_0-\gamma_1\omega_{it-1}(M_{it-1})$, no $\varepsilon$ left. Only needs $E[\eta_{it}Z_{it}]=0$ (moment orthogonality) — unlike MSL, never needs the AR(1) innovation *density* $f_\eta$, sidestepping exactly the complication the 2026-08-12 correction introduced.
- **Corp/unincorp evasion, relaxed from hard constraints to moments:** corp's "$e\equiv0$" hard constraint (previously used only to define the first-stage sample) becomes a centering moment, $E[e_{it}(M_{it})\mid\text{Corp}_i]=0$ or $\text{Med}[e_{it}(M_{it})\mid\text{Corp}_i]=0$ — corp can evade a little, centered at zero, matching the data (near-zero, imprecise evasion at both tails). Unincorporated firms' "evade on average" becomes an inequality moment via ELVIS's own auxiliary-variable device (her Example 1.3): $E[e_{it}(M_{it})\cdot\mathbb 1(\text{Unincorp}_i)-U_2]=0$, $U_2\ge0$.
- **All parameters jointly:** $\beta,\alpha_K,\alpha_L,\gamma_0,\gamma_1,\lambda,\delta_0,\delta_1,\delta_2$ estimated in one system from the full panel (corp + unincorporated together) — no data thrown away building a separate first stage.
- **Counterfactual under ELVIS:** MSL's revenue integral weights by $f_\psi\cdot J_i\cdot f_\omega\cdot f_\varepsilon$, unavailable without the recovered density. Two routes: (i) fallback — plain unweighted MC over $\hat f_\varepsilon$ (already sketched, MSM-style); (ii) ELVIS-native — add $E[R_i(\tilde\tau)\mid Z_i]$ as its own auxiliary-parameter moment (Schennach's own device for bounding functionals of the unobservable, her worked example is literally recovering $E[U]$ this way), fed through the same entropic system already solved for $\theta$; delivers a point value or a genuine **interval** for expected revenue at each $\tilde\tau$. Set-identified $\tau^*$ is **not a non-negotiable outcome** for this project — an interval-valued Laffer-curve conclusion is an acceptable, arguably more honest deliverable.
- **How set-identification actually works (mechanics, not a metaphor):** for candidate $\theta$, solve the inner problem (minimize $\|E_\pi[\tilde g(Z,\theta,\gamma)]\|$ over $\gamma$) to get a sample objective $\hat L_n(\theta)$; evaluate over a grid (matches her Figures 2–4); the identified/confidence set is $\{\theta:\hat L_n(\theta)\le\text{critical value}\}$, with the critical value from proper sampling theory (Chernozhukov–Hong–Tamer 2007, which her paper explicitly invokes for formal inference, or her own conservative $\chi^2$ shortcut) — **not** an ad hoc tolerance. The shape of $\hat L_n(\theta)$ (sharp trough vs. flat plateau) reveals point- vs. set-identification after the fact; you don't choose which regime you're in beforehand. For the scalar counterfactual $\tilde\tau$, a grid is directly practical (same golden-section-search slot already planned); for the full $\theta$ vector, use coordinate-wise profiling rather than a dense multi-dimensional grid.

**Nominal log-share as the FOC-side observable (2026-08-25):** $\rho_tM_{it}$ (nominal materials expenditure) and $P_tY_{it}$ (nominal sales) are each line items read directly off firm accounts — but $\varepsilon_{it}(M_{it})=\ln\beta-\ln(\rho_tM_{it}/P_tY_{it})$ only ever uses their *ratio*, so $\rho_t,P_t$ never need to be separately constructed for the materials-FOC/evasion-FOC side of the model at all: no price index, no deflation, immune to whichever proxy (sector PPI, GDP deflator, generic inflation index) a given dataset forces you to use when it lacks firm-level quantities+prices. The nominal materials/sales ratio (equivalently the log share $s^*_{it}\equiv\ln(\text{materials}_{it})-\ln(\text{sales}_{it})$) is therefore the right *primitive* to construct whenever building an observable for that side — `eq-eva-emp` in `600-opt-tax.qmd` already does this correctly (builds $\mathcal V_{it}$ from the ratio directly, never from separately-deflated $M^*_{it},Y_{it}$). The production-identity/AR(1) side is different and unavoidably exposed: $\omega_{it}(M_{it})=(1-\beta)\ln M_{it}-\alpha_Kk_{it}-\alpha_Ll_{it}-\ln\beta+\ln\rho_t-\ln P_t$ needs $\ln M_{it}$ and $y_{it}$ *individually* (to combine with $K,L$ in Cobb-Douglas), which is where separately-deflated real quantities — and whatever price-index quality issue a dataset has — actually enter. **Decision:** document this asymmetry, don't model it — no separate deflation-error term is being added; the AR(1)/production side's exposure to price-index quality is a known, out-of-scope limitation. When constructing an observable, keep the ratio/share as one variable; only decompose into $m^*,y$ (or $M,Y$) individually when deriving something that structurally needs the components (FOC inversions, $\omega(M)$, the AR(1) identity) — never as if $M^*,Y$ were themselves separately, cleanly observed. See `Paper/sections/9999-elvis.qmd` for where this shows up in the unified-ELVIS derivation and `Paper/sections/9999-density-trans.qmd` for a caveat on the $(\mathcal V,\tilde{\mathcal W})\leftrightarrow(y,m^*)$ bijection section.

**More ELVIS moments from independence assumptions already maintained (2026-08-25):** read `Lit-Papers/ELVIS_supplement.pdf` (32pp, Schennach's own supplement to @Schennach2014) and the reference GAUSS implementation in `Lit-Papers/ELVIS_code/` (`elvisutil.g`, `elvisexample.g`). Two formal results license turning maintained independence assumptions into additional moments: **Prop. B.1** — a conditional-mean restriction $E[Y\mid X]=0$ is equivalent to a *countable* set of unconditional moments $E[Ye^{i\xi X}]=0$ at rational $\xi$ (Chamberlain 1987 gives an alternative basis); **Prop. B.2** — independence (not just uncorrelatedness) between two unobservable-derived quantities factors the same way through characteristic functions, $E[e^{i\xi X}e^{i\eta Y}]=E[e^{i\xi X}]E[e^{i\eta Y}]$ at rational $(\xi,\eta)$. Her own examples use a practical truncation of this (mean moment + a variance/second-moment moment, each with an interacted version — e.g. Appendix C's interval-regression example adds $E[(V^2-\theta_2)X^2]=0$ to narrow the bounds; Example 1.5, the closest analog to ours, includes a "$V_1V_2$" cross-uncorrelatedness moment between its two error terms alongside the FOC-analogue moments). Going through every independence assumption this model maintains but hasn't yet turned into a moment: $\varepsilon\perp\psi$ (used only implicitly in the $f_{\psi,\omega,\varepsilon}=f_\psi f_\omega f_\varepsilon$ factorization in `9999-density-trans.qmd`) → $E[\varepsilon(M)\psi(M)]=0$; $\omega\perp\varepsilon$ (same factorization) → $E[\omega(M)\varepsilon(M)]=0$; $\psi\perp\omega$ (not stated explicitly before — a genuine overidentifying check on the U-shape functional form, testing nothing's left over after $\delta_1\omega-\delta_2\omega^2$) → $E[\psi(M)\omega(M)]=0$; $\varepsilon_{it}\perp\varepsilon_{it-1}$ and $\varepsilon_{it-1}\perp\psi_{it}$ (flagged as maintained-but-new in `9999-density-trans.qmd`'s dynamic extension, never a moment) → $E[\varepsilon_{it}(M_{it})\varepsilon_{it-1}(M_{it-1})]=0$, $E[\varepsilon_{it-1}(M_{it-1})\psi_{it}(M_{it})]=0$ (free — $(M_{it},M_{it-1})$ are already jointly drawn for the AR(1) row). Also extend the two already-used orthogonality moments ($\varepsilon\perp Z$, $\psi\perp Z$) with variance-matching versions $E[\varepsilon(M)^2-\sigma_\varepsilon^2]=0$, $E[\psi(M)^2-\sigma_\psi^2]=0$ and their $Z$-interacted versions, mirroring the reference examples above. **Not a new moment:** $M\perp e\mid\omega$ — that's the structural reason $e(M)$ can be written as a function of $M$ alone in the first place, already fully used by construction. **Cost, not free:** Theorem F.1 in the supplement gives the confidence-region critical value as $\chi^2_{d_g,\alpha}$ where $d_g$ is the number of moments — every added row raises the bar $-2n\hat L_n(\theta)$ must clear, so more moments isn't automatically a tighter region; her own timing note is "a few minutes... to a few hours (for the one with 27 moment conditions)." Full expanded moment table in `Paper/sections/9999-elvis.qmd` and `600-opt-tax.qmd`.

**Implementation details from the supplement/reference code (2026-08-25):** (i) the confidence region should use the *profiled* statistic $\hat Q_n(\beta)=-(\sup_{\eta,\gamma}\hat L_n((\beta,\eta),\gamma)-\sup_{\theta,\gamma}\hat L_n(\theta,\gamma))$ — subtracting the global sup over the whole unrestricted model — not the raw $\hat L_n(\theta)$ against a threshold as earlier drafts had it; (ii) Theorem F.1 hands a ready closed-form conservative critical value, $\{\theta:\sup_\gamma-2n\hat L_n(\theta,\gamma)\le\chi^2_{d_g,\alpha}\}$, no subsampling needed for a first pass — upgrade to Chernozhukov-Hong-Tamer (2007) subsampling only if a tighter region is wanted; (iii) two standard objective choices, both covered by her Definition F.1: empirical likelihood (Owen) or a CUE-type quadratic $-\tfrac12\hat g'\hat V^{-1}\hat g$ — the reference GAUSS code defaults to the CUE version; (iv) numerically, the reference code samples directly from the $\gamma$-tilted density via Metropolis (not a naive self-normalized importance-sampling ratio over the untilted $\rho$) — more robust, especially with a bounded/awkward support like ours ($M\in(M^*_{it}-1/2\lambda,M^*_{it})$); (v) computationally, $\theta$ is gridded/profiled (its optimization problem is "difficult" — possibly a whole set of maximizers) while $(\text{nuisance},\gamma)$ is optimized via a derivative-free simplex (Nelder-Mead) for each $\theta$ (its problem is convex, unique global optimum) — matches the "coordinate-wise profiling" plan already noted above.

**Sign/scaling convention for the eventual test statistic — clarified against AK2020's actual code (2026-08-31), not yet implemented.** The $-2n\hat L_n(\theta,\gamma)\le\chi^2_{d_g,\alpha}$ formula above is written in **Schennach's own sign convention**: her $\hat L_n$ is the *negative*, maximized CUE quadratic (her Definition F.1, $-\tfrac12\hat g'\hat V^{-1}\hat g$) — so $-2n\hat L_n=-2n\cdot(-\tfrac12 g'V^{-1}g)=n\cdot g'V^{-1}g$, always non-negative, as a test statistic must be. Confirmed this is exactly what AK2020 do too, checked directly in their code (`/Volumes/SSD Hans/Github/TopicsDecisionMaking/ReplicationAK/FirstApp/`, also duplicated at `/Volumes/SSD Hans/Github/Adv Micro/Market Power/REP_0621/FirstApp/`): `cudafunctions/cuda_fastoptim.jl:67,100` defines their objective as `Qn2 = 1/2*dvecdum2'*Omega2*dvecdum2` — **positive**, **minimized** (opposite sign/direction from Schennach's $\hat L_n$) — and `procedures/1App_main.jl:78,96,103` then builds their reported test statistic as `TSMC = 2*minf*n`, i.e. $2n\cdot Q_{n2,\min}$. **Stage-2 moment system, finalized (2026-08-28) — dropped the spare-instrument rows, added an eps-heterogeneity block and a new $\sigma^2_\psi$ nuisance parameter.** Design conversation (not a fresh derivation — pressure-testing candidate moments against what's actually maintained in the model) landed on a 15-moment system, replacing the old 7-moment one (`psi`, `psi·Z` for `Z∈{lag_k,lag_l,cross-instrument}`, `eps·psi`, `om·psi`, `eps·om`). $\theta_{\text{smooth}}=(\delta_0,\delta_1,\delta_2,\sigma^2_\psi)$ (grew by one), $\lambda$ still gridded (support-changing, per the existing headline-spec reasoning), $\gamma\in\mathbb R^{15}$ (one per moment, so the full BOBYQA inner problem per $\lambda$-grid-point is now $4+15=19$-dimensional, up from $3+7=10$). Full row list and rationale now live in `Code/Rcpp/1200-stage2-elvis.cpp`'s header comment (authoritative — this entry summarizes the *decisions*, not the final formulas).
- **Dropped `psi*lag_k`/`psi*lag_l`/`psi*(cross-instrument)` entirely.** No longer any economic story needed for lagged $K,L$: the closed-form materials-FOC inversion, $(1-\beta)\ln M=\omega+\alpha_Kk+\alpha_Ll+\text{const}$, shows that **given $\omega$, $M$ is (up to that affine map) a deterministic function of $(k,l)$ alone** — so `psi·k`,`psi·l` interactions were already, indirectly, testing $M\perp e\mid\omega$ (the materials-FOC/evasion-FOC separation) rotated into observables. Replaced by the more direct `psi·ln(M)`, `psi·(ln M)^2` — same identifying content, no observations lost to lagging, and $M$ ("true inputs") is the object the independence assumption is actually about, not a proxy. Used $\ln M$, not raw $M$: raw materials levels are $O(10^4)$ while every other row is a log-scale object $O(0.1\text{-}3)$ — a raw-$M$ row would dominate `cov(Ghat)`'s eigenvalues in `cue_objective`'s relative-truncation step and could silently crowd out genuinely informative smaller-scale rows.
- **Rejected `E[psi*e]=0`.** Given $\omega$, $e$ is not a separate stochastic primitive — it's the evasion FOC's own solution, a strictly monotonic invertible function of $\psi$ ($h(e)=\psi+\delta_0-\delta_1\omega+\delta_2\omega^2$). "$\psi\perp e$" asks whether a policy function's output is independent of the shock that determines it — false by construction, not a testable assumption. $M$, by contrast, is the *materials* FOC's solution, a different optimization problem that doesn't have $\psi$ or $e$ as an argument given $\omega$ — so $\psi\perp M\mid\omega$ (unlike $\psi\perp e$) is a genuine, non-tautological claim.
- **Rejected `E[eM]-E[e]E[M]=0`** as an operationalization of $M\perp e\mid\omega$: law of total covariance, $\mathrm{Cov}(e,M)=E_\omega[\mathrm{Cov}(e,M\mid\omega)]+\mathrm{Cov}(E[e|\omega],E[M|\omega])$ — conditional independence kills only the first term; the second is generically nonzero here since both $E[e|\omega]$ (U-shaped cost) and $E[M|\omega]$ (materials FOC) vary systematically with $\omega$. So this moment is actively misspecified under $H_0$, not merely uninformative — the correctly-conditioned version is `psi·ln(M)` above (psi is already "$e$ purged of $\omega$").
- **New eps-heterogeneity block, matched per firm's own `sic_3`.** CORP $\varepsilon$'s mean is exactly zero within every industry (to floating-point precision) — not approximate, because `first_stage_panel`/`first_stage_panel_me` (`021-deconv-funs.R`) already fit $\beta$ separately per `sic_3`, forcing the residual mean to zero by that regression's own normal equations. But **variance is not homogeneous across industries**: per-`sic_3` sd ranges 0.17–0.63 (pooled 0.42; `stage2_data`, `ins=lag_m`, CORP only), Bartlett's test on the 27 industries with $n\ge30$ gives $K^2=920$, $p<2.2\times10^{-16}$ — pooling would misspecify the variance/skewness rows for most industries, not just lose precision. Central 3rd moment also varies meaningfully ($-0.075$ to $+0.256$). New rows: $E[\varepsilon-\hat\mu_1]=0$, $E[(\varepsilon-\hat\mu_1)^2-\hat\sigma^2]=0$, $E[(\varepsilon-\hat\mu_1)^3-\hat\mu_3]=0$, $E[\psi\cdot(\varepsilon-\hat\mu_1)^2]=0$ — targets computed once per `(sic_3,ins)` from CORP $\varepsilon$ in `Code/Deconvolution/1206-stage2-eps-targets.R` → `Code/Products/1206-stage2-eps-targets.RData`, joined onto the estimation sample by `sic_3`, passed into the Rcpp worker as per-firm vectors (same pattern as `beta`, which is already per-industry). Only 2 of 29 industries are thin ($n<30$: sic 354 $n=22$, sic 353 $n=2$) and fall back to the `ins`-level pooled target. **Reused the existing per-industry computation** (spot-checked against a leftover `fs_list` snapshot in `Code/Products/np-deconv-funs.RData` — sic 331's `epsilon_mu`/`epsilon_sigma` matched the hand-computed sample moments exactly) rather than the abandoned `ecdf()`-based per-industry code in `235-tims-test.R`/`_600-CD-GRN-eps.R` (exploratory, not part of the active pipeline, checked before writing anything new — has a dead-code `<`/`<-` typo). Didn't reuse `030-np-deconv-funs.R`'s `get_stats()`/`np_pdf()` kernel-density machinery either: that's for recovering $f_e$/$f_u$ by deconvolution from the pooled sample; CORP $\varepsilon$ is directly observed ($e=0$ exactly, so $\varepsilon=-\mathcal V$ exactly, no deconvolution error to smooth over), so plain per-industry sample moments are the right, simplest target.
- **New $\sigma^2_\psi$ nuisance parameter**, defined by its own moment $E[\psi^2-\sigma^2_\psi]=0$, unlocking a heteroskedasticity-in-$\psi$ block: $E[(\psi^2-\sigma^2_\psi)\cdot\omega]=0$, $\cdot\omega^2$, $\cdot(\varepsilon-\hat\mu_1)$ — does the cost shock's *dispersion*, not just its mean, still depend on $\omega$/$\varepsilon$ after the U-shape and independence are imposed. Same "anchor-then-multiply" logic as the rest of the system: any quantity pinned to exact zero mean by its own moment (here $\psi^2-\sigma^2_\psi$, elsewhere $\psi$ itself) can be safely multiplied by anything else for a clean $\mathrm{Cov}=0$ test, without needing that "anything else" to be zero-mean itself.
- **Dropped one row as redundant before finalizing:** `psi·(eps^2-var)` alongside `psi·eps_c^2` — algebraically identical in population given $E[\psi]=0$ is already enforced by the `psi` row (the two differ only by $-\text{var}\cdot\psi$, whose expectation is exactly 0 at any point satisfying that row), so only `psi·eps_c^2` was kept.
- **Implemented and smoke-tested clean** (both `corner_mode` values, tiny `n_burn=5,n_keep=20`, single-point $\lambda$ grid; `Code/Deconvolution/1206-stage2-eps-targets.R` → `1200-stage2-elvis.cpp` → `1210-stage2-elvis-driver.R`, all three touched). **Not yet run at real scale** — the wide-grid run from the 2026-08-27 entry needs re-running against this moment system before trusting any $(\lambda,\delta_0,\delta_1,\delta_2,\sigma^2_\psi)$ point estimate; inner-problem dimension nearly doubled (10→19) so expect meaningfully slower BOBYQA per grid point. **`sigma2_psi`'s warm-start init is a placeholder (1.0)** in `1210`'s `par_init` — pending the warm-start redesign (S-draw $\varepsilon$ sampling + moment-shaped objective, next session's task, not yet started).

**Parallelization over firms, implemented (2026-08-28) — `mclapply`, no C++ changes.** Each firm's Metropolis chain inside `mh_tilted_average_cpp` was already fully independent of every other firm's (per-firm reseeded RNG, `base_seed+row_id[i]`, set once when `.row_id` is assigned and untouched by any later filtering), so this was a pure engineering win, not a design question: `cue_objective` (`1210-stage2-elvis-driver.R`) now takes an `n_cores` argument; when `>1`, `dat` is split via `parallel::splitIndices`, each chunk run through `mh_tilted_average_cpp` in a separate forked process via `parallel::mclapply`, results `rbind`ed back. **Verified bit-identical** to the serial path (`max(abs(G1-G_parallel))==0` exactly, not just "close") at both a tiny smoke scale and the realistic wide-grid scale ($n\approx2900$, `n_burn=100,n_keep=1000`) — expected, since row order doesn't affect `colMeans`/`cov`, but confirmed rather than assumed. **Speedup at realistic scale: ~4.7x at 8 cores, ~5.1x at 11 cores** (12-core machine) — sub-linear (fork + serialization overhead), but a real win; at the earlier tiny toy scale (`n_burn=5,n_keep=20`) the same test only showed ~1.6x, i.e. **don't judge this from toy smoke tests, only from realistic `n_burn`/`n_keep`.**
- **RcppParallel not needed.** Was the fallback plan if `mclapply`'s per-call fork overhead (repeated on every BOBYQA objective evaluation, hundreds of times per lambda grid point) turned out to dominate — it doesn't, at the realistic scale that actually matters. Raw OpenMP was never seriously considered: Apple's shipped clang doesn't support `-fopenmp` on macOS (Intel or Apple Silicon), needing Homebrew `libomp` + `Makevars` surgery for no benefit `mclapply` doesn't already deliver here. MPI was never in play — that's for distributed memory across nodes, irrelevant on a single machine.

The sweep script gained a 4th positional arg (`N_CORES`, per process) so the two parallelism layers can be sized together — e.g. `N_CORES=3` on a 12-core machine uses the whole machine across the 4-way sweep without oversubscribing. A single standalone `1210` invocation (not via the sweep script) can safely use a much higher `n_cores`.

**Superseded same day: replaced `mclapply` with `RcppParallel` after actually measuring the overhead it was claimed not to justify.** The "no need for RcppParallel" conclusion above was premature — it was based on "beats serial," not "captures the available gain." Pressed on this, measured directly: pure fork+collect overhead of one `mclapply(mc.cores=8)` call, isolated with a trivial no-op workload in the actual loaded session (not a bare `Rscript -e`, which understates it — a heavier process footprint forks slower), is **~8.65ms**, paid on *every single* BOBYQA objective evaluation, since `mclapply` re-forks from scratch each call rather than keeping a persistent pool alive across the whole search. At the realistic scale ($n\approx2900$, `n_burn=100,n_keep=1000`), `mclapply` was capturing ~4.9x of a theoretical ~8x ceiling on 8 cores — real headroom being left on the table.
- **Implemented:** `Code/Rcpp/1200-stage2-elvis.cpp`'s per-firm loop restructured into an `RcppParallel::Worker` (`TiltedMomentWorker`), dispatched via `RcppParallel::parallelFor` — a TBB-backed thread pool created once and reused across every call from the same R session, paying the setup cost once instead of per objective evaluation. Low-risk refactor, not a redesign: the per-firm computation was already thread-safe by construction (local `std::mt19937_64` per firm, writes only to its own output row), so no algorithm changes were needed — only mechanical restructuring plus one real correctness fix: `Rcpp::NumericVector`/`NumericMatrix` touch R's memory allocator, which isn't safe to call from worker threads, so every per-firm scratch buffer (`g_current`, `g_try`, `g_run`) was switched from `Rcpp::NumericVector` to `std::array<double,15>` (stack-allocated, no R API), and every R-facing input/output wrapped in RcppParallel's `RVector`/`RMatrix` (thread-safe raw-pointer accessors over the same memory, not a copy) before entering the parallel region.

Combined, the *original* serial-with-NumericVector baseline (174.5ms) to the new 12-thread RcppParallel version (10.0ms) is a **~17.5x** total improvement.
- **R-side simplified, not just extended:** `cue_objective` in `1210-stage2-elvis-driver.R` dropped the `mclapply`/`splitIndices` branching entirely — it's back to a single direct call to `mh_tilted_average_cpp`, since parallelism now lives inside that call. ## Counterfactual
Optimal sales-tax rate $\tau$: revenue rises mechanically but higher $\tau$ raises the evasion incentive (Laffer trade-off). Also "tax rate giving median evasion = 0."

**Computing $\tau^*$ is a forward simulation, not new estimation** — $\theta$ (and $f_\psi$, if MSL) are already fixed from stage 2; MLE/MCMC are the wrong tools for this step.
- For each firm, recover the implied type $(\omega,\psi)$ at the *observed* $\tau$ via the same map used in the likelihood (`@eq-Lunc`); hold that type fixed (policy-invariant) and resolve the evasion FOC at a candidate $\tilde\tau$ — closed form under linear $q$: $e'(\tilde\tau)=\frac{\tilde\tau\rho_t-C(\omega,\psi)}{2\lambda\tilde\tau\rho_t}$, $C(\omega,\psi)\equiv\textbf{e}^{\delta_0-\delta_1\omega+\delta_2\omega^2+\psi}$
- $e'$ is a deterministic function of the *same* $\varepsilon$ that generated $(\omega,\psi)$ — integrating $e'$ against its own marginal $f_{e'}$ separately from $\varepsilon$ double-counts (same bug class as the "$e,\varepsilon$ are one latent" insight above). Expected revenue is a single integral over $\varepsilon$, weighted as in the likelihood and normalized by the firm's own likelihood contribution $\mathcal L_i(\theta)$
- **MSL route:** reuse stage-2 quadrature nodes, weighted by $f_\psi$
- **MSM route** (no $f_\psi$ recovered): plain Monte Carlo over $\varepsilon^{(s)}\sim\hat f_\varepsilon$, unweighted; use common random numbers across candidate $\tilde\tau$ so the search doesn't chase simulation noise
- Find $\tilde\tau^*$ by grid/golden-section search over the scalar $\tilde\tau$ (plot the objective first — confirm single-peaked before reporting a point estimate)
