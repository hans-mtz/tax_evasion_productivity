## The model

- Firm overreports materials: observed $M^*=M+e$ (additive), or $u=\ln(M^*/M)$ (multiplicative). $e\ge0$ is evasion.
  - **Notation:** this deck uses $u$ throughout for the overreporting log-ratio (Quarto-Slides: `200-model.qmd`, `600-opt-tax.qmd`, `500-opt-tax-claude.qmd`, `700-deconvolving-evasion.qmd`). The approved first-stage paper (`56-id-evasion.qmd`, footnote `linear-exp`) is untouched/read-only and defines its own "$e$" *multiplicatively* ($M^*=M\exp(e)$) — that paper's $e$ **is** this deck's $u$, not the additive structural $e$ used in $q(e)$, $\kappa(e,\omega)$. Translate when citing it. Deconvolution only ever recovers $u=\ln(1+e/M)$, never the level $e$ — a property of the current CD/log-linear identification strategy (under translog the map would need rederiving, though the general principle survives: whatever enters the log residual $\mathcal V=u-\varepsilon$ additively is some transform of $e$ scaled by $M$). See [[reorg-history]] in `Research-log/log.md` for the switch from $\tilde e$.
  - Firm-invariance of $f_u$ is **not a requirement for deconvolution — it's the (separate, testable) claim about what deconvolution outputs.** The convolution formula $f_V=\int f_\varepsilon(V-u)f_u(u)\,du$ only needs $\varepsilon\perp u$ and known $f_\varepsilon$; it always returns *some* population marginal $f_u$ regardless of whether individual firms' distributions actually differ. Firm-invariance is the stronger, separate claim that this recovered marginal is the same object for every firm — motivated by a scale argument (pooling raw $e$ across firms of different size mixes distributions of different scale; the relative measure $u$ is more plausibly size-invariant), not (yet) empirically tested. Don't test it by regressing $\mathcal V_{it}$ on $M^*_{it}$ — that's mechanical, since $\mathcal V_{it}=\ln(\rho_tM^*_{it}/P_tY_{it})-\ln\hat\beta$ has $M^*_{it}$ inside its own formula; a valid diagnostic buckets by a proxy that doesn't enter $\mathcal V$'s definition (e.g. $K_{it}$ or $L_{it}$) and repeats the 1-D deconvolution within each bucket — equivalently, deconvolves $f_{u|Z}$ under the joint condition $\varepsilon\perp(u,Z)$ (continuous $Z$ needs nonparametric deconvolution with a covariate, e.g. Fan–Truong 1993 — much harder, slower rates).
  - $M_{it}\perp e_{it}\mid\omega_{it}$ ($\omega$ exogenous): neither firm's materials FOC nor its evasion FOC contains the other's choice variable. Relevant to a supervisor's general framing: any $h(M,e)$ entering $\mathcal V=h(M,e)-\varepsilon$ is recoverable by deconvolution provided $\varepsilon\perp(e,M)$ and $M\perp e$ — here $h(M,e)=u=\ln(1+e/M)$ is the specific instance.
- Production: $Y=K^{\alpha_K}L^{\alpha_L}M^{\beta}\exp(\omega+\varepsilon)$. $\omega$ = productivity, $\varepsilon$ = **measurement error in output** with $E[\varepsilon]=0$ — NOT an ex-post output shock as in GNR (2020). Supervisors' choice. Consequences: productivity is $\exp(\omega)$, not $\exp(\omega+\varepsilon)$ as in the canonical productivity literature (Olley–Pakes onward); the firm's FOC involves no $\mathcal E=E[\exp\varepsilon]$, so the materials share is $\ln(\rho M/PY)=\ln\beta-\varepsilon$ and $\hat\beta=\exp(E[s\mid\text{corp}])$ (code: `first_stage_panel_me`, `big_E <- 1`). The approved paper `56-id-evasion.qmd` still carries the GNR-style $\mathcal E$ (predates the change); translate when porting. Test and deconvolution use only $\ln D=E[s\mid\text{corp}]$, so they are unaffected either way.
- Firm maximizes expected profit with detection probability $q(e)$ (audit + always caught, à la Allingham 1972) and evasion cost $\kappa(e,\omega)$.
- **Evasion FOC:** $\tau\rho_t\big(1-(1+\phi)(q+q'e)\big)=\partial\kappa/\partial e$ (usually $\phi=0$).
- **SOC (cost linear in $e$):** $-q''e/q'<2$.
- $\rho_t$ = materials price (sector–time); $\tau$ = tax rate. $\tau\rho_t$ is the **benefit shifter** — the key source of identification (see Estimation). Under the two-tax-rate model below, this is specifically $\tau_P\rho_t$.

### Detection function $q(e)$ — DECISIONS
- **Linear $q=\min\{\lambda e,1\}$ is the HEADLINE spec, and stays headline after testing the alternative below.** SOC holds unconditionally ($q''=0$); FOC ceiling $\lambda e<\tfrac12$; detection $=50\%$ at the ceiling. Audit-threshold story: $T\sim U[0,1/\lambda]$, audit if $e\ge T$; $1/\lambda$ = max tolerated overreporting, mean threshold $1/2\lambda$.
- **Concave/exponential $q=1-e^{-\lambda e}$** — robustness check, tested and rejected as a fix for the extreme-evader leverage problem (see Estimation → current status): only delays the ceiling by a fixed ~2x factor ($\lambda e<1$ vs. linear's $<\tfrac12$), doesn't come close to matching what trimming the top 1% of firms by $M^*$ buys. Kept as a documented robustness check, not pursued further for that purpose. $T\sim\text{Exp}(\lambda)$, ceiling $\lambda e<1$, $\approx63\%$ at $e=1/\lambda$.
- **Power (convex) $q=(\lambda e)^k$** — nests linear at $k=1$; SOC holds for all $k>0$; ceiling $\lambda e<(1+k)^{-1/k}<1$. **Cannot normalize $\lambda e\in[0,1]$** (violates the FOC ceiling → log of negative). Not tested (would only tighten the ceiling further — the wrong direction for the extreme-evader problem); deprioritized.
- **Report detection probability in relative, not absolute, terms.** Without audit-rate data, an absolute $q(e)$ level isn't independently credible/calibratable — a skeptic can always dispute $\hat\lambda$'s level. Under the headline linear $q$, the ratio $q(e_A)/q(e_B)=e_A/e_B$ is exactly $\hat\lambda$-invariant (it cancels), so relative detection risk against a benchmark (e.g. the median evader) is a robust, assumption-light readout of the evasion distribution alone — defensible even to someone who rejects the fitted $\hat\lambda$ outright. Implemented as the "Rel. to median" column in `Code/Deconvolution/1303-detection-prob-table.R` → `Paper/tbls/1303-detection-prob-table.png` (e.g. the P99 evader is ~41–55$\times$ more likely to be caught than the median firm, depending on instrument).

### Cost function $\kappa$
$\kappa_{it}=e_{it}\exp\{\delta_0-\delta_1\omega_{it}+\delta_2\omega_{it}^2+\psi_{it}\}$ — linear in $e$, **convex in $\omega$** (min at $\omega^*=\delta_1/2\delta_2$, estimated near the 86th percentile; describe it as convex, never "U-shaped": the evasion-by-size pattern it rationalizes is highly asymmetric, rising roughly linearly to p90–95 and then falling off a cliff at the very top, as the most productive firms substitute to legal avoidance). $\psi$ = idiosyncratic cost shock. **$f_\psi$ is the central object MSL would recover** (moot for the ELVIS route currently in use, which never recovers $f_\psi$ — see Estimation). $\theta=(\lambda,\delta_0,\delta_1,\delta_2)$, plus $\sigma^2_\psi$ and a relative-evasion floor $\eta$ in the current stage-2 code (see Estimation).

### Corporations — DECISIONS
- **Constrained non-evaders**: $e=0$ is a hard institutional constraint (audited statements, third-party reporting), **outside the FOC**, used **only in the first stage**.
- **No $\delta_C$ shifter** (supervisors rejected giving corps an exogenous cost shifter). **Corner term DROPPED from the ELVIS estimation** — can't classify corner firms exogenously; U-shaped cost handles both tails through the interior channel. (The MSL likelihood does **not** need to classify firms and includes the $e^*=0$ mass — see the MSL entry below.) Data show near-zero, imprecise evasion at both tails.

## Estimation

**Two stages.**
1. **First stage** (`Paper/sections/56-id-evasion.qmd`, supervisor-approved, read-only). Non-evaders (corps) with $e=0$ give $\mathcal V=-\varepsilon$ → recover $\beta$, $f_\varepsilon$, and by **deconvolution** $f_e$. Constructs the two observed residuals: $\mathcal V_{it}=u_{it}-\varepsilon_{it}$ (`eq-ob-ev`) and $\tilde{\mathcal W}_{it}=\omega_{it}+(1-\beta)\varepsilon_{it}$ (`eq-w-squiggle`).
2. **Second stage**: estimate $\theta$ (and $f_\psi$, under MSL only) from the unincorporated firms plus corp firms as a specification check.

**Key econometric insight:** $e$ and $\varepsilon$ are **one** latent, not two — observed $\mathcal V$ ties them. Integrate out only $\varepsilon$. $f_e$ was already consumed in the first stage; it does NOT reappear in the second-stage likelihood.

### Three candidate estimators for stage 2 — status

**MSL (quadrature-MLE) — PARAMETRIC secondary/robustness estimator, NOT the headline (decision 2026-09-19, tollgate paragraph below). Built as the density-based alternative (plan agreed with the user after the Tim Conley conversation; spec in `Paper/sections/9999-msl-implementation.qmd`, simulation-recovery harness `Code/Deconvolution/1400-msl-sim-harness.R`).** Standalone, cast in $\varepsilon$-space (per-firm quadrature over $\varepsilon$) — deliberately NOT built on the ELVIS $M$-space header (`1200-stage2-elvis-common.h`). Order: parametric $f_\psi=N(0,\sigma^2_\psi)$ first, $E[\psi]=0$ (as in ELVIS), then a penalized-B-spline logspline $f_\psi$ (existing R machinery `030-np-deconv-funs.R`, moved to C++; knots/support from the ELVIS forward-simulated $\psi$); $f_\omega$ **Version A** first (unconditional logspline recovered from $\tilde{\mathcal W}$ on all firms, thin industries pooled like $\varepsilon$), Version B (AR(1) $f_\eta$, 2-D integral) after; same trimmed lagged-observation sample as ELVIS for comparability. The full likelihood contribution is the interior $\varepsilon$-integral **plus** the $e^*=0$ branch $f_\varepsilon(-\mathcal V)f_\omega(\omega_0)[1-F_\psi(h(0)-C(\omega_0))]$, $C(\omega)=\delta_0-\delta_1\omega+\delta_2\omega^2$, $\omega_0=\tilde{\mathcal W}+(1-\beta)\mathcal V$. **Validated on the model-faithful DGP** (`Code/Deconvolution/1410-dgp-model.R`, `1412-msl-mc.R`; 10-seed Monte Carlo): M-only unbiased (all $|t|\le1.3$); M+K with Version A biases $\delta_{0,1,2}$ by +38/+39/+62% (unconditional $f_\omega$ ignores the $\omega$-$k$ dependence), Version B (per-firm normal $f_{\omega_t\mid\tilde{\mathcal W}_{t-1}}$, closed form, no C++ change) cuts it to +13/+11/+16% with $\lambda$ within 2%. **Feasible Version Cf** (Gaussian $f_{\omega_t\mid\tilde{\mathcal W}_{t-1},k_{t-1},k_t}$, from regressing $\tilde{\mathcal W}_t$ on them since $\varepsilon_t$ is independent of all three) cuts it to +5/+3/+4% ($|t|\le1.2$), $\lambda$ +3%; the derivation's AR(1) step conditioned on too little (a noisy $\tilde{\mathcal W}_{t-1}$ is not sufficient for $\omega_{t-1}$ — the posterior must use the whole observed information set), and the exact-observables likelihood equals the current one up to a $\theta$-free factor, so only $f_\omega$ conditioning matters. Residual small bias is documented as a limitation. **Quadrature is over $s=\ln(1-2\lambda e)$, not $\varepsilon$** (the $\varepsilon$ rule mis-integrates a spike at the ceiling and produced degenerate spurious optima; real-data fit unaffected). The dynamic-likelihood expression in `9999-density-trans.qmd` Step 3 integrates $\varepsilon_{t-1}$ against its prior, which is wrong — corrected in `9999-msl-implementation.qmd`; that note and `610-MSL.qmd` still carry the old form. Likelihood = density of the observables $(\mathcal V,\tilde{\mathcal W})$, a structural MLE in the sense of Hausman (1985), built from the FOC via change of variables:
$$\mathcal L_i^{\text{unc}}=\int f_\psi(\psi(\varepsilon))\,J_i(\varepsilon)\,f_\omega(\omega(\varepsilon))\,f_\varepsilon(\varepsilon)\,d\varepsilon$$
with maps $e(\varepsilon)=M^*(1-e^{-\mathcal V-\varepsilon})$, $\omega(\varepsilon)=\tilde{\mathcal W}-(1-\beta)\varepsilon$, $\psi(\varepsilon)=h(e)-\delta_0+\delta_1\omega-\delta_2\omega^2$, $h(e)\equiv\ln(\tau_P\rho_t)+\ln(1-2\lambda e)$ for linear $q$ (domain $e<1/2\lambda$). Jacobian $J_i(\varepsilon)=|h'(e)|\,M^*$ ($M$, the true unobserved materials level, held fixed as $e$ varies — not $M^*$, which moves with $e$). Correctly conditioning on the firm's own AR(1) persistence replaces bare $f_\omega$ with $f_{\omega_{it}\mid\tilde{\mathcal W}_{it-1}}$, built from $f_\eta$ and the POSTERIOR of $\omega_{it-1}$ given $\tilde{\mathcal W}_{it-1}$ (not the prior of $\varepsilon_{it-1}$: $\tilde{\mathcal W}_{it-1}$ contains it); for ANY densities (logspline, kernel, empirical) this is a per-firm 1-D integral over $\omega_{it-1}$ that does NOT depend on $\theta$ — it involves only stage-1 objects ($f_\varepsilon,f_\omega,f_\eta,g$) and $\tilde{\mathcal W}_{it-1}$ — so it is tabulated once per firm on a fixed $\omega$-grid and interpolated inside every likelihood evaluation (checked numerically against the Gaussian closed form to $3\times10^{-15}$); the estimator's $\theta$-loop stays a 1-D integral over $\varepsilon_{it}$. Under normality it collapses to a normal with firm-specific mean/sd (used in the simulation, no C++ change). Corp $\varepsilon$ is visibly skewed in the real data (skewness $\approx2$ in some industries), so normal $f_\varepsilon$ is not adequate there. A firm's first-observed period uses the unconditional $f_\omega$ (or is dropped, as the lagged-observation sample does). Full derivations, and the corrections that got the Jacobian and the $\omega\perp(K,L)$ assumption right, are in `Paper/sections/9999-density-trans.qmd` and `Research-log/log.md`. $\Psi=\psi+\nu$ is **NOT a clean deconvolution** ($\text{Cov}(\psi,\nu)\ne0$ because $e=e^*(\psi,\omega)$ is endogenous). Preferred implementation if pursued: deterministic quadrature (1-D integral) + logspline/sieve for $f_\psi$ (Chen 2007; Kooperberg–Stone 1991; Eilers–Marx 1996), or simulated MLE (Train 2009; consistent under Lee 1992, moot under quadrature). Two-step SEs need the Murphy-Topel (1985) correction or a joint bootstrap.

**TOLLGATE, settled 2026-09-19 (owed a paper/slide statement): ELVIS is the headline stage-2 estimator; MSL is kept as a parametric robustness check to be refined later.** Real-data cross-test (lag_m, trim 0.5%, each estimator's best point evaluated under the others' tests; `1440`–`1443`): ELVIS's absolute test ($TS=2n\hat L$, $\chi^2_{9,.95}=16.92$) does not reject its own point ($TS=13.9$) and rejects MSL's point (1,715) and naive MSM's (2,320); the naive MSM statistic ($\chi^2_{5,.95}=11.07$) rejects at every point, including its own minimum (66k/95k/29k) and, in simulation, the TRUE $\theta$ (80–92k) — no discriminating power; MSL's LR (vs its max, $\chi^2_{4,.95}=9.49$) and plant-clustered efficient-score test both reject ELVIS's point (46,481; $T=3{,}392$) and MSM's. **Argument (user's, adopted):** MSL is parametric — it imposes normal $f_\psi$, $f_\varepsilon$, $f_\omega$ (Version A: unconditional), shapes we do not know to be true — so its rejection of ELVIS's point is "the best point under that specification rejects ELVIS", not evidence against ELVIS; ELVIS leaves the distribution of the unobservables free (needs only $\hat\beta$ from stage 1, never $f_\psi$ or $f_\varepsilon$) and is robust in exactly that sense. MSL's tests also carry no goodness-of-fit component (and Version A is biased on the sim: +38–62% in the $\delta$'s), so they cannot adjudicate; the information-matrix test was considered and dropped (low power, uninformative either way). Honest limits to state: this is an argument for ELVIS's robustness to distributional assumptions, NOT a demonstration that ELVIS recovers the truth — simulation validation of ELVIS (data-calibrated 321 DGP, `1450`/`1451`) is deferred as future robustness, and the ELVIS-on-simulation cube was stopped. Future work: refine MSL (feasible $f_{\omega_t\mid\tilde{\mathcal W}_{t-1},k_{t-1},k_t}$, skewed/nonparametric $f_\varepsilon$, logspline $f_\psi$) as a parametric robustness check. Full numbers in `Research-log/log.md` (2026-09-19, "night").

**MSM — rejected (naive version); a posterior-weighted MSM is redundant given MSL.** The natural-looking alternative (simulating $\psi(\varepsilon^{(s)})$ and averaging, or wrapping that in GMM) does **not** deliver a valid moment: $\psi(\cdot)$ is nonlinear in $\varepsilon$, so the simulated average converges to $\int\psi(\varepsilon;\theta)f_\varepsilon(\varepsilon)d\varepsilon$, not to the firm's true $\psi_{it}$ — missing exactly the posterior reweighting that makes MSL's analogous average valid. A *correct* simulated moment averages over the firm's posterior $w_i(\varepsilon)/\int w_i$, with $w_i=f_\psi(\psi(\varepsilon))J_i(\varepsilon)f_\omega(\omega(\varepsilon))f_\varepsilon(\varepsilon)$ — it needs $f_\psi$, which is exactly what MSL estimates (and the denominator is the MSL likelihood), so weighted MSM adds nothing over MSL; ELVIS is the $f_\psi$-free robustness check. See `Research-log/log.md` for the full rejection argument and `610-MSL.qmd`'s "correct MSM" slides.

**Empirically confirmed, 2026-09-13/14 (Tim Conley's own proposed variant, `Code/Deconvolution/1201-MSM.R`).** Tim (thesis committee, spatial-inference specialist, skeptical of ELVIS on preference grounds) proposed a concrete fix: draw $\varepsilon$ from the population/industry-pooled CORP-$\varepsilon$ distribution subject only to feasibility ($e>0,M>0$), then estimate $\theta$ via efficient-weighted GMM (Hansen 1982) and test fit via the resulting statistic (under test inversion $\theta$ is fixed at each guess, so its limit is $\chi^2_{d_g}$, not $\chi^2_{d_g-d_\theta}$, even when exactly identified — not an overidentification test). Implemented, two real bugs found and fixed (CUE quadratic form was pairing the eigenbasis-rotated weight matrix with the un-rotated raw moment vector instead of the correctly-projected one — dimensionally legal, silently wrong, confirmed by a ~15% error on a synthetic check; and the moment covariance was pooling all firm×draw rows as i.i.d. instead of collapsing to one per-firm average first, per SMM asymptotics — McFadden 1989; Pakes & Pollard 1989), then rerun with a genuine overidentifying moment ($\psi\cdot\ln M$, $d_g=5>d_\theta=4$). **Result: decisive rejection for both instruments** (lag_m: $J=29{,}258$; lag_2_cal_W: $J=28{,}961$; both vs. $\chi^2_{5,.95}=11.07$ — off by a factor of ~2,600), lag_2_cal_W's $\delta_0$ pinned exactly at its box bound ($-50$), and both instruments returning economically implausible $\lambda,\delta_1,\delta_2$ (wrong-signed for lag_2_cal_W) and enormous $\sigma^2_\psi$ (321.6, 81.8). **The exactly-identified case (4 moments, dropping $\psi\cdot\ln M$; reference $\chi^2_{4,.95}=9.49$) rerun separately under the same corrected objective shows this isn't about the added moment**: $\hat L_n\approx1.0$–$1.02$ there too (barely different from the overidentified fit), with a parameter pinned at $\pm50$ in both instruments (lag_m's $\hat\delta_1$; lag_2_cal_W's $\hat\delta_0$) and $\hat\sigma^2_\psi$ up to 6,416 — no $(\lambda,\delta_0,\delta_1,\delta_2)$ reconciles even the first four moments alone. This is the same underlying failure as the theoretical argument above, now confirmed via Tim's own preferred remedy rather than just asserted: population-marginal, feasibility-filtered sampling of $\varepsilon$ is not a valid substitute for a firm's true posterior-consistent conditional distribution, and no fixed $\theta$ reconciles the resulting moments — exactly the gap ELVIS's entropy tilt $\gamma$ is built to close. Full numbers and diagnosis in `Research-log/log.md`.

**ELVIS — the headline, currently-implemented estimator (decision 2026-09-19).** Schennach (2014, *Econometrica*, `@Schennach2014`) solves exactly MSM's failure mode (a moment nonlinear in an unobservable) without the naive-averaging bug. Recast the moment in the **true materials level** $M_{it}$ as the one unobservable: $e(M)=M^*_{it}-M$, $\varepsilon(M)=\ln(M^*_{it}/M)-\mathcal V_{it}$, $\omega(M)=\tilde{\mathcal W}_{it}-(1-\beta)\varepsilon(M)$, $\psi(M)=h(e(M))-\delta_0+\delta_1\omega(M)-\delta_2\omega(M)^2$, support $(M^*_{it}-\tfrac1{2\lambda},M^*_{it})$ truncated at the physical floor $M\ge0$. Her Theorem 2.1: the question "does *some* conditional distribution of $M$ given $Z$ satisfy $E[g(M,Z,\theta)]=0$" is equivalent to a finite-dimensional system in $(\theta,\gamma)$ built from an entropy-maximizing exponential tilt $\exp(\gamma'g)$ of a chosen dominating measure $\rho(M|Z)$ — the tilt **is** the posterior reweighting MSM was missing, solved for as a Lagrange multiplier rather than assumed. Payoffs: doesn't need $f_\psi$ (like MSM), and — by her Remark 2.3 — doesn't need $f_\varepsilon$ either, since the choice of $\rho$ has zero effect on the point estimate (only support matters); only $\hat\beta$ is needed from stage 1. Delivers $\theta=(\lambda,\delta_0,\delta_1,\delta_2,\sigma^2_\psi,\eta)$ only, not $f_\psi$. Needs $\ge4$ instrument-interacted moments for point identification; under-identification degrades gracefully to a set estimate. Identification of $\lambda$ comes from sector–time variation in $\tau_P\rho_t$ interacted into $Z$. Primary sources: `Lit-Papers/ELVIS.pdf`, `Lit-Papers/schennach-2022-measurement-systems.pdf` (her 2022 *JEL* survey), `Lit-Papers/ELVIS_supplement.pdf`, and a second real-world reference implementation, Aguiar & Kashaev 2020 (`Lit-Papers/AK2020.pdf`, RES — advisor Nail Kashaev's own paper, Julia/CUDA code at `TopicsDecisionMaking/ReplicationAK/`). Full rederivation, including the counterfactual-without-$f_\psi$ auxiliary-parameter device, in `Paper/sections/9999-elvis.qmd`.

**Unified single-stage ELVIS — designed, held in reserve, not implemented.** The recast-in-$M$ move generalizes further: $M_{it}$ is the *only* true unobservable in the whole model, for every firm-period (corp and unincorporated alike). Solving the materials FOC and production identity in $M$ directly would let one system estimate $\beta,\alpha_K,\alpha_L,\gamma_0,\gamma_1,\lambda,\delta_0,\delta_1,\delta_2$ jointly from the full panel, with no separate first stage, no $\mathcal V/\tilde{\mathcal W}$, no Jacobian. Corp's "$e\equiv0$" and unincorp's "evade on average" both become moments rather than hard constraints or a separate sample split. Not pursued yet — a bigger sell than any one meeting has time for; sequenced after the stage-2-only version (already implemented) proves out. Full derivation in `Paper/sections/9999-elvis.qmd`, drafted (not rendered) in `600-opt-tax.qmd`.

### ELVIS implementation conventions (evergreen, apply to any moment system built)
- Gamma is **unbounded** in the optimizer — Schennach (ELVIS.pdf p.359) and AK2020 both treat $\gamma\to\pm\infty$ (a degenerate limiting conditional distribution) as an expected solution, not a numerical failure. Report `max|gamma|` per fit to watch it, but don't box-constrain it.
- **Gamma's dimension never counts against the order condition.** It's a Lagrange multiplier, sized automatically to the moment count and solved jointly with the nuisance parameters (not a structural unknown competing for identifying variation). The order-condition comparison is $\dim(\theta_{\text{smooth}}\text{ incl. nuisance})$ vs. $d_g$ (moment count) — gamma never on the parameter side.
- **Sign/scaling of the test statistic:** our `cue_objective_common` follows AK2020's convention (positive, minimized CUE quadratic `Lhat`), not Schennach's own sign (negative, maximized). The correct test statistic is **`TS = 2*n*Lhat`**, compared against $\chi^2_{d_g,\alpha}$ — mirroring AK2020's literal `TSMC=2*minf*n`. Applying the textbook `-2n*Lhat` formula directly to our own `Lhat` gives the wrong (negative) sign. $n$ is the feasibility-filtered sample size; $d_g$ is the actual moment-row count of whichever system produced `Lhat`.
- **Auxiliary-parameter moments (e.g. $E[e]$, counterfactual revenue) must be jointly estimated with their own moment** ($E[U-\tilde\theta]=0$), never read off a post-hoc average over a tilted sample generated at an already-fixed $(\hat\theta,\hat\gamma)$ — Schennach (2022 JEL, p.1250) warns explicitly against interpreting the entropy-maximizing distribution as the actual distribution of the unobservable. The identified set for $\tilde\theta$ is what's reported, not a point average.
- **When more than one parameter is genuinely "of interest" within the SAME estimation (e.g. the joint $(\lambda,\delta_1,\delta_2)$ grid), grid them jointly as a surface and profile only the true nuisance parameters ($\gamma$)** — not separate coordinate-wise profiled curves for each. Matches Schennach's own Example 1.5 Continued (Fig. S.3, a joint CI for that example's own two structural parameters — no separate nuisance block exists there to fix or profile, so this citation only supports the "grid jointly, don't do coordinate-wise curves" part). **Corrected 2026-09-11 — this does NOT extend to a policy counterfactual's $(\Delta,R)$: checked both papers directly (full-text search) and AK2020's actual code.** Schennach's `ELVIS.pdf`/`ELVIS_supplement.pdf` never use the word "counterfactual" (zero hits); her 2022 JEL survey has exactly one throwaway motivating mention, no methodology. AK2020's own counterfactual (`Appendix_F/F_main_shell.jl`) does the OPPOSITE of "profile the nuisance parameters" — `theta0=0.975` (their structural/support parameter, the direct analog of our $\lambda$) is a hardcoded constant for the entire exercise, confirmed in their paper text too (footnote 67: "we set... the support of the random discount factor to..."); only `gamma` (their Lagrange multiplier, full vector) is ever passed to `bboptimize`/`NLopt.optimize`. The previous version of this bullet cited AK2020 backwards. See the Counterfactual section's own "$\theta$ must be fixed, not reprofiled" tollgate below for what actually governs the $(\Delta,R)$ grid.
- Confidence sets: Theorem F.1's conservative $\chi^2$ critical value is the current default (no subsampling); Chernozhukov-Hong-Tamer (2007) subsampling is the upgrade path if a tighter region is ever wanted.
- More moments strengthen an identified set (Schennach: "adding more moment conditions leads to substantial reductions in the uncertainty... requires no extra analytical work") at the cost of solve time, not validity — don't preemptively cut moments to save compute.
- An **"anchor" licenses a cross-moment regardless of the other factor's heterogeneity**: any quantity forced to exact zero mean by its own defining moment (e.g. $\psi$, $\varepsilon_c$, $\omega-\mu_{\omega,j}$) can be multiplied by *any* other quantity, at any power, and the product's expectation is still validly zero — even if that other quantity has substantial cross-group heterogeneity of its own (verified via $E[\text{anchor}\cdot(X-\mu_{X,j(i)})]=E_j[\mathrm{Cov}(\text{anchor},X\mid j)]$, since the anchor's own construction kills the between-group contamination term). This is the rule for deciding whether a candidate moment needs per-industry demeaning of its non-anchor factor, or can stay pooled.
- A quantity known to be tautological given the model's own optimality conditions (e.g. $\psi\cdot e$ — $e$ is literally the evasion FOC's solution given $\psi,\omega$) is not a valid independence moment; only quantities that are genuinely separate primitives given the conditioning variables (e.g. $M$, from the *materials* FOC, vs. $e$/$\psi$ from the *evasion* FOC) license one.
- A moment nonlinear enough in $e$ to blow up near $e$'s FOC ceiling (e.g. $h'(e)=\partial h/\partial\lambda$) needs a bounding transform before entering the moment vector — otherwise a single extreme-$e$ firm can dominate the CUE weighting matrix's eigenvalues and silently collapse the effective moment count. Any function of $e$ alone stays validly independent of $\varepsilon$ under the same argument that licensed the untransformed version (independence never depended on the specific functional form) — but not every bounded transform is equally usable: one that decays *exponentially* in the raw quantity (e.g. $\exp(h')$, or $\tanh$) can numerically saturate to the same machine-precision constant for most of the sample, silently discarding information rather than just controlling magnitude. A transform that decays only *polynomially* (e.g. $h'/(1-h')$, currently used for this exact row) stays distinguishable across a much wider range.

- Corner firms ($\tau_P=0$, ~10% of unincorp firm-periods, concentrated in genuinely VAT-exempt goods like raw foodstuffs, not misreported zeros): included (`corner_mode="include_zero"`, `"drop"` retired) — $M$ fixed at $M^*$, psi-carrying moment rows set to exactly 0 (uninformative, not "satisfied at 0"), only $\varepsilon$/$\omega$-only rows computed. - A relative-evasion floor $\eta\in[0,1)$ ($M\ge\eta M^*$) is jointly optimized alongside $\delta_0,\delta_1,\delta_2$, as a sampler support restriction (not a gamma-weighted moment) — guards against the CUE objective's eigenvalue-truncated weighting failing to penalize $M\to0$. Estimates come back at $\hat\eta\approx0$ so far — the data doesn't want this floor to bind, but it's a structural guarantee rather than an implicit hope.

**$h'$ score-moment numerical fix, done in two rounds, both 2026-09-07.** The $h'$-transform score moment for $\lambda$ (moment set A row, and B) computes $h'=\partial h/\partial\lambda$ from a **floored** denominator (`H_DENOM_FLOOR=1e-6`, shared between `h_of_e` and `h_prime_of_e` in `Code/Rcpp/1200-stage2-elvis-common.h` — both must agree, or `h_prime` reports a nonzero slope where the floor has made `h` locally flat) — raw, unfloored $h'$ diverges near the FOC ceiling (the sampler's own support puts positive density arbitrarily close to it, `u01~Uniform[0,1)` never reaching the edge exactly) and was found to silently collapse the CUE objective's effective moment count via eigenvalue truncation. **Round 1's transform, $\exp(h')$, is superseded — do not use it.** It bounds to $(0,1]$ but decays *exponentially* in $|h'|$, which underflows to exactly `0.0` for roughly 73-75% of firms in this data (median $e\approx841\Rightarrow h'\approx-1683$, deep in `exp`'s underflow zone) — silently uninformative for most of the sample, not a numerical nicety. **Current transform: `h_prime_bounded(e,\lambda) = h'/(1-h')`** — still bounded ($(-1,0]$), still licensed by the same "any function of $e$ alone stays valid" argument, but decays only *polynomially*, so firms stay numerically distinguishable across the full range of $e$ (checked directly: $e=59{,}171$ and $e=236{,}714$ give $-0.999992$ vs. $-0.999998$ — genuinely distinct in double precision, unlike either $\exp$ or $\tanh$, which both saturate to a shared machine-precision constant well before $|h'|$ reaches values this data routinely produces). z-score normalization was also prototyped (in the R warm-start, cheaper to iterate) and rejected — it introduced a rough, sample-mean/sd-dependent objective that made even that simpler search fail outright for one instrument. Live in the shared header, used by moment sets A and B. See `Research-log/log.md`'s "$h'$ numerical fix" and "standalone grid estimator" entries for the full diagnosis.

**Two-tax-rate model ($\tau_S\ne\tau_P$).** The evasion FOC only ever contains $\tau_P$ (purchases-side rate), never $\tau_S$ (sales-side) — `sales_tax_rate_purchases` is the theoretically correct $h(e)$ input, already wired into the stage-2 driver. $\partial e^*/\partial\tau_P>0$ (higher $\tau_P$ increases evasion — confirmed via closed form and the implicit function theorem; this sign **cannot** be checked by regressing $\mathcal V_{it}$ on $\tau_{P,it}$, since both are built from the same $M^*_{it}$ in opposite positions, a mechanical negative link regardless of the true economics — resolve this only via the 1983 fiscal-reform diff-in-diff or the structural estimation itself, not more bivariate regressions). **Nominal log-share as the FOC-side observable.** $\varepsilon_{it}(M_{it})=\ln\beta-\ln(\rho_tM_{it}/P_tY_{it})$ only ever uses the *ratio* of nominal materials expenditure to nominal sales — no price index or deflation needed for the evasion-FOC side of the model at all. This asymmetry is documented, not modeled — no separate deflation-error term is added; it's a known, out-of-scope limitation on the production/AR(1) side only.

**Identification of $\lambda$:** from the response of evasion to $\tau_P\rho_t$ across sector–time cells (within a cell, $\tau_P\rho_t$ is absorbed into $f_\psi$'s/the moments' location). **Location normalization:** $\operatorname{median}(\psi)=0$ (or $E[\psi]=0$); $\delta_0$ absorbs the level (no corner mass to anchor it).

**Why $\lambda$'s identification is structurally weaker than $(\delta_1,\delta_2)$'s — worked out theoretically 2026-09-11/12, explains the whole session's $\lambda$-identification difficulties as one fact, not several.** $\psi=h(e)-\delta_0+\delta_1\omega-\delta_2\omega^2$ is *linear* in $(\delta_0,\delta_1,\delta_2)$ for any fixed $\lambda$ (since $\lambda$ only enters through $h(e)=\ln(\tau_P\rho_t)+\ln(1-2\lambda e)$, not through the $\theta_{\text{smooth}}$ coefficients). Moment set A's four $\psi$-only rows ($\psi$, $\psi\omega$, $\psi\omega^2$, $\psi\ln M$) are therefore four *linear* equations in the three unknowns $(\delta_0,\delta_1,\delta_2)$, for whatever $\lambda$ is plugged in:
$$E[h(e;\lambda)]=\delta_0-\delta_1E[\omega]+\delta_2E[\omega^2],\quad E[h(e;\lambda)\omega]=\delta_0E[\omega]-\delta_1E[\omega^2]+\delta_2E[\omega^3],\ \ldots$$
**Take the first three: a 3×3 linear system, exactly solvable for $(\delta_0,\delta_1,\delta_2)$ given ANY $\lambda$** (generic nonsingularity of $\omega$'s moment matrix is the only condition) — these three moments have *zero* power to discriminate between candidate $\lambda$'s, not merely weak power, since they're just-identified linear algebra with $\lambda$ sitting outside as a free input every time. The 4th moment ($\psi\ln M$) is the only overidentifying restriction within this block, and its power is capped by how much $\ln M$ varies independently of $\{1,\omega,\omega^2\}$ — likely limited, since $\omega$ is itself constructed from $\ln M$ via the production-function/AR(1) machinery. **All of $\lambda$'s real identifying power has to come from the one place $\lambda$ enters *nonlinearly*: $\ln(1-2\lambda e)$'s curvature, exploited by the $h'_{\text{bounded}}(e,\lambda)\cdot\varepsilon$ score moment** — and that moment is only informative for firms near the FOC ceiling (a thin tail of aggressive evaders), a structurally much weaker signal than the whole-sample linear system pinning down $(\delta_1,\delta_2)$ (hence $\hat\omega^*=\delta_1/2\delta_2$, which is why it lands in the same place across nearly every grid this project has run, while $\lambda$ keeps showing up as a ridge/ trade-off direction). This is *why* adding the $h'_{\text{bounded}}\cdot\varepsilon$ score moment in the first place (2026-09-07 fix) was necessary and helped — it was the only source of genuine nonlinear leverage on $\lambda$ at all. **Natural next step, not yet implemented: add a second, higher-order score-type moment (e.g. $E[\varepsilon^2\cdot h'_{\text{bounded}}]$ or another nonlinear-in-$e$ functional) to give $\lambda$ a second, independent nonlinear anchor** rather than relying on the single existing score row alone.

## Counterfactual

Optimal sales-tax rate: revenue rises mechanically with the rate but higher rates raise the evasion incentive (Laffer trade-off). Also framed as "the tax rate giving median evasion = 0."

**Each $(\Delta,R)$ grid cell requires estimating (profiling) the nuisance parameters and $\gamma$ jointly — this is NOT a forward simulation off a fixed $\hat\theta$.** Same nested-optimization structure as the $(\lambda,\delta_1,\delta_2)$ grid: at every cell, $\lambda,\delta_0,\delta_1,\delta_2,\sigma^2_\psi,\eta$, per-industry nuisance, and $\gamma$ all get (re-)estimated via NLopt, not plugged in from a single upstream fit.

**Per-firm type must NOT be "recovered and held fixed" from a post-hoc tilted MCMC draw either** (Nail Kashaev correction, 2026-09-04, full citation trail in `Research-log/log.md`): Schennach 2022 JEL p.1250 warns explicitly against interpreting the entropy-maximizing/tilted distribution as the actual distribution of the unobservable — an earlier draft of this section did exactly that, "recover the implied type $(\omega,\psi)$, hold it fixed." Corrected: the counterfactual objects below are deterministic functions of the *same* latent $M_i$ the main ELVIS system already carries, never a separately "recovered" per-firm point; any population quantity built from them ($E[R]$, the Laffer curve) comes from the auxiliary-parameter joint-moment device in the Inference plan below, never from averaging over draws from a $(\hat\theta,\hat\gamma)$-tilted chain after the fact.

Closed form under linear $q$, a deterministic function of $M_i$ evaluated at candidate $\tilde\tau_P$:
$$e'(\tilde\tau_P;M_i)=\max\!\left(0,\ \frac{\tilde\tau_P\rho_t-C(\omega(M_i),\psi(M_i))}{2\lambda\tilde\tau_P\rho_t}\right),\qquad C(\omega,\psi)\equiv\textbf{e}^{\delta_0-\delta_1\omega+\delta_2\omega^2+\psi}$$
**The $\max(0,\cdot)$ clip is required, not optional** — some interior (currently-evading) firms will have their true optimum become a corner solution ($e^*=0$) as $\Delta$ moves the benefit shifter, and this genuinely will bind for some firms, not just the institutionally-corner ($\tau_P=0$) ones already handled specially in estimation. The revenue formula below is already well-defined and continuous there with no extra special-casing needed: at $e=0$ the evasion term simply vanishes ($R_i(\Delta)=t1_i-\tilde\tau_{P,i}M_i$) — unlike the *estimation*-side moments, where $e=0$ makes the FOC-derived $\psi$ meaningless (not just zero, forced to 0 by convention), revenue itself is a real, always-defined outcome at any $e\ge0$, corner or interior.

**Structural property, settled 2026-09-10 (tollgate — still owed a slide in `600-opt-tax.qmd`): under linear $q$ and linear $\kappa$, the new evasion is an EXACT linear function of the firm's own baseline evasion, with a firm-invariant intercept.** Expanding the closed form: $e'(\Delta)=\frac{1}{1+\Delta}\,e_i+\frac{\Delta}{2\lambda(1+\Delta)}$ — slope $1/(1+\Delta)$ on the firm's *own* $e_i$, plus an intercept that is identical for every firm (no $\tau_P,\rho_t,\omega,\psi$ dependence survives — they cancel exactly). This is not a derivation artifact; it follows mechanically from $\kappa$ being *linear* in $e$, so its marginal cost $\partial\kappa/\partial e$ is a constant (the firm's type $C(\omega,\psi)$, not a function of $e$ itself) — the FOC is therefore linear in $e$, and shifting the benefit side by $\Delta$ shifts every firm's optimum by the *same absolute amount*, regardless of size or current evasion (a cost function with increasing marginal cost, e.g. quadratic in $e$, would instead dampen the response for firms already evading heavily — not this specification). Because $1/\lambda$ is large (per the $\eta$-removal finding above), that uniform intercept dominates almost immediately, which is the exact mechanism behind the revenue collapse documented below. **Scope caveat, load-bearing for how far $\Delta$ can be pushed:** $e_i$ (hence $C(\omega_i,\psi_i)$) is the firm's *baseline* type, held fixed in this comparative-statics exercise — the counterfactual asks "how does this firm, with its own unchanged productivity and cost draw, respond to a different tax rate," not "what would the whole economy's equilibrium look like," and doesn't speak to any margin (output, materials sourcing, exit) that would let $(\omega,\psi)$ themselves respond to a large enough tax change. That's an argument for keeping $\Delta$ small in any exercise taken seriously, not a caveat to relax later.

**Headline finding, settled 2026-09-10 (pure forward-simulation, best no-$\eta$ operating point, lag_m, $\lambda=3.501\times10^{-7}$ — tollgate, owed the same slide as the paragraph above): diminishing marginal returns to a purchases-tax cut identify an economically motivated stopping point, distinct from the mathematical corner.** Define $MR(\Delta)\equiv[R^{\text{real}}(\Delta-0.01)-R^{\text{real}}(\Delta)]/0.01$ — the extra mean real revenue per firm-period bought by the next 0.01 of cut, evaluated on `1275`/`1276`/`1277`'s fine ($\Delta$-step-0.01, then 0.05 beyond $-0.5$) forward-simulated grid. Two clean regimes, no optimization involved (deterministic $e_i$-by-$e_i$ resolve at the fixed operating point):
- **Near $\Delta=0$, $MR$ is large and falling fast** as firms progressively "unclip" from $e'=0$ and resume evading (each firm's own clip threshold is $\Delta=-2\lambda e_i$, tiny given $\lambda\sim3.5\times10^{-7}$): $MR=1359$ at $\Delta=-0.01$, $866$ at $\Delta=-0.03$, $750$ at $\Delta=-0.10$.
- **By $\Delta\approx-0.20$, $MR$ has flattened to a constant $\approx743$** and stays there (measured flat at $-0.21,-0.25,-0.40$, and even out to the $-0.90\to-0.95$ leg at step 0.05) — once virtually every firm has hit its own $e'=0$ clip, the only remaining effect of a further cut is mechanical ($MR\to-\text{mean}(\tau_P M)$, no behavioral/evasion channel left to exploit). Revenue itself is exactly linear in $\Delta$ (constant first differences) from $\Delta\approx-0.20$ out to $-0.95$; only the region between $-0.20$ and the revenue/$\Delta$ zero-crossing at $\Delta\approx0.0325$ (interpolated on `1275`/`1276`, real terms) is curved.

**Three-zone breakdown of the full simulated curve, tollgate, settled 2026-09-10 (checked directly against `1275`'s first differences, real terms):**
1. **Zone 1, $\Delta\in[-0.95,-0.20]$ — linear, flat MR.** First difference constant at $-7.4326$ per 0.01 step out to $-0.30$, drifting by under 0.1% by $-0.20$ (still $-7.4372$) — the flat-$MR\approx743$ region above.
2. **Zone 2, $\Delta\in[-0.20,0.0325]$ — the ONLY genuinely nonlinear zone, and the one that matters economically.** In properly-scaled marginal-revenue terms ($MR(\Delta)\equiv[R(\Delta-0.01)-R(\Delta)]/0.01$, same definition as the headline finding above), $MR$ accelerates smoothly from $\approx745$ at $\Delta=-0.19$ up to $1{,}359$ at $-0.01$ and $5{,}970$ right at $\Delta=0$, then a second, sharper kink sits exactly at $\Delta=0$: revenue keeps falling fast for $\Delta>0$ too, before leveling off (still collapsing) out to the $0.0325$ crossing. **Fair-comparison caveat, corrected 2026-09-10 (user caught this — see below): don't compare $5{,}970$ to a "per-unit-$\Delta$" rate computed from a $\Delta>0$ step**, since the $\Delta>0$ side's slope only holds locally over a fraction of a percentage point (unlike zone 1's, which holds over 0.75 of $\Delta$) — rescaling a small step there to "per unit $\Delta$" manufactures an inflated, not-really-comparable number. This is where the diminishing-returns headline argument and the identified $\Delta\approx-0.20/-0.25$ optimum both live, and the only zone worth zooming a presentation plot into.

**Mechanism behind the sharp kink exactly at $\Delta=0$, settled 2026-09-10: a real structural pile-up, not a bug.** Every firm has its own unclipping threshold $\Delta=-2\lambda e_i$ (always $\le0$), and because $\lambda$ is tiny, this threshold sits extremely close to zero for most firms — the **median** firm ($e_i\approx841$) has threshold $\Delta\approx-0.00059$, essentially at $\Delta=0$ itself. The intercept term's own slope at $\Delta=0$ is $1/(2\lambda)\approx1{,}428{,}163$ — huge, and shared by every unclipped firm regardless of its own $e_i$. So as $\Delta$ rises from $-0.01$ toward $0$, a large mass of firms (everyone with small-to-median $e_i$) cross their own threshold and "switch on" this enormous per-firm slope almost simultaneously in a very narrow band — not a mathematical discontinuity (each firm's $e'(\Delta)$ is individually smooth), but the *population-average* $MR$ shoots up nearly vertically because so many firms transition at once. Confirmed against the finer 0.001-step onset grid (`1276`): $MR$ is already $\approx53{,}165$ at $\Delta=0.001$ and stays there through $0.02$ — the jump genuinely happens in an infinitesimally narrow window at $\Delta=0$, not gradually over the visible grid. **Once $\Delta>0$, every firm is unclipped** (thresholds are always $\le0$), so this is a qualitatively different regime from $\Delta<0$ (where most firms are still clipped) — the two sides answer different policy questions ("cut further" vs. "reverse part of an increase") and should be shown as separately-scaled panels, not one continuum, to avoid the misleading impression of a single smooth curve or of mixed-up signs.

**Revenue formula (two-tax-rate model — $\tau_S$ enters and must be netted against $\tau_P$, even though only $\tau_P$ is shocked):** VAT revenue is sales-side tax collected minus the purchases-side credit claimed; only the credit side is exposed to evasion:
$$R_i(\Delta;M_i)=\underbrace{t1_{it}}_{\tau_{S,it}\cdot P_tY_{it},\ \text{fixed}}-\underbrace{\tilde\tau_{P,it}\Big[M_i+\big(1-q(\tilde e_i(\Delta))\big)\tilde e_i(\Delta)\Big]}_{\text{shocked by }\Delta},\qquad \tilde\tau_{P,it}\equiv(1+\Delta)\tau_{P,it}$$
$t1$ is the raw "sales tax paid on sales" column, used directly since $\tau_S$ never changes in this exercise. Corner firms ($\tau_P=0$): $R_i=t1_{it}-\tilde\tau_{P,it}M^*_{it}$, no detection term (nothing to catch). K/L don't enter (VAT-exempt). Units are nominal COP throughout — never substitute the real, log-scale `m`/`y` columns. Deflate once at aggregation ($R_i^{\text{real}}=R_i/P_t$, `p_gdp_new`), not mid-formula, since the panel spans a decade of inflation.

**Inference plan (AK2020-style test inversion, §5.5 and their Appendix F code, `Lit-Papers/ReplicationAK2020/Appendix_F/`):** grid $(\Delta,R)$ jointly — $R$ as its own ELVIS auxiliary parameter with its own moment $E[R_i(\Delta;M_i)-R]=0$ — and at each cell, profile out everything else ($\lambda,\delta_0,\delta_1,\delta_2,\sigma^2_\psi,\eta$, per-industry nuisance, $\gamma$). Confidence set: $\{(\Delta,R):TS(\Delta,R)<\chi^2_{d_g,0.95}\}$ where $TS=2n\hat L_n$ per the sign convention above, computed as the literal min/max of *tested* grid points passing the test (matches AK2020's `F_figure1.R`). A genuine interval-valued Laffer-curve conclusion (rather than a point $\tau^*$) is an acceptable, arguably more honest deliverable — set-identification is not a fallback to avoid.

**Economic mechanism behind the cut/increase asymmetry, settled 2026-09-13: it's linearity of $q$ and $\kappa$ *combined with* $\lambda$ being small — not linearity alone.** Rewriting the counterfactual evasion closed form as $e'(\Delta)=\big[e_i+\Delta/(2\lambda)\big]/(1+\Delta)$ makes the mechanism a horse race between the firm's own baseline $e_i$ and the common intercept $\Delta/(2\lambda)$. The intercept dominates only because $\hat\lambda=5.427\times10^{-7}$ is roughly an order of magnitude below the threshold ($\lambda\sim\Delta/(2e_i)$) where the two terms would be comparable — a *quantitative* fact about this data's estimated enforcement intensity, not a generic property of linear specifications. Taking $E[\cdot]$ over $e_i$: $E[e'(\Delta)]=E[e_i]/(1+\Delta)+\Delta/(2\lambda(1+\Delta))$ — the intercept term is literally the *average* extra fictitious-materials claim (hence extra credit paid out) added on top of each firm's own scaled baseline, which is the direct mechanism behind the revenue collapse ("refund compounding"). On the cut side, the same intercept (now negative) is what a heterogeneous, individually-floored ($e\ge0$) population staggers into a gradual response — small-$e_i$ firms hit zero first, large-$e_i$ firms need a bigger cut — while on the increase side nothing analogous constrains the upside, so the shift looks synchronized across firms instead of staggered.

**Alternative detection-probability curvatures were worked through analytically (not yet estimated) to check whether the "uniform jump" survives them.** Both directions have a real economic story — convex $q$ ("red flags compound": small overreporting is invisible, but claims growing implausible relative to firm scale get caught increasingly fast — a smooth version of a sales-based reporting cap) and concave $q$ ("audit capacity saturates": any anomaly gets some scrutiny immediately, but catching a firm already evading heavily isn't much easier at the margin). The mere right-skew of the *observed* evasion distribution does not cleanly distinguish between them — that skew is confounded with the (separately, already right-skewed-generating) distribution of firm type $C(\omega,\psi)$, so matching it isn't a valid identification argument on its own. Solved anyway, for both: convex $q=(\lambda e)^k$ gives $e=\frac1\lambda\big([1-C/(\tau\rho_t)]/(1+k)\big)^{1/k}$ (nests the linear closed form exactly at $k=1$); concave $q=1-e^{-\lambda e}$ gives $e=[1-W_0(K\exp(1))]/\lambda$ where $K=C/(\tau\rho_t)$ and $W_0$ is the principal branch of the Lambert $W$ function (also consistent with the documented ceiling $\lambda e<1$, since $W_0(\exp(1))=1$ exactly at the boundary). **Both break the uniform-constant-shift result** — in both cases the mapping from the benefit shifter to $e$ is a genuinely nonlinear function of the firm's own type $C$ (hence of $e_i$), so $\Delta$ and firm heterogeneity are no longer additively separable the way they are under linear $q$+linear $\kappa$. This confirms the uniform-jump property is a **knife-edge feature of that one exact specification**, not a robust prediction — any curvature in either $q$ or $\kappa$, in either direction, restores heterogeneous, dampened responses and would smooth the increase-side kink.

**Planned robustness check, not yet run: cap realized revenue at $R_i\ge0$ (a firm can evade down to paying nothing, but the government never issues a net cash refund purely from suspicious over-claimed credits).** Proven algebraically (not just assumed) that this is *exactly* equivalent to capping the firm's own $e'$ at the level where $R_i=0$ — not an approximation — because $R_i(e')$ is strictly monotonically decreasing in $e'$ over the model's own existing domain ($e'<1/(2\lambda)$, i.e. uncaught evasion $e'-\lambda e'^2$ is strictly increasing there). **"No free lunch" — what the variance reduction actually costs, since it isn't a classical bias-variance trade ($E[g^{\text{adj}}]=E[g^{\text{raw}}]$ exactly, by construction).** Not "the residual is thin" — that's inherent to what a control variate does, not a cost (the thin residual *is* the signal). The real, identified costs: (1) **a measured, non-hypothetical spillover**: forcing $\gamma$ to reconcile a tight $R$-target inflates `eps*e`'s own SE $5.4\times$ at the tested point — reducing one moment's noise reshapes the whole fitted system via the joint entropic tilt, not a local operation; (2) **the deepest one — higher power cuts both ways**: a test sharp enough to detect a true $\Delta$-effect is also sharp enough to reject on any other small thing wrong elsewhere in the model that the blunter raw-$R$ test never noticed, and this can't currently be distinguished from a genuine rejection.

**The exogeneity/$\Delta$-invariance concern (whether $\hat\beta$ is a data-mined number) is now substantially addressed, 2026-09-18: an independent, non-data-fitted check confirms $\hat\beta\approx1$ isn't an artifact.** Built `row9_mode=theory`: the same control-variate operation but with the coefficient fixed at exactly 1 (from the revenue identity $R_i=t1_i-\tau_P[M_i+(1-q(e'))e']$, i.e. testing $R_i(\Delta)-t1_i$ directly) rather than regression-fitted. Tested at the same 9 $\Delta$'s as CV-$R$'s own headline table ($-8,-5,-4,-3,-2,0,+0.5,+1,+2\%$): **every single one gives a clean, well-behaved bracket (no exceptions) whose implied CI nests entirely inside CV-$R$'s own fine-resolved hard-CI**, several upper bounds matching almost exactly (e.g. $0\%$: 1744.6 vs. 1745; $+2\%$: 1064.3 vs. 1065). This is strong evidence $\hat\beta$'s value is close to theoretically correct, not data-mined — a sharper, more direct check than a $\hat\beta\pm2$SE sensitivity refit would have been. Contrast with the already-implemented Loss moment (which changes *what's measured*, introducing zero-inflation): the theory-coefficient version changes only *which fixed coefficient* nets out $t1_i$, stays as smooth/non-degenerate as $R_i$ itself, and shows none of Loss's three failure modes (empty region, vacuous region, erratic region). Full numbers: `Research-log/log.md`.

## Working conventions
- **Two presentation framings for prior empirical exercises — don't mix them:**
  - *Thesis / job-talk (first-time audience):* frame each of the three exercises (deconvolution, policy change, size) as motivating evidence for a structural feature: "reduced form showed X → the model's Y rationalizes X."
  - *Supervisor meetings (they already tracked the progress as it happened):* the question for them is validation, not motivation — does the prior result **hold or break** now that the structural model exists? They also can't take more than one topic per meeting: **one exercise per meeting**, and never bundle the `600-opt-tax.qmd` estimation/counterfactual pipeline with any of the three legacy audits, or bundle multiple legacy audits together.

- **New content that doesn't replace anything writes in live** (not commented out) once agreed in chat. Commenting-out is reserved specifically for the case where a draft would otherwise **erase or replace** existing slides: **always confirm before erasing anything** — the user may instead want the superseded text kept, commented out, alongside the new version rather than deleted.

- **Slide audience**: default target is supervisors first, then reused in JMP job-talk prep — write for first-year econ PhD students / non-specialist professors, not for the user. Build intuition before formalism. When refining slide content mid-conversation, present the refined version as if explaining to that audience — don't frame it as correcting what the user got wrong during the refining stage.
